It was a message rolled out gradually throughout the social networks: Bryan Johnson, the celebrity
multimillionaire best known for injecting himself with his son’s plasma so as to slow the aging
process, posted on all his accounts this week that he’d “been thinking things over and wonder if
i’ve taken this whole longevity thing too far.” He didn’t elaborate further, and my own
attempt to interview Johnson over this epiphany fell through. So the public was left only with the
implication that the man whose entire claim to fame revolves around his quest for immortality was
perhaps having some regrets, inspiring a flood of bemused comments and aggregation posts.

This standout declaration was just one entry in a public diary demonstrating how Johnson, once a
firm adherent to his supposedly life-prolonging and surprisingly popular “Blueprint protocol,”
was now taking things in even more unorthodox directions. In recent days, Johnson has shared that he
ate meat for the first time in six years, stowed his girlfriend’s menstrual blood in a deep
freezer, started developing a “clone” of himself in a Petri dish (which is not possible), and
began collecting his honest-to-goodness tears in order to study “eye longevity.”
