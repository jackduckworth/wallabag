

====================================================================================================

The 8 Greatest Sci-Fi Book Trilogies of All Time, Ranked

====================================================================================================

Sci-fi has always been about thinking on a grand scale. By the genre's very nature, sometimes an
author's ideas can't be contained comfortably in one book. As a result, science fiction has produced
more than a few phenomenal trilogies, from the multi-century sweep of _Foundation_ to the hard
sci-fi suspense of _The Three-Body Problem_.

THE BOOK SERIES BELOW ARE ALL CREATIVE AND AMBITIOUS, whether rooted in political intrigue,
cyberpunk dystopia, action-packed space opera, or philosophical allegory. They should contain
something for every kind of sci-fi fan, and the fact that they come in threes should be a bonus for
anyone who loves both the genre and literature as a whole.

8 MARS TRILOGY (1992–1999)

 [The cover of the novel Red Mars]Image via Random House
"To be whole is to be part; true voyage is return." Beginning with _Red Mars_ before continuing
through _Green Mars_ and _Blue Mars_, KIM STANLEY ROBINSON's _Mars Trilogy_ is perhaps the finest
exploration of humanity's colonization of another planet. The series follows the First Hundred
settlers who establish the first permanent colony on Mars, charting the centuries-long process of
transforming the Red Planet into a world capable of supporting human life. Along the way, these
pioneers must grapple with political conflict and competing visions of civilization's future.

A BIG PART OF WHAT MAKES THESE BOOKS WORK IS THE DEDICATION TO REALISM, or at least plausibility.
Robinson devotes enormous attention to the practical realities of terraforming without sacrificing
compelling human drama. Everything is deeply researched, and the canyons, volcanoes, and plains of
the Red Planet are described in immersive detail. Finally, the major characters are fittingly
three-dimensional and psychologically complex.

7 IMPERIAL RADCH TRILOGY (2013–2015)

 [ancillary justice book cover]Image via Hachette Book Group
"I am not a person who matters." With _Ancillary Justice_, _Ancillary Sword_, and _Ancillary Mercy_,
ANN LECKIE reinvented the modern space opera. The trilogy centers on Breq, the lone surviving
fragment of a vast AI hive mind that once controlled a warship serving the expansionist Radch
Empire. Stripped of nearly all her former power, Breq embarks upon a quest for vengeance, in the
process becoming entangled in a civil war that threatens the empire itself.

Breq is an intriguing protagonist, to say the least. Once existing simultaneously across thousands
of bodies, she experiences individuality in profoundly unfamiliar ways, and Leckie uses her as a
vehicle to COMMENT ON IDENTITY AND PERSONHOOD. The antagonist is likewise a multi-body being that
adds further layers to the story, both literally and metaphorically. Refreshingly, THE STORY IS
CHARACTER-DRIVEN AND FOCUSES ON PERSONAL stakes rather than the typical "blow up the galaxy" space
opera tropes.

6 RED RISING TRILOGY (2014–2016)

 [Cover of 'Red Rising' by Pierce Brown]Cover of 'Red Rising' by Pierce BrownImage via Del Rey Books
"I live for the dream that my children will be born free." The original _Red Rising_ trilogy is the
chronicle of a revolution. We see everything through the eyes of Darrow, a laborer born into the
oppressed Red caste beneath the surface of Mars. After suffering a devastating personal tragedy,
Darrow infiltrates the ruling Gold elite in order to dismantle the brutal color-based hierarchy that
dominates the Solar System from within.

Although the beginning borrows familiar elements from dystopian YA fiction, the trilogy rapidly
evolves into AN EXPANSIVE, MORALLY COMPLICATED MILITARY SPACE OPERA. The plot is loaded with tragic
twists and shifting allegiances, while the combat scenes are all punchy, visceral, kinetic, blending
ancient Roman tactics with high-tech weaponry, like pulse shields, razor swords, and armored
grav-boots. Author PIERCE BROWN writes all this with a relentless momentum that keeps the pages
turning.

5 BAS-LAG NOVELS (2000–2004)

 [Perdido Street Station Book cover]Image via Macmillan
"The city was like a vast engine dreaming." Few sci-fi trilogies are quite as bizarre or imaginative
as the Bas-Lag books. Across _Perdido Street Station_, _The Scar_, and _Iron Council_, writer CHINA
MIÉVILLE creates a setting that gleefully ignores conventional genre boundaries. SCI-FI MINGLES
WITH FANTASY, HORROR, STEAMPUNK, WEIRD FICTION, AND POLITICAL ALLEGORY. Miéville populates this
world with an astonishing assortment of creations, including golems, demons, monstrous moths, winged
people known as Garuda, insect-headed ones known as Khepri, and entire pirate cities that drift
across the ocean.

The trilogy's supernatural elements are equally unconventional. A type of spellcasting known as
thaumaturgy functions almost like an alternate form of science, while the enigmatic magical force
called the Torque can warp living bodies and even distort time itself. Stranger still is the Weaver,
a godlike multidimensional being resembling an enormous spider. IT'S ALL RATHER PSYCHEDELIC AND
OFFBEAT but incredibly worthwhile.

4 REMEMBRANCE OF EARTH'S PAST (2006–2010)

 [The book cover for 'The Three-Body Problem' by Liu Cixin.]The book cover for 'The Three-Body
Problem' by Liu Cixin.Image via Tor Books
"The universe is a dark forest." Author LIU CIXIN fired a shot across the hard sci-fi bow with _The
Three-Body Problem_, then built on that foundation masterfully with follow-ups _The Dark Forest_ and
_Death's End_. What starts with humanity's first contact with an alien civilization gradually
evolves into a breathtaking exploration of astrophysics, game theory, cosmic sociology, and the
ultimate fate of intelligent life across billions of years. Talk about ambitious.

Along the way, THE SERIES HIT US WITH ALL KINDS OF MIND-BENDING IDEAS, like proton-sized
supercomputers, aliens capable of holding entire dimensions, and a three-sun solar system that
periodically inflicts apocalypse upon the aliens' home world. Yet despite all this enormous
conceptual scope, the trilogy remains grounded in believable characters and compelling moral
dilemmas. The TV series, overseen by DAVID BENIOFF and D.B. WEISS, is solid too, and worth checking
out.

3 SPRAWL TRILOGY (1984–1988)

 [The book cover of William Gibson's 'Neuromancer']The book cover of William Gibson's
'Neuromancer'Image via Ace Books
"The sky above the port was the color of television, tuned to a dead channel." Many sci-fi fans may
not know this, but WILLIAM GIBSON's cyberpunk classic _Neuromancer_ is actually the first
installment in a trilogy, being followed by _Count Zero_ and _Mona Lisa Overdrive_. Set in a
near-future world dominated by AI, sprawling megacities, and uber-powerful multinational
corporations, the series follows hackers, mercenaries, artists, and criminals navigating a society
transformed by cyberspace and rapidly advancing technology.

GIBSON'S VISION PROVED ASTONISHINGLY PROPHETIC. Decades before the internet became central to
everyday life, he was already seeing a future with imagined virtual worlds, ubiquitous digital
networks, cybercrime, and the growing influence of powerful tech companies. He also coined a bunch
of jargon, including the term "cyberspace" (which he fittingly described as a "consensual
hallucination"). On the technical side, his DENSE, LYRICAL, FRACTURED PROSE style perfectly captures
the grim, techno-industrial atmosphere of his world.

2 FOUNDATION TRILOGY (1951–1953)

 [The cover of the book Foundation]Image via Gnome Press
"Violence is the last refuge of the incompetent." This genre landmark centers on mathematician Hari
Seldon, whose revolutionary science of psychohistory predicts the inevitable collapse of the
Galactic Empire. Unable to prevent civilization's downfall, Seldon establishes the Foundation at the
edge of the galaxy with the goal of preserving humanity's knowledge and shortening the coming dark
age from thirty thousand years to just one thousand.

With this larger-than-life setup, writer ISAAC ASMIV helped establish many of the conventions of
modern space opera. The most striking feature of _Foundation_ is the sheer scale and scope. Rather
than focusing on a single protagonist, THE TRILOGY UNFOLDS ACROSS CENTURIES, with successive
generations confronting new threats and crises. This narrative sweep was unlike any sci-fi series
that had come before, breaking open EXCITING NEW POSSIBILITIES FOR THE GENRE AND INSPIRING COUNTLESS
FLEDGLING WRITERS.

"Fear is the mind-killer." When it comes to color, creativity, and lasting influence, it's hard to
top FRANK HERBERT's original _Dune_ trilogy, comprising _Dune_, _Dune Messiah_, and _Children of
Dune_. Beginning on the desert planet Arrakis, the books follow Paul Atreides as he rises from
dispossessed heir to messianic emperor, before examining the unforeseen consequences of his absolute
power. IT'S A MASTERCLASS IN INTRICATE WORLDBUILDING AND POLITICAL INTRIGUE.

While the _Dune_ chronicles eventually expanded into six books, the original trilogy forms a
complete, self-contained narrative arc that forever changed sci-fi by SHIFTING THE FOCUS FROM
HIGH-TECH GADGETS TO ECOLOGY, RELIGION, POLITICS, and a deconstruction of the superhero myth.
Herbert does so while still immersing is an an awesome, fascinating world of mind-altering spice,
strange powers, layered cultures, and terrifying sand-worms. Thanks to DENIS VILLENEUVE's movie
adaptations, the series once again looms large in the public consciousness.
