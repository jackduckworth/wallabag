Treasure Island review – this TV stinker does its best to ruin a literary classic

====================================================================================================

A lever groans, a pipe judders and _thunk:_ another unsolicited adaptation of a beloved literary
classic is deposited on our screens. This particular emission is a six-part take on Treasure Island,
Robert Louis Stevenson's timeless tale of swashbuckling adventure, high-seas treachery and thick
eyebrowed youths in tiny britches wrestling with double-edged superpowers. The 19th-century novel
has of course long captured the public imagination, its themes of … hang on. _Superpowers_? Aye,
that there be. An' plenty more bilge besides. For this US-UK co-production be what lubbers refer to
as a "reimagining". Which essentially means much effort has been made to "jazz up" – or, if you
will, "ruin" – the source material.

And so, for better or significantly worse, the Jim Hawkins (Tom Sweet) of this new Treasure Island,
titivated by writer Robert Murphy, has been given a photographic and possibly quasi-mystical memory;
a preternatural talent for recollection that is demonstrated in scenes of much whooshing excitement.
Here we find the youngster scowling at various documents while the soundtrack whispers mysteriously
and the camera zooms slowly in then quickly out of his face, like a bee too embarrassed to ask for
directions to the toilet. Not that any of this makes him interesting, mind.

[embedded content]
Indeed, Jim's main contribution to the first couple of episodes is to stand with his mouth open as
"feisty" mum Bess (Hayley Atwell) snogs pirates, bribes pirates and burns maps belonging to pirates
while bellowing things such as:"I am but a poor widow". Poor Jim Hawkins; one of literature's most
spirited young heroes has been reduced here to a gulping afterthought, his buccaneering twinkle
eclipsed by mummy's smudged eyeliner.

To darkest Devon, then, where a storm brings with it further indignities in the form of the plot.

Innkeeper Bess Hawkins 'as lost her 'usband and 'er aitches in "the siege of Gibraltar" and now must
eke a livin' from 'er dull son's ability to speed-memorise passages chosen at random from the King
James Bible for the entertainment of 'er soused clientele.

But avast, who be the stranger who just staggered in from the sea? And what is the strange an'
hauntin' cacophony that emanates from his beard?Could it be … an _accent _of some sort? It's
certainly possible! "I hem por iiin coin but riiiich in dreeeeeam," gargles Billy Bones (Tomer "The
Boys" Capone) as Bess paws his incomprehensible chest. "An udder quart o' yor Jamaican _rem_ por
favorrrr …?"

From which benighted pocket of the continent could such a tortuous tumult originate? Frangland?
Spitaly? Eeet ez a mysteree!

[A pirate looks inside a treasure cheston a beach]
Enter terrifying "sea bastard" Long John Silver, who has understandably taken exception not just to
Bones' Vowels de Confusión but also to the fact that his former shipmate has nabbed their dead
captain's treasure map. The nerve! Silver wants said map back and is not best pleased to learn that
it now exists solely in Jim's noggin, the latter having committed its squiggles to memory prior to
Bess torching the thing in a fit of widowy pique.

Silver is played by David Oyelowo, who appears to be acting in another adaptation altogether. An
adaptation that doesn't expect him to endure lines such as "we're sailin' to freedom!" while
balancing a CGI parrot on his shoulder. He is mesmerising and his explosive charisma reduces
everyone in the blast zone to a smoking hillock of tricorn. But, alas, not even Oyelowo can lift the
plot, which drags its wooden leg towards the Caribbean and limply waggles its rubber cutlass at the
ensuing guff storm. Further pointless reimaginings include slow-motion spy battles, additional
swirly "magic memory" scenes and family altercations more befitting a teatime soap than a big swanky
thing on Paramount+ ("It's hard doing this all by myself!")

At one point, a grizzled seadog wobbles to his feet and, apropos not much, barks; "This place
stinks!" An observation to which one feels compelled to reply: that it does, matey. That it does.

Treasure Island is on Paramount+ now
