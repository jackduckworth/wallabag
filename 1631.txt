Harry and Meghan Visited King Charles After Four Years. The Real Villain Was All Too Clear.

====================================================================================================

Last week, a grandfather received a visit from his grandchildren who live overseas. Such things
happen all over the world, every single day, with no fanfare whatsoever. If those people are members
of Britain’s royal family, however, the fanfare cannot be dispensed with. We the public must,
apparently, know about the event, pore over its every smallest detail and decide, at the end of it,
who came out looking best. And so it was when Harry and Meghan made their first visit in four years
as a family, their children Archie and Lilibet in tow, to see King Charles.

Such is the life that Harry and Meghan have been trying to avoid since they moved out of the U.K.
and renounced royal duties in 2020. Have they stayed out of the spotlight altogether? Far from it.
If you’ve had the misfortune of watching _With Love, Meghan_, you may have come away with more
knowledge about the potential uses of edible flowers than anybody needs, and a bad taste in your
mouth to boot. It would be difficult to argue that producing endless Netflix documentaries and
sitting for tell-all interviews are the actions of people who want to be left alone.

But I feel conflicted about Harry and Meghan because, on the one hand, yes, their TV shows are not
very good, and they seem personally sort of blah, and I would happily not hear so much about them
and what they’re up to in California. But on the other, they are 100 percent correct in their
view that the British media are a bunch of parasitic monsters when it comes to their coverage of the
royals. Anyone in their right mind would cross an ocean to get away from it. I am, however, adding
my voice to the choir here—and I appreciate the irony, so bear with me—in order to paint a
picture of precisely how doggedly invasive the British press is when it comes to the royal family.

This visit by Harry and Meghan was a private event. It happened at Highgrove House, a sort of royal
family satellite property outside London, and there were no photos released or any details given
about what the family did together or spoke about during the visit. Who knows, and frankly, who
cares.

And yet, even so, the tabloids are, I think, probably budgetarily mandated to churn out analysis of
who “won” this visit. Take this article from the Express, one of the U.K.’s bigger red-top
papers, headlined “Meghan and Harry Just Got Shown Who’s Boss—King Charles Has Played a
Blinder.” Apparently, by not releasing public images of this meeting, the king has bested his son
in a PR battle, and the columnist writes that she wouldn’t be surprised “if they have brought a
sneaky Netflix camera crew to film the children at Diana’s grave.” It ends with this
spit-take-worthy line: “The message from London is quiet but clear, and perfectly managed: You are
always welcome as family, but the days of turning the British monarchy into a media circus are
officially over.” The Daily Mail is running a poll for readers on whether it was “right” for
the king to meet his grandchildren in this manner, and one of its columnists has declared the
Sussexes “agents of chaos” for not confirming to press definitively enough whether the private
family visit was going ahead.

What can be gleaned about the state of the relationship between the British king and his second son
from this visit? Practically nothing new, and yet so heightened is the obsession with the royals
that gleaning must be done. It never ends. Prince William’s son Prince George went to Wimbledon to
watch the tennis this weekend and wore a suit—cue column inches about the fact that some people
thought it was too hot for him to be wearing a suit. Kate’s use of a fan in hot weather at the
tennis match was, according to whatever species of brain worms is de rigueur among royal reporters
at the moment, a dig at Meghan somehow. And, of course, none of the writers of these articles
believe a word of this; they just have to turn out some slop to feed the machine, because people
have been trained since birth in this country to click on information relating to the royal family
like Pavlov’s dog. The media circus will never close its doors so long as selling tickets to see
the performing monkeys is profitable.
