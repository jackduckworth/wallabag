TV tonight: the final series of hit Aussie romcom Colin from Accounts

====================================================================================================

COLIN FROM ACCOUNTS

10PM, BBC TWO

Three years have passed since Gordon's misjudged proposal and the final season of this miraculous
Aussie sitcom gets off to a melancholy start. Gordon and Ashley have unsatisfactory new partners and
the brewery is struggling. Still, at least Colin seems well. A chance encounter brings the pair
together again and the feelings are still strong, but there's baggage to discard first. It's not
without plotting contrivances but, crucially, continues to feel wonderfully messy and real in
emotional terms. The comedy and drama balance immaculately and the chemistry between Patrick
Brammall and Harriet Dyer is as perfect as you'd expect from a real-life couple. Still the sweetest
thing on TV. _Phil Harrison_

9/11: THE SAUDI CONNECTION

8PM, BBC ONE
A quarter of a century after the 2001 attacks on the US, the origin stories of the perpetrators
remain far from settled. This documentary explores the implications of thousands of newly released
documents that, some argue, suggest the involvement of the Saudi Arabian government. The revelations
centre on Omar al-Bayoumi, who has been accused of being a middle man between the Saudis and the
first two hijackers (al-Bayoumi and the Saudi government deny these claims). _PH_

FRED'S ULTIMATE HOTELS

8PM, CHANNEL 4
Fred from the First Dates restaurant, TV's favourite maître d', continues his fancy hotel tour in a
South African safari lodge. But service is never far from his mind, as he offers to help the team
prepare a "bush braai" outdoor barbecue. There's also time for an encounter with an elephant.
_Hollie Richardson_

MY LIFE IS MURDER

8PM, U&ALIBI

[A man tinkers with the back of an old-fashioned looking clock.]
Crushed by a gargoyle thrown from a balcony is one hell of a way to go. Worse still if it happens
during your nightly "ablution stop" in a garden. That's what happens to a toy inventor this week,
and it's up to Alexa to work out who did it: "I hope he managed to zip up first." _HR_

UNIVERSITY CHALLENGE

8.30PM, BBC TWO
Amol Rajan hosts another brain-off between two student teams: Birmingham and an adorably chaotic
quartet from Leicester. Highlights include a spectacular mishearing of a famous novel's title and a
classics question that's much easier than it would have been a few years ago. _Jack Seale_

999: WHAT HAPPENED NEXT

9PM, CHANNEL 4
Another new series of copaganda, this one exploring the aftermath of 999 calls. It begins with a
fascinating case: a train passenger has noticed something deeply troubling on a fellow traveller's
phone and informed the police. The unfolding investigation is bigger than anyone could have
expected, involving a network of child exploitation. _PH_

FILM CHOICE

[Juanita Moore as Annie in Imitation of Life]
IMITATION OF LIFE (DOUGLAS SIRK, 1959), 4.25PM, FILM4
Douglas Sirk's melodrama is remarkable in that it bats around a number of knotty, topical questions
about racism, sexism and class but is also a powerfully intimate drama about the bond between two
mothers and their daughters. Lana Turner headlines as actor Lora, whose independent spirit leads to
theatre fame, despite being a single parent to Sandra Dee's Susie. But it's the other pairing that
provides the main dramatic force: Juanita Moore as Annie, Lora's Black maid, and Susan Kohner as her
unhappy child Sarah Jane, who can, fatefully, pass for white. _Simon Wardell_
