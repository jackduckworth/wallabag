

====================================================================================================

Scotland hold off late Australia rally to secure narrow victory

====================================================================================================

After the pyrotechnics in Paris on Friday and a wondrous Welsh win in Cardiff on Saturday, this was
a much more prosaic affair. Not that the stakes were low: both teams badly needed a positive result,
and Gregor Townsend's side got one by dealing with the quintessentially Scottish conditions better
than their opponents.

Scotland could scarcely afford another patchy performance like last weekend, when they excelled in
the first half against New Zealand before capitulating in the second. They ultimately edged across
the line at a sodden Murrayfield, but might have fallen to a calamitous defeat had Darcy Graham not
foiled a last-ditch Wallabies attack.

"If Darcy hadn't made that tackle it would have been a tricky time," Townsend said of the dramatic
final play. "I'm proud of the players that they helped each other out. We knew Australia were going
to run it from deep, so that's the frustrating thing … But I think we deserved the win given the
effort that had gone in, and given we'd led throughout."

Thus the hosts narrowly won a match that Les Kiss, the Wallabies head coach, described as "turgid".
But the limitations of this Australia side are well known, and the visitors would have hoped for
anything but heavy rain. Australia have lost four in a row and will go to Wales next week even more
desperate for victory. Scotland leapfrog Wales to move fourth in the northern hemisphere table on
points difference.

A knee injury to the captain, Sione Tuipulotu, prompted a late reshuffle: Bristol's Tom Jordan
started at No 12, with the openside flanker Rory Darge taking the armband. Flower of Scotland sung
at Murrayfield always stirs the spirit, and as smoke from pre-match fireworks hung in the air, the
teams set about each other under leaden skies. Australia repeatedly repelled Scotland on their
try-line but became fatally narrow in defence, allowing Ben White to chuck a pass right for Matt
Fagerson to dive over on eight minutes.

Harry Potter's excellent try had the Wallabies back on terms: Carter Gordon sparked an attack down
the visitors' left before Fraser McReight supplied a pass for the former Leicester back to score.
The Australia scrum‑half Ryan Lonergan attempted a drop-goal but pulled it: a scruffy effort that
summed up the overall mood. Graham then touched down on 24 minutes, after a clever pass by Darge,
but Kyle Steyn had put a foot in touch. The second-row Scott Cummings then trudged off with a thigh
injury, Max Williamson replacing him.

When Australia were penalised for not rolling away, Finn Russell kicked for the corner, and the No 8
Jack Dempsey burrowed over. Russell's conversion, making it a seven-point game, felt significant.
But Australia came again. Billy Pollard was shoved over, only for the score to be disallowed for
obstruction by Jeremy Williams after a check by the television match official. Scotland thus nursed
their seven-point lead to the interval.

Pollard's afternoon worsened five minutes after half-time, when an upright tackle and clash of heads
with Blair Kinghorn brought a trip to the sin-bin. It stayed a yellow card on review, thankfully for
the Wallabies hooker, but when he returned the scoreboard stubbornly remained at 12-5.

Australia failed to build attacking continuity, with the centres Joseph‑Aukuso Suaalii and Len
Ikitau virtually anonymous. Townsend's men kept tackling but it wasn't much of a spectacle. Inside
the final 10 minutes, when Scotland worked into the opposition 22, a shot at goal seemed the obvious
option when they won a penalty. Russell kicked it for 15-5.

Game over? This being Scotland, not quite. Max Jorgensen scuttled across the try-line, his score
converted by Ben Donaldson, and suddenly there were three points in it. When Suaalii finally made a
line break, bursting out of the 22 with the clock in the red, Scotland were exposed and fearing the
worst. Jorgensen fed the full-back Tom Wright, but Graham was on hand to bundle him into touch. "I'm
glad there was cover there," Townsend said.

Does Kiss believe he has time to build something for the Rugby World Cup next year? "I'm happy with
that time we've got," he said. "Now we've got two more important Test matches starting with Wales
… I think there's every chance there will be improvement [for the World Cup]. It's in our back
yard and it's down the track. First we've got Wales in their back yard, and they were pretty
impressive yesterday. But yes, I feel we can do something in that time." Wallabies fans will hope
so.
