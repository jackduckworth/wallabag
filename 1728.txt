TV tonight: Jon Hamm's offbeat adult animation lands in the UK

====================================================================================================

GRIMSBURG

10.15PM, SKY ONE
A triple bill of this adult cartoon starring Jon Hamm, which quietly debuted in the UK last week.
Hamm plays police detective Marvin Flute, who has moved back to his home town to reconnect with his
family and continue solving bizarre crimes. This time, there are easy laughs to be had as he finds
himself accused of murdering a judge and tries to clear his own name. _Hollie Richardson_

[Romario Simpson in Granite Harbour.]

GRANITE HARBOUR

8PM, BBC ONE
The Scottish cop drama wraps up its third series with youthful duo DS Bartlett and DC Lindo
determined to untangle the motives behind a teenage girl's murder on a country estate. But with
their prime suspect dead and the special police investigations service scrutinising their conduct,
it won't be easy. _Graeme Virtue_

SURGERY SECRETS OF THE SUPER RICH

8PM, CHANNEL 4
Do we all live inside the "circle of shame" now? In this chillingly dystopian testament to beauty
standards, Hannah is celebrating her engagement with a "bridal package" of treatments for her big
day. These include inserting threads in her cheeks and up her nose for a liquid rhinoplasty. All for
a cool £3k. Congratulations! _Priya Elan_

CORSICA: JEWEL OF THE MEDITERRANEAN

8PM, CHANNEL 5
Joanna Lumley's velvety tones narrate this look at the good life in Corsica, an island off the south
coast of France. We meet a banker turned baker who makes irresistible biscuits, and a university
lecturer who is also a chestnut farmer. Plus, the citadel gets a facelift. _HR_

BERGERAC

9PM, BBC TWO

[Damien Molony in Bergerac.]
Damien Molony is an alluringly unstable and impassioned detective in this smart reboot of the 1980s
Jersey crime drama. This week, Jim Bergerac alarms his colleagues with a manic whiteboard
presentation, fuelled by his obsessive desire to bring down supercilious bigwig Arthur Wakefield
(Philip Glenister). _Jack Seale_

ANN DROID

9.30PM, BBC ONE
An endlessly laugh-out-loud episode of the comedy about a widow and her robot carer. Sue (Sue
Johnston) invites her son and his girlfriend over for wine and crisps, but she's still not convinced
about them: "Not eating prawn cocktail crisps? He's forgotten who he is." Meanwhile, unbearable
frenemy Phyllis gets bad news. _HR_

FILM

THE LAST HOUSE (LOUIS LETERRIER, 2026), NETFLIX
The population has been confined indoors because of a little-understood external threat, meaning
parents must homeschool their children, supplies are rationed and communication with neighbours can
happen only at a distance … sound familiar? The Last House comes on like a Covid lockdown
flashback but it's actually an intriguing sci-fi horror, with a plot reminiscent of 2007 film The
Mist. It benefits greatly from the presence of charming leads Wagner Moura (The Secret Agent) and
Greta Lee (Past Lives), as a couple forced to extremes for their family's survival. _Ellen E Jones_

HOARD (LUNA CARMOON, 2023), 11.05PM, BBC TWO

[Joseph Quinn (left) and Saura Lightfoot-Leon in Hoard.]
If a film-watching experience can be described as "fetid", then Hoard is that – but in a good way.
Writer and director Luna Carmoon was only 26 when she made her debut feature, announcing herself as
a thrillingly distinctive talent in British cinema. Saura Lightfoot Leon stars as Maria, a young,
care-experienced woman whose loving but unstable childhood is revealed in the detritus of her
chaotic present. Hayley Squires (I, Daniel Blake) plays Maria's bin-diving, hoarder mother and
Joseph Quinn (Stranger Things) is her foster brother turned love interest. _EEJ_
