TV tonight: the terrifying and tragic case of a New Year's Day arson attack

====================================================================================================

MURDER CASE: FIRE IN THE SUBURBS

9PM, BBC TWO 
"I awoke and the whole room had exploded," recalls Bex Williams of New Year's Day 2017, when she was
the victim of an arson attack. Bex survived to tell the tale but, tragically, her boyfriend Cameron
Logan died. This visceral two-part documentary tells the story of the aftermath, including Bex's
long recovery and the police investigation which concentrated on a brotherly feud. _Phil Harrison_

THE FUTURE WITH HANNAH FRY

7.30PM, BBC TWO 
What would a pain-free future look like? Against the backdrop of the global opioid crisis comes a
surge of new technology that could reduce or even eliminate it. Fry talks to people living with pain
and the scientists who have the potential to change their lives in a thought-provoking episode.
_Hannah Verdier_

RORY PECK AWARDS

7.55PM, CHANNEL 4 
These awards celebrate freelance film-makers who are bringing important international stories to the
screen. This series of capsule documentaries showcases the 2026 nominees. It begins with Lebanese
journalist Janay Boulos and Syrian director Abd Alkader Habak, who collaborate from thousands of
miles apart. _PH_

CELEBRITY RACE ACROSS THE WORLD

8PM, BBC ONE

[A young woman with blonde hair smiles as she looks through a camera as two men look up in a jungle]
After reaching Cairns, harmonious Andre siblings Junior and Princess are in the lead. They now need
to make their way to Hamilton Island – but despite Australia being their dad Peter's homeland,
they've only ever visited the Gold Coast before. They'd best decide on the route quickly, as Sam and
Kaz are next to depart. _Hollie Richardson_

HUNTING ALICE BELL

9PM, CHANNEL 4 
Things are looking up for the women's support group as this patchy but compelling drama continues.
Their plight is gaining media traction and they've been invited to a conference in Dublin. However,
public exposure, an unwelcome presence in the audience and plenty of cocktails prove a dangerous
mix. Do they really trust each other? _PH_

FIRST DATES

10PM, CHANNEL 4 
Would you be willing to go on a double date with your parent or child? Brian and his son Thomas test
the waters in the restaurant this week. Over on another table, widower Mark is looking for new love
ahead of turning 60 – and they need to double up as a great dance partner. _HR_

FILM CHOICE

GENTLE MONSTER, (MARIE KREUTZER, 2026),NETFLIX

[Léa Seydoux and Catherine Deneuve in Gentle Monster.]
Marie Kreutzer's follow-up to her bravura feminist historical reboot Corsage is a devastating drama
of love, betrayal and family secrets. Léa Seydoux – who never seems to make a bad film – stars
as musician Lucy, based in the countryside outside Munich with her film-maker spouse Philip
(Laurence Rupp) and their young son Johnny (Malo Blanchet). Then one day, police officer Elsa (Jella
Haase) turns up and Philip is arrested for possession of child sexual abuse images. As Lucy's
certainties about her life collapse around her, Elsa has to deal with her own father's dementia and
associated bad behaviour, in a tale that grows more disturbing with each revelation. _Simon Wardell_

When a Witness Recants, (Dawn Porter, 2026), HBO Max 
Dawn Porter's documentary is an enraging tale of a major miscarriage of justice in 1983 Baltimore,
when teenage friends Alfred Chestnut, Andrew Stewart and Ransom Watkins were wrongly convicted of
the fatal shooting of a 14-year-old boy. The prosecution case rested on witnesses who later said
they were coerced by police into changing their statements. We hear from the trio – who were
released and exonerated after 36 years – and one witness who talks for the first time. But there's
a sting in the tale, as the freed men talk heart-rendingly about their lost years and continuing
lack of closure. _SW _
