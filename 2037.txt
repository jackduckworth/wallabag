If Republicans Were Sh*tting The Bed About Talarico Before, That CNN Town Hall Didn’t Help

====================================================================================================

You can tell how well the Talarico campaign thinks last night’s CNN town hall went by how promptly
they added one million clips from it to their Instagram, so everybody could see. Y’all, he’s
good at this.

James Talarico’s opponent Ken Paxton has been famously a giant coward about debating him, so
obviously there was no Ossoff-style beatdown last night.

[Jon Ossoff Backs Up Car, Runs Over Mike Collins Again. And Again. And Again. And Again.]

But that’s not quite Talarico’s style anyway. (Though we still would absolutely enjoy seeing
what Talarico would do in that format. We think he’s more than up to the task.)

Instead, last night, Talarico was with the voters, specifically Republicans and independents who
probably _really really really really really_ don’t want to vote for Ken Paxton, because he’s a
criminal and a scumbag, but they need to find out if Talarico is going to force Jesus to have sex
genders during third period. (The crowd was evenly split between Republicans, Democrats and
independents.)

It was co-hosted by those Jubilee people — you know, every time there's a “One nice person
surrounded by 20 anti-abortion lunatics!” or “One fascist surrounded by 20 normals!” thing?
That’s Jubilee — so hooray, they did something of value for once.

“I am tired of us being divided into teams: Left versus Right, blue versus red, urban versus
rural. Because the truth is we're all on the same team,” he said.

He then asked people to raise their hands if they have an elderly family member who needs care; if
they’ve ever worried about how to pay for things these days; if they know what student loans and
medical debt are like; and so forth.

“So do I,” he kept saying.

“Those big money interests, those social media platforms, those cable news networks, they want to
divide this team. Imagine what we could do if we did it together. Imagine who we could take on in
this country if we did it together. As one team, we are unstoppable.”

So that is why they are afraid of him.

Here is Talarico talking about why he recently went to Ohio to get transgender surgeries in a mental
institution while dying repeatedly.

He said you’re supposed to stay home when you have the flu and not get other people sick. (WOKE.)
But he did note how Paxton and the inbreds who love him let their conspiracy theory freak flags just
_fly_ when he got sick and worked from home for a few days:

> TALARICO: These false claims got millions of views online. And this is an example of what I call
> the rage economy. Conspiracy theorists can post anything they want on the internet. They get
> clicks. The billionaires who own these platforms get rich. We all get angry, and nobody's life
> gets any better. Conspiracy theories don't put food on the table. They don't put gas in the tank.
> They don't actually improve our communities.

Do people in Texas want food and gas and better communities? Oh, they do? Huh.

It was interesting watching Talarico engage with the social conservatives in the room, the
right-wing Christians, since so much of the campaign has been Republicans saying his Christianity is
fake because he doesn’t embrace the conservative Christian gospel that Jesus died on the cross so
that fags and trans kids might burn in hell.

Why, he once even said that God is non-binary, which directly contradicts the thousands of Bible
verses about God’s dick.

He directly addressed that in this clip with a conservative Christian white woman. Note how he
admitted he was being provocative with the God being non-binary thing, while not remotely taking it
back.

> TALARICO: We as Christians, you and I are both members of the body of Christ, you are my sister in
> Christ, and it’s OK if you and I have disagreements about our interpretations of scripture, or
> certainly about political and policy topics. That should be encouraged, not discouraged, and we
> can do that as we’re called to do in scripture, do it with love.
> 
> And so I actually want to clarify a few of the things that you said, because I know that this has
> been used in attack ads all over the state. You know, the first comment, about God not being
> defined by gender, when I said that I was being provocative, and I want to admit that, and come
> clean about that.
> 
> But I think what I was saying was not controversial theologically. The idea that we can’t define
> God using human categories. Not only did I say that, but the late Charlie Kirk said that. The late
> Pope Benedict said that.

_Ohhhhhh shit, he reverse Charlie-Kirk-ed everybody!_ With a slice of dead Nazi pope on the side!

That’s what you get for making your campaign about God’s penis, Ken Paxton.

Here’s an exchange that’s getting a lot of attention, about abortion. He’s tailoring his
message, obviously, because he’s talking to a severely anti-abortion Catholic woman, a professor
from Fort Worth. But note how he goes about it. He has this way of almost demanding that people join
him in arguing in good faith, which we find interesting.

Talarico had earlier said, correctly, that the Bible is “silent on abortion.” This woman’s
interpretation, however, was that the Ten Commandments say “Thou shalt not kill,” and somehow
this applies to blastocysts. This is a good time to remind readers that we are not the audience for
this. Texas voters looking for any permission structure they can find to vote against Ken Paxton are
the audience for this.

First, Talarico met her where she was, saying that _if_ he agreed with her that abortion is murder
— he doesn’t — then he wouldn’t support it. He said, “it’s OK that you and I come to
different conclusions” about the theology, and even later expressed eagerness to talk about it
with her further.

And then he pivoted to Texas’s dangerous, murderous total abortion ban, which is leaving people to
die in emergency room parking lots from sepsis, because doctors are scared to provide any care that
could fall under the umbrella of “abortion.” He referred to 10-year-old girls who need abortion
care, and he called it “abortion care.”

“I think you and I agree more than we think we do. Again, the theological debate is interesting,
and you and I should grab a coffee or do a podcast, and I would love to get into that, but as a
policymaker, I’m always like how do we solve the problem, how do we get better outcomes? And
that’s what you want too!”

What was she going to say? “No, I do not want better outcomes, because I am a total monster”?

At one point, he said, “if we treat this issue as a public health problem and not a culture war
problem, then we can actually make progress together.” He talked about reducing the number of
_unwanted pregnancies_. (This is one of the tells of whether somebody truly has a _moral problem_
with abortion or whether they just want to control people’s bodies and sex lives. If they don’t
support reducing unplanned pregnancies, then they are just sicko forced birth perverts.)

We’re not the audience for this, again. But his audience was across from him, and she was
genuinely smiling by the end of that interaction. (Afterward she said she might even reconsider her
vote.)

He said GRRR TEXAS BORDER AARGH things, because he’s running to be the senator from Texas, saying
he’d stand up to Democrats if they try to do anything funny with the border. He said he doesn’t
support abolishing ICE, because again, he’s running to be the senator from Texas. But then he
tempered that with flaming progressive sentences like “We don’t want federal agents murdering
our neighbors before our very eyes.” He noted that Trump’s ICE is a “secret police force” of
“masked men in unmarked vehicles, coming through and terrorizing our communities.” He
specifically referenced the ICE murder of Texan Lorenzo Salgaudo.

> TALARICO: I think about Lorenzo Salgaudo, who was our fellow Texan, he lived in Houston for 35
> years. As a construction worker, he sent his three boys to college. His oldest is a public school
> teacher. His middle son is an engineer. His youngest is still in school. Lorenzo Salgaudo embodies
> the American dream, he’s exactly the kind of person we want in this country. His three boys
> called him _el mundo entero_, the whole world, and they watched as their whole world died in a
> video posted on Facebook. Lorenzo Salgaudo was a child of God, endowed with infinite worth.
> Lorenzo, our fellow Texan, deserves justice, and that’s exactly what I’ll fight for in the US
> Senate.

So that’s what he had to say about that.

He made very Texas sounds about protecting oil and gas jobs, saying, “I will stand up to my own
party if they mess with our oil and gas industry,” he said. But he also said he would stand up to
Republicans when they try to fuck with renewables.

You notice how good he is at just refusing to accept the notion that any Texas voters are his
enemies, or even that people are as opposed to each other as they think they are on these issues?

Obviously, we will hold him accountable when he’s in a position to legislate, and if he starts
throwing people under the bus, we’ll get his ass. But it appears he may have divined the secret
sauce to getting elected statewide as a Democrat in Texas, at least at this juncture. You’ll note
he’s not doing it by acting like a garbage human like Joe Manchin or John Fetterman.

Here’s one more, about data centers. “We have an affordability crisis because we have a
corruption crisis.” Hits even harder when you consider who his opponent is. And he mentioned that:
“We have an opportunity in this race not just to defeat the most corrupt politician in Texas, we
have an opportunity to defeat this entire corrupt system.”

In the third quarter, James Talarico raised $64 million, more than any Senate candidate in history.
He is ahead in just about every poll. We don’t want to say he’s going to do the thing, because
it’s not over until it’s over — Ken Paxton might introduce a secret weapon, like more of his
famous sex appeal or integrity! — but we think he’s going to do the thing.

What would that be like in six years if Talarico was in Jon Ossoff’s position and Texas had just
had a Democratic senator for six years, asking to be re-elected? The answer to that question is what
keeps every Republican strategist and politician up at night.
