The Pro–Patrick Clancy and Anti–Patrick Clancy Sides of the Internet Watched His Viral Interview. They All Came to the Same Conclusion.

====================================================================================================

Bari Weiss’ _60 Minutes_ was never going to change hearts and minds. Since the former New York
Times columnist and Free Press founder took over as CBS editor in chief in 2025, the show has
changed drastically and perhaps for the worse. Veteran reporters like Scott Pelley and Sharyn
Alfonsi were fired as executives meddled more and more in the show. Unqualified replacements, like
former Times columnist Nick Bilton, who has never produced a news television show before, took over
as _60 Minutes_’ executive producer. At this rate, if the show replaced its iconic ticking
stopwatch with an A.I.-generated image of an iPhone clock, it wouldn’t even be that surprising.

But last night, for its second episode under Weiss, the show had a marquee guest destined to bring
viewers back in, or at least to bring in the postbroadcast YouTube-clip crowd. Patrick Clancy, the
ex-husband of Lindsay Clancy, who was recently on trial for killing her three children in what she
says was a haze of postpartum psychosis, sat down for his first interview since the case ended in a
mistrial. Interviewed by former anti-abortion columnist and Brain cosplayer Ross Douthat, Patrick
Clancy sat down with his second wife, Dr. Rachel Danis (now also pregnant), to talk about his grief
since losing his three children.

Indeed, when it comes to what responsibility Patrick Clancy might bear for the deaths, neither
hearts nor minds have been changed.

The more pro-Clancy corners of the internet—like r/DuxburyDeathsFreeTalk, with its almost half a
million visitors per week—remain staunchly on his side. “After a major tragedy some people just
go to pot, physically age etc… turn to drugs, booze, ya know all that!” wrote one user just
after the episode aired. “He’s done the opposite! He takes care of himself which is what we all
should learn to do more of.” Meanwhile on TikTok, the people who previously believed that Clancy
did it are no less shaken in their convictions. “Patrick wasn’t really asked anything by the
host of actual substance,” said the _Crime Junkie_ podcast on TikTok. “Literally every question
that you guys would have for Patrick Clancy was not asked and was not answered. Watching this was
tough for me … Interviews like this are important but they’re important when you ask the right
questions.”

Meanwhile, people online are dissecting the sincerity of his wife’s smile, replaying her words in
slow motion to try to catch a flinch or a tick when she says “I can’t bring his kids back,” as
if it’ll crack the case wide open. Inconsistencies between the teaser and the segment, namely
details about how the two of them met that didn’t end up in the final interview segment itself,
have been picked apart. Anyone who previously thought that Clancy was the one who killed the
kids—something he has not been charged with or accused of by investigators or even the
defense—still seemed to believe that even after watching his interview.

The internet right now is filled with two people: one claiming they’re an empath and that
Clancy’s interview made them weep in commiserating grief, and another also claiming they’re an
empath and saying his interview made them feel nothing at all, sure that he was just faking it. The
interview was a retread that has only made every viewer more entrenched in their beliefs about what
happened in Clancy’s marriage.

The _60 Minutes_ episode is a strange entry in this never-ending saga. It offers no real insight
into the case, nor does it offer husbands a helpful framework for how to better support their own
wives. Clancy didn’t give too many details about the warning signs he may have missed, and spoke
only briefly about how the online mob has affected him personally since his children’s deaths.
“It is a huge distraction from what really matters here, which is perinatal mental health,” he
said. “It’s painful to see. And it makes it difficult to be in public, and it’s possible that
some of those people, after seeing lie after lie after lie, they start to believe it.”

The lackluster showing is a bleak reminder of how irrelevant _60 Minutes_—once the last stop for a
gutting, propulsive, and necessary interview—has become under the leadership of Weiss and her new
producers. An interview with the father of three killed children, previously heard only through
courtroom testimony, should have been an obvious slam dunk for the show. Instead, it was a
mealymouthed 15-minute interview that left much of the audience furious and all of the audience
wanting more. For those defending Clancy, _60 Minutes_ didn’t give him any actual real estate to
talk about how the conspiracy theories about him have affected his life, or even his new marriage
and soon-to-be fourth child. For those who believe he’s guilty, either directly or because he
didn’t get his wife the medical care she needed when she said she was struggling, the interview
was a passive victory lap. How could an interview like this end up so tedious in its final cut?

Today, following the _60 Minutes_ interview, Lindsay Clancy’s defense attorney called for the
judge to investigate the lone holdout juror, saying he filed his forms out inaccurately. He’s
specifically asking the judge to look into the juror’s alleged history of domestic violence, as
well as reports that he used his phone during deliberations, all details that would affect how fair
the trial really was.

Douthat didn’t ask Patrick Clancy or his new wife about this juror. There was no discussion about
this piece of the trial, which has captivated media attention more than Clancy’s full interview
did. Whether Lindsay is retried or not, Clancy will likely resume a private life, as is his right.
What’s remaining are more compelling and worrying questions that will continue to affect wives and
pregnant people: Do pregnant people have access to care when they experience postpartum depression?
Have we, as a disjointed country, learned anything valuable about how to best listen to women when
they say something is wrong with their physical or mental health? Have husbands learned how to
better support their wives? Even when Douthat broached these topics, it was to skate across the
surface. There are fringe benefits to that approach: If you don’t go that deep, you’ll never
need to worry about nuance.

All these questions are still lingering. All we know for sure is that _60 Minutes_ isn’t going to
ask any of them. The new _60 Minutes_ is no longer an institution you can trust for the final word.
By now, you can barely trust the show, it seems, for an interesting word.
