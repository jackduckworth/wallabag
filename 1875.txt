

====================================================================================================

Slow Horses to Neagley: the seven best shows to stream this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
SLOW HORSES

There are various blasts from the past as this sour and salty espionage drama returns for season six
– although some have more staying power than others. In a development that might hint at a show
approaching the end of its natural lifespan (although it's been renewed for a seventh season),
someone is killing former occupants of Slough House. Gary Oldman's Jackson Lamb is typically
unsentimental about this but other Horses are more concerned – particularly given the return of an
old antagonist who proved to be a ruthless and relentless foe with a connection to the team. Slow
Horses feels unlikely to surprise us at this point but it's still pungently scripted and beautifully
performed. 
_Apple TV, from Wednesday 16 September_

-------------------------

MONSTER: THE LIZZIE BORDEN STORY

[Ella Beatty in Monster: The Lizzie Borden Story.]
It's an unspoken rule of fact-based fiction that the older the source material, the wilder the
approach can be. This suits Ryan Murphy fine – the latest instalment of his
never-knowingly-understated Monster anthology deals with Lizzie Borden (Ella Beatty), a woman who
was accused of killing her father and stepmother with an axe in 1892. Borden was acquitted and lived
the rest of her life in the same town, so whether she deserves to be posthumously labelled a
"monster" is highly debatable. Still, various theories around the murders are tried out for lurid
size. 
_Netflix, from Thursday 17_ _September_

-------------------------

NEAGLEY

[Maria Sten in Neagley.]
Jack Reacher's female protege gets her own story in a drama which shares plenty of stylistic and
narrative DNA with its parent show. Ex-military PI Frances Neagley (Maria Sten) is, you guessed it,
a lone wolf. She's awkward, persistent and formidable. She's investigating the mysterious death of
an old friend, which looks like a drug-related suicide but may be linked to a sinister cult. She's
battling both the police department and the cult leaders – but fortunately, she's got some extra
muscle she can call on if necessary … 
_Prime Video, from Wednesday 16_ _September_

-------------------------

GOLDEN AXE

[From left: Liam McIntyre voices Ax Battler, Carl Tart voices Chronos 'Evil' Lait and Lisa Gilroy
voices Tyris Flare in Golden Axe.]
You'd imagine that people who are old enough to have fond memories of the 1989 Sega adventure game
Golden Axe might now be too old to watch a cheesily animated version of that game. This series
attempts to address that potential problem by undercutting the essential silliness of the scenario
(three warriors attempt to save the land of Yuria from the giant Death Adder) with self-aware
humour. It doesn't really land: the comedy is never quite cutting enough to work as self-parody,
while the plotting is too leaden to sustain much in the way of drama. 
_Paramount+, from Wednesday_ _16_ _September_

-------------------------

CITY OF BLOOD

[Soma Pysall as Phoebe in City of Blood.]
Set in the near future, this German drama (clunkily dubbed in English) is a pick'n'mix of modern
apocalyptic scenarios. Pandemics. Economic collapse. Rampant inequality. And it's boiling hot.
Berlin is a medieval nightmare (but thankfully, banging techno seems to have survived). We see this
world through the eyes of two sisters, Bea and Phoebe. Bea was adopted by a rich couple whereas
Phoebe deals blood to survive. When they are reunited, they uncover a terrible secret underpinning
society. It's incoherent but the berserk relish of the world-building has appeal. 
_Disney+, from Wednesday_ _16_ _September_

-------------------------

THE DOLL

[Tomasz Schuchardt and Sandra Drzymalska in The Doll.]
The 1890 novel by Bolesław Prus is considered to be one of Poland's greatest ever works of fiction.
It's a love story of sorts, exploring the desire of thwarted, working-class Stanisław to move into
Warsaw's high society – which to him is represented by the beautiful, elusive, frequently scornful
figure of Izabela, with whom he is obsessed. This adaptation will appeal to fans of the more pointed
and polemical end of period dramas: it's a lavish and imaginatively staged affair with an edge of
darkness and danger as Stanisław spirals into paranoia. 
_Netflix, from Wednesday_ _16_ _September_

-------------------------

MOBLAND

[Helen Mirren as Maeve Harrigan and Pierce Brosnan as Conrad Harrigan In MobLand.]
This shonky gangland drama returns, still charmingly unaware of its own absurdity. Conrad and Maeve
(Pierce Brosnan and Helen Mirren, chewing the scenery with real ferocity) are still nominally in
charge of the Harrigan empire but a comically bloody shootout could be the catalyst for change.
Meanwhile, Harry and Jan (Tom Hardy and Joanne Froggatt) are separating but their daughter Gina is
establishing a criminal operation of her own. Oh, and there's a flimsy subplot involving YouTubers
which is the only indication MobLand was written this side of 1997. 
_Paramount+, from Friday_ _18 September_
