

====================================================================================================

My Brilliant Career to Mourinho: the seven best shows to stream this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
MY BRILLIANT CAREER

Miles Franklin's 1901 coming-of-age novel about free-spirited country girl Sybylla Melvyn is
regarded as a proto-feminist classic. It's punchily brought to life in an adaptation that feels like
a dusty but dynamic Australian second cousin to Bridgerton. Philippa Northeast stars as Sybylla:
pushed reluctantly towards high society by her dirt-poor family but desperate to become a writer and
fiercely resistant to, as she sees it, being "slowly turned on the spit of propriety". However,
there's plenty about her family's financial and social circumstances that she doesn't fully
understand. Northeast is an effervescent lead while Christopher Chung brings quiet charisma to
Sybylla's love interest, Harry. 
_Netflix, from Thursday_ _13 August_

-------------------------

THE CHOSEN IN THE WILD WITH BEAR GRYLLS

[Bear Grylls talks to Dallas Jenkins in the woods in a scene from The Chosen In The Wild With Bear
Grylls.]
Performative Christian wholesomeness squared in this series in which Bear Grylls takes the cast of
religious drama The Chosen for gruelling but rewarding outdoor rambles around American natural
wonders including the Adirondack Forest and the Great Basin Desert. As is customarily the way with
such things, the combination of fresh air, nerve-racking challenges and extreme earnestness around
campfires brings many issues to the surface – and, one by one, the actors (who include Jonathan
Roumie, Paras Patel and Elizabeth Tabish) start to open up. 
_Prime Video, from Sunday 9 August_

-------------------------

MOURINHO

[José Mourinho points from the touchline in the titular documentary series.]
One of the more interesting and divisive characters of the Premier League era gets the deep-dive
documentary treatment in this three-part series which, helpfully for Netflix, coincides with José
Mourinho's arrival at Real Madrid. It's clearly made with Mourinho's full cooperation (as a tour of
his home, with a focus on his well-stocked trophy cabinet, suggests) but that doesn't mean it
glosses over the various controversies that have marked his career, most of which seem to stem from
his almost pathological sense of ambition. As he wryly points out: "You wouldn't do a documentary
with a guy who won nothing." _
Netflix, from Tuesday 11 August_

-------------------------

REACHER

[Alan Ritchson as Jack Reacher on a subway train.]
TV's foremost combiner of deadpan quips and deadly violence returns for another magnificently
concussive adventure. When Jack Reacher (played with relish by the mountainous Alan Ritchson)
encounters a crying woman on the subway, he gets more than he bargained for. He has stumbled into a
massive conspiracy and his attempts to set the record straight only entangle him further. Still,
even if it pits him against the rich and powerful, Reacher isn't a man to take no for an answer.
Very close to self-parody and absolutely none the worse for it. 
_Prime Video, from Wednesday 12 August_

-------------------------

WOMEN IN BLUE

[Natalia Tellez (Valentina), Barbara Mori (Maria), Amorita Rasgado (Gabina) and Ximena Sarinana
(Angeles) take on a serial killer in Women in Blue.]
This drama about an all-female division within the Mexican police is (very loosely) inspired by real
1970s events. But as the show returns, it seems more comfortable simply regarding itself as a
fiction. There is a slightly cartoonish quality to these shrewd, principled, immaculately turned-out
female cops as they battle both crime and conservative impulses within the force. There's another
serial killer on the loose – but the women don't trust their own police department to follow the
evidence wherever it might lead. Cue fun crime-fighting exploits with added subterfuge. _
Apple TV, from Wednesday 12 August_

-------------------------

AN AMPUTATION OBSESSION: THE MONSTER AND THE SURGEON

[The case of Neil Hopper is examined in An Amputation Obsession: The Monster and the Surgeon.]
The story of Neil Hopper will surely be attracting the attention of documentary-makers for years to
come. However, Prime is quick out of the traps with this true-crime special about a man who was
jailed for insurance fraud in 2025 in the wake of a journey into body modification that culminated
in him voluntarily losing both legs. The criminal case is explored but, more interestingly, the
documentary also looks at extreme body integrity dysmorphia and the underground networks devoted to
it. _
Prime Video, from Thursday 13 August_

-------------------------

EVERYBODY LIES

[Vincent Elbaz (Vincent) and Mariama Gueye (Alice) star in Everybody Lies.]
This engrossing French drama series seems very fitting for 2026, as it centres on Vincent Verner, a
former cop who has been fired from the force for getting a little too close to bringing down a rich,
deeply corrupt and dangerously well-connected man. However, his outsider status has made him useful
in a semi-official capacity – he's now part of a new unit charged with investigating cases and
organisations whose power and influence make them too big to fail. Can this righteous maverick
succeed where conventional law enforcement has failed? _
Channel 4, from Friday 14 August_
