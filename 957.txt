

====================================================================================================

You Watched the Bad Bunny Super Bowl Show. I Watched the “All-American” One. Oh My God.

====================================================================================================

Just because there’s one very famous story about an underdog taking on a leporine opponent and
winning does not mean it worked out that way for Turning Point USA in its quest to compete with Bad
Bunny’s Super Bowl halftime show. In fact, don’t insult the tortoise community by bringing them
into this. TPUSA has only itself to blame for the utter lameness of its All-American Halftime Show.

The right-wing nonprofit’s decision to offer counterprogramming to the official halftime show
followed a wave of conservative backlash over the selection of the Puerto Rican, Spanish-speaking
artist Bad Bunny as this year’s headliner. Though President Donald Trump has frequently struggled
to get big-name artists to perform at events associated with him, he was able to land Carrie
Underwood, Rascal Flatts, and more for his second inauguration, and that was before Nicki Minaj went
MAGA. I knew TPUSA’s show wouldn’t be able to compete with the production value or the star
power of the real show, but I had no idea it how bad it would be.

Then, about a week ago, the organization announced its slate of performers: Kid Rock would be
headlining, joined by Brantley Gilbert, Lee Brice, and Gabby Barrett. Kid Rock, he of the private
jet with a middle finger on its tail, embracing Trump is still vaguely disappointing—I’m old
enough to remember his glory days, such as they were—but no longer surprising. And if your
reaction to the latter of those three names is that they sound made up, well, I was right there with
you.

My report after watching? It was as dumb as I expected it to be, but satisfying in one important
way: They clearly struggled to fill the room. While it was more crowded than, say, a New York City
screening of _Melania_ (I would know), the VMAs this was not. At times, there was enough room
between that stage and the extremely subdued audience to safely do a cartwheel. The YouTube stream
earned 16 million views, so the audience was not no one, but it’s nevertheless nice to know that
when it came down to it, people can criticize mainstream pop culture’s supposedly overly woke
values till they’re blue in the face, but they didn’t want to skip their Super Bowl parties for
this gallery of has-beens and nobodies. That very much includes our president, who, despite
complaining bitterly about the performers, tuned in to the real thing anyway. We know this because
he posted a little review on Truth Social: He declared the show “one of the worst, EVER!”

Trump hates to be associated with losers, and he could sense the loser energy from the All-American
Halftime Show from Air Force One. I don’t actually believe he watched most of the Bad Bunny
performance, by the way—his ADHD is too raging. (His poor attention span is the excuse he gave for
not attending the game in person, though he was probably worried about booing too.) Some of us
weren’t as blessed as Trump in this regard. Don’t worry, though: I didn’t have to skip Bad
Bunny for Kid Rock and friends. It was pre-negotiated with my editor that I was allowed to watch the
real halftime show first.

TPUSA’s event began with an intro by the Department of War’s own Pete Hegseth, which led right
into an electric-guitar rendition of “The Star Spangled Banner” expressly designed for the
purpose of making Jimi Hendrix roll over in his grave. “This is real America,” some guy whose
identity I still don’t know announced early on, a member of a band that seems to require everyone
to wear their hair in a faux-hawk and had a song that contained a lyric that went, “My dad taught
me how to stand my ground.” _Literally who are any of these people?,_ I wrote in my notes. I kept
getting bored and picking up my phone to check if anyone had figured out if that was really Liam
Conejo Ramos in Bad Bunny’s performance (eventually, someone did and it wasn’t), only to
remember that I was supposed to be paying attention. A woman in a blue pantsuit performed—she was
my favorite, girl power!—and then a thick-necked man came out to sing about how “it ain’t easy
being country in this country nowadays.” Sample lyric: “little boys ain’t little girls.”
This was followed by a song about how he’s “hard to love.” I’ll bet.

Finally, it was time for the main event, Kid Rock, who graced us with his best-known song,
“Bawitdaba” while wearing a fedora and jean shorts. Does it still count as gracing us if he was
neither singing or managing to lip-sync convincingly? I feel for him on some level: At 55, it must
be a lot harder to summon the energy that song requires, and I’m worried about his joints with all
the jumping he does. After singing like, oh, half the song, he left the stage momentarily, at which
point a a cellist and a violinist came out. He was eventually reintroduced as “Robert Richie,”
which is his real name, thus signifying that the night was about to take a soulful shift. He sang a
slower song—dammit, I stopped paying attention, again! Sorry!—and before long we rolled into
some tribute clips of Kirk. The first, and hopefully last, All-American Halftime Show was over.
