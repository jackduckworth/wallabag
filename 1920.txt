Case not proven: Trent Alexander-Arnold's England recall is based on patchy evidence

====================================================================================================

Thomas Tuchel said Trent Alexander Arnold had enjoyed a good start to the season, which came as news
to those in England who have not been watching him at Real Madrid. It also, in truth, came as news
to those in Spain who have. If the England manager did not see enough in Alexander-Arnold to include
him in the World Cup in the summer he has not really had the chance to see enough to change that
decision as autumn begins. Two unremarkable league starts provide insufficient evidence with which
to make a case for the full-back's recall, or indeed his exclusion, but he is in the squad.

Alexander-Arnold's most high-profile match this season has been against Inter at the Santiago
Bernabéu. That night, he started from the middle of midfield. At the pre-match press conference,
José Mourinho had refused to answer a direct and apparently well-informed question about employing
the right-back in midfield, given the absence through injury or suspension of Eduardo Camavinga,
Arda Guler, Bernardo Silva, Aurélien Tchouaméni and Thiago Pitarch, as well as his determined
conclusion that Jude Bellingham is better used further up.

In the post-match press conference he said he had no other option, thanked Alexander-Arnold for
trying and admitted that the "specifics" of midfield were different from those of full-back,
mentioning physicality and perspective on the pitch. In short, this hadn't worked and Alexander
Arnold is not a midfielder.

That is not a reason not to include Alexander-Arnold for England, of course. Tuchel was clear in
saying the former Liverpool player had been called up as a right-back. Nor, though, has
Alexander-Arnold made some great change in his natural position after a first season in which he
played 30 of 56 Madrid games. Absent from the World Cup, Alexander-Arnold was one of eight players
able to join Mourinho at the start of pre-season. That might have give him a head start, and the
arrival of another coach offered him the chance of a new start too, but it has not worked out that
way.

On the opening day he began on the bench, coming on with 10 minutes remaining as Madrid finally
found the late Carlos Espí goal that defeated Espanyol. He then sat out their 4-1 win over Real
Sociedad. In both, Denzil Dumfries, the right-back signed over the summer, began ahead of him.

Against promoted Málaga, currently 19th, Alexander-Arnold got 90 minutes and offered a performance,
greeted with enthusiasm, which suggested maybe this could be a new beginning. This, one Madrid-based
paper said, was "a new Trent". It was the kind of performance that could lead, eventually, to an
international call-up. Alexander-Arnold had more touches and completed more passes than anyone else,
created two chances and provided Kylian Mbappé with the assist for Madrid's third goal in a 4-0
win. The pass was very much his kind of pass, and there were others too, a range and precision that
impressed.

Not much followed. Alexander-Arnold came on as a sub against Real Betis with the score at 0-0 and
they lost 1-0 to a Troy Parrott goal. He played 26 minutes. Against Rayo he remained on the bench.
And against Elche in midweek, he was withdrawn with three minutes of normal time remaining and with
Madrid having let a 2-0 lead slip to be drawing 2-2. Again, a late Espí goal saved them. It had
been a night of rotation; the conclusion drawn from Alexander-Arnold's inclusion was that he is not
going to start the derby against Atlético on Sunday. Dumfries, notably, has made four starts in the
league and one in the Champions League.

The doubts about Alexander-Arnold's defensive capabilities had not been allayed in that Elche game
or in the previous few. Of six league matches, he has started two, been introduced in two and sat
out two. There has been one assist and one international call-up.
