

====================================================================================================

Toy Story 5 to Hamlet: the seven best films to watch on TV this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
TOY STORY 5

Not particularly hot off the production line – Toy Story 4 was seven years ago – Woody, Buzz and
their boxful of plastic friends are back, but WALL-E director Andrew Stanton maintains the
franchise's beguiling focus on the joys and pitfalls of childhood. With the retired Woody (Tom
Hanks) absent, Joan Cusack's Jessie is the new sheriff in town/bedroom. But she faces an existential
threat when her owner, Bonnie, ostracised by her tech-savvy peers, gets a tablet – the superior
Lilypad "Lilly" (Greta Lee) – and loses all interest in play. While having a dig at society's
digital obsessions, it's also a nostalgic eulogy to a world where pure imagination and the capacity
to be silly are cherished. _
Wednesday 23 September, Disney+_

-------------------------

HAMLET

[Riz Ahmed in Hamlet.]
There is little Riz Ahmed can't turn his hand to, so it's no surprise that he gives a bravura
performance as Shakespeare's tragic hero in Aneil Karia's modern-day adaptation. The royal Danes are
now a wealthy British Hindu family of property developers, but the film retains the Bard's language
while cutting down the text to focus exclusively on Hamlet's trauma. It's a bold move that largely
pays off, as we follow the heir to the firm as he descends into angst, rage and revenge after
discovering his uncle Claudius (Art Malik) murdered his father. 
_Saturday 19 September, 10.05am, 6pm, Sky Cinema Premiere_

-------------------------

WASTEMAN

[From left: Davis Jonsson and Tom Blyth in Wasteman.]
The brutal realities of prison life in Britain are dissected in Cal McMau's claustrophobic, sweatily
tense drama. David Jonsson, soon to be the next Black Panther, plays a very different hero here:
Taylor, a drug addict incarcerated for manslaughter. He's soon to be eligible for parole so he's
trying to keep his head down. Unfortunately, his new cellmate, Dee (a menacing Tom Blyth), will do
anything to take over the jail's drug trade from Paul (Alex Hassell, definitely not in Rivals mode)
and forces the desperate Taylor to assist him. 
_Sunday 20 September, Netflix_

-------------------------

HOW TO LIVE ON EARTH

[Xiye Bastida in How to Live on Earth.]
Benedict Cumberbatch's measured tones introduce "the world's greatest how-to video" – Fredi
Devas's suggestion box of solutions to the climate crisis. The documentary sweeps across the globe
from China to Chad, Brazil to Singapore, with chapters focusing on ways to protect and respect
nature, create sustainable agriculture, even put a monetary value on biodiversity. It's all
tentatively optimistic despite the devastation on display, and the praiseworthy activists and
scientists profiled offer at least some hope for the future. 
_Sunday 20 September, YouTube_

-------------------------

NIRVANNA THE BAND THE SHOW THE MOVIE

[Jay McCarrol and Matt Johnson in Nirvanna: the Band the Show the Movie.]
This mockumentary is a sequel to a 2007 Canadian web series and 2017 Vice TV sitcom you probably
haven't heard of. But don't let that get in the way of enjoying a gloriously silly Back to the
Future rip-off. Endearingly inept Toronto musicians Matt Johnson and Jay McCarrol (playing
themselves) decide to skydive from the CN Tower to promote a gig they haven't even secured. But an
inadvertent trip in a time-travelling RV throws their plan into comic chaos. 
_Sunday 20 September, 6.45am, 6.15pm, Sky Cinema Premiere_

-------------------------

THE LOVE HYPOTHESIS

[Lili Reinhart and Tom Bateman in The Love Hypothesis.]
Weirdly, the bestselling novel on which Claire Scanlon's campus romance is based started life as
Star Wars fan fiction about Rey and Kylo Ren. Not that you'd know it, save for the involvement in
this film of Tom Bateman, Daisy Ridley's husband who stars as Adam, AKA "Dr Evil", a loathed
Stanford biology lecturer. He is persuaded by doctoral student Olive (Lili Reinhart) to pose as her
boyfriend, so her best pal, Anh (Rachel Marsh), will date a boy Anh thinks Olive still fancies. In
return, he will look more committed to the university and have his funding renewed. Love among the
lab coats ensues. 
_Wednesday 23 September, Prime Video_

-------------------------

28 YEARS LATER: THE BONE TEMPLE

[Chi Lewis-Parry as Samson and Ralph Fiennes as Dr Kelson in 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple.]
For the second instalment of the post-pandemic horror trilogy, Nia DaCosta takes the directorial
reins from Danny Boyle for a poignant yet surprisingly funny tale, which largely shuns the series'
rabid terror in favour of pathos and a tiny sliver of hope. Teenager Spike (Alfie Williams) has been
captured by Jack O'Connell's disturbed Satanic cult leader Jimmy and his gang. Meanwhile, Dr Kelson
(the inestimable Ralph Fiennes) develops a bizarrely moving relationship with an infected man,
Samson (Chi Lewis-Parry). 
_Friday 25 September, 9pm, Sky Cinema Premiere_
