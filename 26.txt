The Saga of Haley Stevens’ Fiancé Keeps Getting Weirder

====================================================================================================

The question of “what the hell Haley Stevens is doing” remains an active topic of discussion in
Michigan and around the country, but at least one theory is gaining traction: Whatever it is, it
seems to have something to do with her fiancé, Jason Turkish.

Over the weekend, Stevens, who had previously endorsed Abdul El-Sayed for Senate after losing to him
in August’s Democratic primary, pulled a heel turn and endorsed his opponent, Republican Mike
Rogers—this after Stevens spent months likening Rogers to Donald Trump and calling his agenda
“extreme” and “corrupt.” It also was only a few days after Turkish, who is also a Democrat,
announced that he was voting for Rogers, primarily because of El-Sayed’s criticisms of Israel.
Stevens had been publicly critical of a pro-El-Sayed ad, but as of Friday, she was telling everyone
she spoke to, including the New York Times, that she still planned to vote for him. That same day,
Turkish went on TMZ, where he declared, “I don’t think she’s going to be supporting Abdul
El-Sayed very much longer.” He was right, it turns out. Cue that disastrous Anderson Cooper
interview.

Turkish is a 39-year-old lawyer who specializes in disability rights. He is also Jewish and active
in pro-Israel advocacy. Stevens is not Jewish, but has said she is considering converting before her
and Turkish’s wedding next summer. The two announced their engagement in August.

As Stevens’ shocking switch-up played out over the weekend, speculation about Turkish’s role in
it and influence over Stevens grew. A former Michigan governor who had dinner with the couple on
Friday evening told Gongwer News Service that Turkish scolded Stevens right in front of him. “He
was working her over. The way he was saying it was, ‘Everyone is disappointed in you,’ ” Jim
Blanchard said.

Elsewhere, people who claimed to know Stevens reported disturbing behavior on Turkish’s part. One
self-described friend, Jordan Acker, posted to X that he could not reach out to Stevens because
Turkish took her phone away. (Turkish denied this, claiming Haley chose to turn her phone off. He
has since made his posts private.) A former staffer also went to X to accuse Turkish of
“vaporizing Haley’s future so that he can be a main character in this moment,” adding that he
was an “extraordinarily toxic presence, was verbally abusive to staff and had some of the worst
political instincts I’ve ever witnessed.” Publications like Mother Jones have pointed out that
Turkish seems “dedicated to ruining [Stevens’] career.”

More than observer has compared Stevens and Turkish to another couple, Elizabeth Holmes and Billy
Evans, whose depiction in the documentary _You Can See Everything_ has made them the subject of a
lot of chatter in recent weeks. Some of the same people who came out of the movie asking, “Has she
been unfairly villainized when she’s really the victim of a toxic man in her life?” may now have
the same question about Stevens and Turkish. As tempting as it is to wonder if actress Amanda
Seyfried might be available to parachute into Michigan to crack some skulls, as with Holmes and
Evans, discovering that the male half of a couple is noxious does not actually exonerate the female
half. If she doesn’t like how he’s treating her, by all means, she should dump him, but
unfortunately, she may be with him _because_ he’s like this. It’s fully possible that Stevens
and Turkish (and Holmes and Evans) are both sinister, and are in fact in sinister cahoots together.
From a certain angle, it’s even a little romantic.

At least they’ve got each other. And the wedding, by the way, appears to be fully on. On Monday, a
campaign reporter shared that on Monday Stevens sent out save-the-dates in the form of iCloud
calendar invitations for her wedding. Nothing like a little wedding planning to distract yourself
from a professional crisis.
