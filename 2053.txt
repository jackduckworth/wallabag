Putin has Trump right where he wants him

====================================================================================================

America’s descent into chaos and depravity was foretold 10 years ago at a press conference. Donald
Trump, who had just been nominated for president at the 2016 Republican National Convention, issued
an invitation for one of America’s foes to hack the emails of Hillary Clinton, the former
secretary of state and Democratic nominee for president. “Russia, if you’re listening, I hope
you’re able to find the 30,000 emails that are missing,” he said. “I think you will probably
be rewarded mightily by our press.”

The call came only days after emails from the Democratic National Committee were released following
the hacking of the organization’s computer servers, which was widely assumed to have been
perpetrated by Russian agents. As would become the habit over the following decade, many dismissed
Trump’s remarks as mere trolling that weren’t intended to be taken seriously. 

This was not the first time he had invoked Russia. Throughout the primary season, Trump had gushed
in multiple interviews about Russian President Vladimir Putin and signaled his intentions to draw
closer to Putin once in office. Trump even defended the dictator’s extra-judicial killings,
blithely explaining, “Our country does plenty of killing.” But the press conference did mark the
first time he enlisted Russia as his ally. 

The overtures continued throughout Trump’s first term, most infamously when he sided with Putin
during a bilateral meeting over America’s own intelligence agencies, which had found that Russia
had interfered in the 2016 election. The president suggested an “election integrity” sharing
operation between the two countries, a proposal that made it clear Putin had Trump wrapped around
his little finger. 

Then came Russia’s war of aggression against Ukraine. Throughout the 2024 election, Trump pledged
to end the war in Ukraine within 24 hours of taking office — or of being elected. (His rhetoric
often shifted.) He apparently believed that he could get on the phone with Ukrainian President
Volodymyr Zelenskyy and order him to immediately surrender or face his mighty wrath. Needless to
say, this did not happen, and since then Trump has grown increasingly frustrated that he’s been
shown to be nothing more than a paper tiger, a frustration that has now reached fever pitch due to
his abject failure in the Iran war. Desperate for a “win” — by which he means a Russian win
— the president is now in the process of completely selling out Ukraine to his good friend Vlad.

In recent days, Trump has signed an executive order temporarily lifting the sanctions on Russian
oil. While supposedly done to secure diesel fuel for the United States, which has seen prices
skyrocket due to the president’s war of choice with Iran, the act is in practice a sign of
goodwill toward Putin, America’s enemy, and will help him fund his war against Ukraine, our
erstwhile ally. A few days later, Trump waived several additional sanctions that a bipartisan
congressional majority had passed just a few weeks ago at the behest of the late Sen. Lindsey
Graham, R-S.C., who foolishly believed himself to have the president under his control. 

Worse yet, in what will go down as one of the most appalling betrayals by a U.S. president in
history, Trump and his good friend Elon Musk apparently sabotaged Ukrainian intelligence by slowing
the Starlink and Starshield communications systems the country relies on for its defensive and
offensive weapons systems. Russia took swift advantage of the favor and unleashed one of the most
massive bombardments of the war, killing civilians and hitting Ukraine’s energy sector. Trump also
called for Zelenskyy’s ouster, and in an interview with Bloomberg published Sunday, the Ukrainian
president accused Trump of pushing the country to surrender to Russia.

Trump has a number of apparent rationales for these moves. He wants to be seen before the midterms
to be doing something about the price of gas and diesel. But that is a crisis of his own making due
to his misbegotten war in Iran, for which he has no way out that will solve the problem. His ongoing
war has altered the global energy markets in ways that are not easily fixed, which was the reason no
president before him was stupid enough to attack Iran. So now Trump is blaming Ukraine because it is
bombing Russian oil refineries and demanding they stop so that Russia can sell its diesel — and
finance its war, which led to the sanctions in the first place. Not to be overlooked is that Russia
is a staunch ally of Iran and is helping them fight us, which Trump has waved off as having
“nothing.” Some might argue that could constitute providing “aid and comfort” to America’s
enemy.

Meanwhile, fingers are being pointed in multiple directions. Musk said the government is to blame
for the Starlink/Starshield gambit. According to sources in the Pentagon, the order came from the
White House. Trump claimed he doesn’t know anything about it. Secretary of State Marco Rubio
shrugged that “it just sort of happened.” Now, lest the Europeans get it in their heads that
they should try to step up their own defenses by restricting Starlink in favor of a European-based
system, the U.S. government is reportedly threatening them with “devastating” or
“destructive” economic retaliation.

As a cherry on top of the administration’s full-on embrace of Russia, the Wall Street Journal
reported that Putin has floated a summit with Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping to decide
Ukraine’s fate. (On Monday, Trump told reporters he was open to a meeting but denied it would
focus on Ukraine, saying he wanted to discuss denuclearization.) If Trump knew any history, he
would know that such a meeting would prove to be his Yalta, the trilateral conference between
President Franklin Roosevelt, British Prime Minister Winston Churchill and Soviet Premier Joseph
Stalin that carved up Europe in the wake of World War II. The summit became widely seen by the right
as “the sell-out at Yalta” because the Soviets ended up with Eastern Europe, but things have
changed within the GOP. 

The summit is clearly intended to elevate Russia back to superpower status, which it is not in any
economic or military sense, and to demonstrate a new order in which Trump, Putin and Xi run the
world. In reality, Putin and Xi now have the United States dancing to their tune, handing them power
and influence to dominate their respective spheres. 

The president’s flagrant betrayal of Ukraine is an outrage that has seemingly awakened some of the
slumbering Republicans who know they are facing a drubbing at the polls in the quickly approaching
midterm elections. Whether it will arouse the consciences of enough to truly step up and stop this
breach of trust is unknown. But the mere fact that Trump has gone this far to salve his ego, defy
his critics by embracing Putin even more fervently and drum up a lame excuse for what may be the
worst national security decision in American history leaves this country weaker, poorer and more
distrusted around the world. It hardly seems possible, but we have reached yet another new low.
