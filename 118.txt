Emily St. John Mandel on her new novel Exit Party, parallel Americas, and the art of the bad first draft.

====================================================================================================

Emily St. John Mandel’s new novel, _Exit Party_, is set in Los Angeles in the first spring after
the collapse of the United States. In this excerpt, she explains how the book’s two versions of
the country grew out of her thinking about the counter life of a state. This partial transcript has
been edited and condensed for clarity.

EMILY BAZELON: SO YOU’RE BUILDING THIS WORLD IN LOS ANGELES, THIS POST-COLLAPSE WORLD. AND WHILE I
DON’T WANT TO HAVE TOO MANY SPOILERS IN THIS PODCAST, I FEEL LIKE WE CAN’T HAVE NONE. SO I AM
GOING TO SAY THAT YOU’RE ACTUALLY BUILDING TWO WORLDS, THAT THERE’S A DOUBLING THAT’S
HAPPENING. THERE’S A SIDE A OF THIS BOOK AND A SIDE B. AND IS ONE OF THEM A SIMULATION OF THE
OTHER? IT FEELS LIKE YOU’RE REALLY INTERESTED IN THE THEME OF DOUBLING THROUGHOUT THE BOOK.

• Video from Slate 

Emily St. John Mandel: I am. No, neither is a simulation. Here’s what I was thinking about. And
actually, you know what? I want to back up for a second and just acknowledge, in my understanding,
physicists are more or less evenly split on the question of whether or not we’re living in a
multiverse, which is fascinating, and incidentally has real implications for what constitutes
speculative fiction.

I have spent a lot of time in my work over the years thinking about the “counter life.” So your
counter life, that is the life you did not live, where you went to a different school, you married a
different person, you took a different job, immigrated instead of staying, whatever that inflection
point was. And with this book, I found myself thinking, you know what? I’ve spent so much time
thinking about the counter life of the individual, but what is the counter life of the state?

Because that is a really interesting thought experiment and every country has multiple or maybe even
infinite counter lives:, just imagining the election that went the other way, or a decision taken to
go to war versus not, or a set of policies that were enacted or weren’t. So I started off, as I
mentioned, in a version of the US that feels a little bit like ours—w. We live in a very fractured
country—, just extrapolated forward by a few steps. But then I thought, what would be an
interesting counter life to that country? So that was where I got the doubling and thinking of a
fully different version of the United States.
