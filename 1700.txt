

====================================================================================================

Heretic to Love Me: the seven best films to watch on TV this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
HERETIC

Sophie Thatcher (Yellowjackets) and Chloe East (The Fabelmans) play a pair of unworldly Mormon
missionaries who arrive at the door of an avuncular Englishman hoping to make a new convert. That
Englishman, however, is played by Hugh Grant – once a foppish romcom idol, now deep into his
villain era – and what at first seems like a playful willingness to debate religion turns into
something much more terrifying. With a script by A Quiet Place co-writers Scott Beck and Bryan Woods
to work with (the two also co-direct), Grant commands the screen with glee. It's a career-best
performance, with enough dark miracles to fully restore your faith in A24's waning horror output.
_Saturday 1 August, 9.35pm, Sky Cinema Premiere_

-------------------------

LOVE ME

[Steven Yeun and Kristen Stewart in Love Me.]
Millennia have passed since humanity's extinction, and on Earth's half-frozen ocean a
smart-tech-enabled buoy (Kristen Stewart) floats alone, emitting a "waiting to connect" signal. One
day this is answered by a passing satellite (Steven Yeun), and together the devices search the
internet, hoping to derive an understanding of life and love from this oddly incomplete record.
First-time film-makers Sam and Andy Zuchero combine animation and live action into awe-inspiring
visuals – and there's comedy too, in Stewart and Yeun's portrayal of the YouTube influencer couple
the devices choose for avatars.
_Saturday 1 August, 10.25am, Sky Cinema Premiere_

-------------------------

SINGIN' IN THE RAIN

[Gene Kelly in Singin' in the Rain.]
If you're one of the many devotees of Damien Chazelle's 2016 Oscar-winner La La Land – or maybe
just in need of a cheer-up – be sure to check out this foundational 1952 Hollywood musical. Gene
Kelly's genius is on full display as Don Lockwood, a star of the silent era who is attempting to
ride out the transition to sound cinema with his integrity intact. There are also glorious
supporting turns from Donald O'Connor and Debbie Reynolds, while the defiant exuberance of that
title song shines through every scene, and every rain cloud. 
_Sunday 2 August, 8pm, Sky Cinema Greats_

-------------------------

STAND BY ME

[From left: Jerry O'Connell, River Phoenix, Wil Wheaton and Corey Feldman in Stand by Me.]
"I never had any friends later like the ones I had when I was 12." With that closing line, this 1986
adaptation of a Stephen King novella cemented its status as the ultimate coming-of-age movie. Its
1950s-set story concerns four boys (including an iconic performance from the late River Phoenix) who
set out on a macabre journey to find the body of a missing classmate. Stand By Me was always a
classic, but subsequent events – including the tragic death of much beloved director Rob Reiner
– have invested rewatches with added poignancy. 
_Tuesday 4 August, 3am, Sky Cinema Greats_

-------------------------

ALL THAT HEAVEN ALLOWS

[Rock Hudson and Jane Wyman in All That Heaven Allows.]
The lush melodramas of Douglas Sirk have influenced film-makers all over the world, including John
Waters, Wong Kar-Wai, Pedro Almodovár and Todd Haynes, whose 2002 film Far from Heaven, starring
Julianne Moore and Dennis Haysbert, is a direct descendent of this 1955 film. Jane Wyman plays Cary,
a middle-aged widow whose suburban life is disrupted for ever when she falls for hunky, younger
gardener Ron (Rock Hudson). It still enthrals with emotion as intensely vivid as its colour palette.

_Thursday 6 August, 4.50pm, Film4_

-------------------------

THE LAST HOUSE

[Greta Lee as Ann in The Last House.]
The population has been confined indoors because of a little-understood external threat, meaning
parents must homeschool their children, supplies are rationed and communication with neighbours can
happen only at a distance … sound familiar? The Last House comes on like a Covid lockdown
flashback but it's actually an intriguing sci-fi horror, with a plot reminiscent of 2007 film The
Mist. It benefits greatly from the presence of charming leads Wagner Moura (The Secret Agent) and
Greta Lee (Past Lives), as a couple forced to extremes for their family's survival. 
_Friday 7 August, Netflix_

-------------------------

HOARD

[Saura Lightfoot-Leon in Hoard.]
If a film-watching experience can be described as "fetid", then Hoard is that – but in a good way.
Writer-director Luna Carmoon was only 26 when she made her debut feature, announcing herself as a
thrillingly distinctive talent in British cinema. Saura Lightfoot Leon stars as Maria, a young,
care-experienced woman whose loving but unstable childhood is revealed in the detritus of her
chaotic present. Hayley Squires (I, Daniel Blake) plays Maria's bin-diving, hoarder mother and
Joseph Quinn (Stranger Things) is her foster brother turned love interest. 
_Friday 7 August, 11.05pm, BBC Two_
