Donald Trump’s Reaction to His New, Fawning Time Cover Tells Us One Thing for Absolutely Sure

====================================================================================================

As asinine as it is that our president decided to pick a fight with Time magazine because he
doesn’t like the photo of him that was chosen for its most recent cover, what really grinds my
gears is that, well, I see his point. It _is_ a pretty bad picture.

If you haven’t seen it yet, I’m talking about a cover that Trump, by all accounts, should have
been thrilled with: It features the cover line “His Triumph” and essentially implies he solved
Middle East peace, which could help with a certain prize he’s obsessed with. Trump has long been
both enthralled with legacy media institutions like Time and harbored a chip on his shoulder about
how they cover him, so finally, here was a win. But Trump couldn’t see past the choice of cover
photo, because of course he couldn’t. So he fired off a classic rant on Truth Social: “Time
Magazine wrote a relatively good story about me, but the picture may be the Worst of All Time. They
‘disappeared’ my hair, and then had something floating on top of my head that looked like a
floating crown, but an extremely small one. Really weird!”

He went on: “I never liked taking pictures from underneath angles, but this is a super bad
picture, and deserves to be called out. What are they doing, and why?”

Again, I am loathe to take Trump’s side here, but is he not right that it’s a “super bad
picture”? Have I too not transformed into a petty tyrant when confronted with an ungainly
representation of myself? My bad behavior has consisted of begging a friend to delete it, not
wild-eyed ranting, but still. I would also hate it if a photo of me “disappeared” my hair or
captured me from “underneath angles,” famously the worst angles, and goddammit, Trump has once
again gifted us some incredible syntax here. “Deserves to be called out,” he wrote, as if it was
bad service from an airline. “What are they doing, and why?” I love how he’s suddenly capable
of asking direct, prosecutorial questions when it’s about something that doesn’t matter at all.
I’m a little confused about the “floating crown” part, though, if we’re being honest. Does
he just mean the M in the magazine’s logo?

It should go without saying that I don’t support Trump using the office of the presidency to whine
about pictures of himself he doesn’t like. All I’m saying is that I get it. Who among us
hasn’t felt this? Before long, his political opponents, including California troll-in-chief Gavin
Newsom, were sort of making his point for him:

Still, I was surprised, frankly, that Trump still has it in him to be this vain. It’s not like he
looks great most of the time. He’s 79! I’ve seen so many other awful pictures of him! His famous
coif might be styled to look some version of presentable on TV, but it’s not all that surprising
that it looks a little thin from below. But maybe this being on a cover that was ostensibly supposed
to be praising him made it sting that much harder.

In Time’s defense, the magazine is not beholden to Trump or making his administration look
good—physically or otherwise—nor should it be. The way the photo might be slightly undercutting
the cover line is part of what makes it interesting, and a strong visual choice. As for Trump, he
has out-narcissist-ed himself once again. It’s not a good look, sir, and deserves to be called
out.
