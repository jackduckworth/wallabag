TV tonight: Idi Amin's forced expulsion of Asians from Uganda

====================================================================================================

ESCAPING IDI AMIN

9PM, BBC TWO 
In 1972, Uganda was home to 80,000 Asians. But 18 months after dictator Idi Amin came to power, he
gave them 90 days to get out. In this deeply affecting two-part documentary, people who lived
through this as children share their terrifying and complex testimonies – from curfews to daily
radio countdowns, extreme street violence and abuse, and massacres. _Hollie Richardson_

THANK YOU SIR KEV

8PM, BBC ONE 
Rugby league legend Kevin Sinfield has raised millions of pounds for motor neurone disease since his
best friend and fellow sports star Rob Burrow was diagnosed in 2019 (he died of the disease two
years ago). Prince William leads the tributes to Sinfield while friends and family paint the full
picture of the man and his achievements. _HR_

CLICKBAIT CLINIC WITH STACEY DOOLEY

8.30PM, BBC ONE 
Dooley concludes her series on viral medical claims with the most emotive conundrum so far: can the
dog deworming treatment, fenbendazole, cure terminal human cancer? Surely not, but questioning
desperate people clinging to any sliver of hope is, as she discovers, not a pleasant task. _Jack
Seale_

PIERRE

9PM, CHANNEL 4

[A woman is restrained by four police offices at a demonstration]
Brenda (Cherrelle Skeete) is arrested while fighting for justice over her nephew Michael's death,
and the more duty solicitor Pierre (David Harewood) "sniffs around", the more resistance and threats
he encounters. Meanwhile, was the attack on his son Eli a warning? "For God's sakes, why would
someone go to all this trouble to stop me digging around if there wasn't something bigger going on?"
_Ali Catterall_

CRYSTAL LAKE

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC

What is a woman to do after mutilating two men to death and feeding their bodies to a bear? Go on a
self-help retreat, of course! That's what Pam (Linda Cardellini) has planned in episode two of the
stomach-churning Friday the 13th prequel. Meanwhile, local cop Levon – who has the hots for Pam
– finds the remains in the woods. _HR_

TRAUMA: MY BROKEN BRAIN

10PM, CHANNEL 4

This series about young people dealing with the aftermath of strokes is a tough watch. However,
seeing Dan (a dancer), Saskia (a social butterfly) and Dec (who has just begun a new relationship)
deal with their changed realities is uplifting, too – the trio face a long road but their
resilience is remarkable._Phil Harrison_

FILM CHOICE

BACKROOMS (KANE PARSONS, 2026), HBO MAX

[A shot from behind of a man walking down a sparse yellow corridor]
Director Kane Parsons was just 20 when he shot this genre-redefining horror, based on the
"creepypasta" YouTube series he had been developing since his mid-teens. Chiwetel Ejiofor stars as
Clark, a failed architect who discovers an apparently endless liminal space behind the strip mall
store where he works and, depressingly, also lives. Renate Reinsve is Clark's therapist, Mary, a
woman working through her own traumas around enclosed spaces, who nonetheless follows him into these
backrooms to attempt a rescue. The wallpaper may be monotonously yellow, but there are scares of
every shade here and, underlying it all, the heart-pounding thrill of pure originality. _Ellen E
Jones_
