Elon Musk’s Biggest Twitter Nemesis Is an 87-Year-Old Pulitzer Prize Finalist

====================================================================================================

Did you know that Elon Musk _reads_ _books_? And has, in fact, read _many_ books? And has watched
movies? And enjoys things? That is what Tesla’s CEO—who has just been promised $1 trillion of
stock compensation over the next 10 years should he manage to reinvigorate car sales and build an
army of 1 million robots—has been hoping to impress upon X users over the past week.

On Monday: “Man on Fire is great!” Sunday: “Fifth Element has great style.” The Thursday
before that, more detailed praise of 1997’s _The Fifth Element_: “Great movie.” Meanwhile, X
boosted a promoted post from the book-summarizing app Blinkist insisting that “Elon reads a lot”
and suggesting “9 nonfiction books” he has recommended.

One author he will not promote, however? The 87-year-old Pulitzer finalist and Twitter legend Joyce
Carol Oates, whose “laboriously pretentious drivel” would be less enjoyable than “eating a bag
of sawdust,” per the world’s wealthiest man.

Why launch this stray at the woman who gave us _We Were the Mulvaneys_ and _Blonde_, among dozens of
other classics—including a handful of poems for Slate? Well, one thing many people have long known
about Oates, even if they’ve never read her award-winning short stories, is that she loves to
tweet, a lot, in a rather outrageous manner. This has continued well into the Musk era of
X-née-Twitter, with the liberal Oates occasionally directing her snipes and barbs at the
platform’s white nationalist owner.

In this most recent instance, JCO offered some thoughts after Texas state Sen. Roland Gutierrez
kicked off a beef with Musk by posting a video outside Tesla’s Austin Gigafactory, where he
referred to Musk as a “greedy hog” for taking the trillion-dollar stock agreement, along with
countless federal subsidies, while everyday Americans were losing their SNAP benefits. Musk
responded with rage, clarifying that he would not earn the trillion dollars if he failed to meet
investor demands and denigrating the lawmaker as “a taker, not a maker. All you’ve done your
whole life is take from the makers of the world.”

Musk fans naturally loved that, with one conservative writer approvingly sharing a screenshot. It
was at this point that Oates entered the picture, quote-tweeting the post with an extended
observation.

> So curious that such a wealthy man never posts anything that indicates that he enjoys or is even
> aware of what virtually everyone appreciates—scenes from nature, pet dog or cat, praise for a
> movie, music, a book (but doubt that he reads); pride in a friend’s or relative’s
> accomplishment; condolences for someone who has died; pleasure in sports, acclaim for a favorite
> team; references to history. In fact he seems totally uneducated, uncultured. The poorest persons
> on Twitter may have access to more beauty & meaning in life than the “most wealthy person in the
> world.”

>  — Joyce Carol Oates (@JoyceCarolOates) November 8, 2025

This obviously got some attention from the liberals on X, one of whom told her to “pinch his
wretched little dick off you icon.” But it also drew complaints from the most sycophantic Musk
stans, who will always volunteer their time to defend him. Sharing screenshots of the
tweet—presumably to avoid JCO’s wrath—various Tesla investors ran interference and accused
Oates of “dehumanizing” their king, to his vocal gratitude. “Everything she says in her post
about me can be shown to be demonstrably false with a simple search,” Musk whined in one reply.
“Oates is a lazy liar and … an abuser of semicolons!”

The aforementioned “bag of sawdust” post came in response to a screenshot of older tweets in
which Musk had praised Iain M. Banks’ _The Culture_—including one reply to Cory Doctorow in
which Musk posited that Banks “certainly wasn’t pro-union in the Culture books. At all. And
wouldn’t be in the case of Tesla.”

Now, here’s the thing: Anyone who’s actually read Banks and the _Culture_ books knows well that
the late Scottish author was an avowed leftist who loathed oligarchs and repeatedly described the
setting of his best-known series as a “socialist” or “communist” utopia whose citizens
transcend the gender binary. So, pretty distant from Musk’s transphobia and far-right
techno-centrism. Not that this is the only time Musk has clearly misunderstood a book he claims to
love: As I wrote two years ago, his assertion that he based X’s lewd A.I. chatbot on _The
Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy_ betrayed a complete misunderstanding of the iconic series _and_
of author Douglas Adams’ animating philosophy.

Joyce Carol Oates, for her part, had spent much of her post-diss free time ignoring Musk and
actually discussing literature with other readers at the Charleston Literary Festival. But like any
true poster, she couldn’t stay away for long. The day after the festival, Oates refocused on the
hurt Tesla CEO, resharing an anti-Musk newsletter from Robert Reich and responding to a fan of her
Musk insult with an explanation: “Truly it was out of curiosity: why a person with unlimited
resources exhibits so little appreciation or even awareness of the things that most people value as
giving meaning to life.” It didn’t stop Musk lovers from swarming her replies. So the prolific
octogenarian doubled down, retweeting other Tesla and Musk haters and praising the mogul’s
estranged transgender daughter. (“She is impressive! A normal parent would be very proud.”)

It should be noted that this isn’t the first time Musk has been compelled to interact with Oates:
After last year’s election, when JCO decried Republicans’ anti-trans campaign ads, the devoted
transphobe J.K. Rowling sneered at Oates’ concern, while Musk referred to the author as “a bit
of an extinctionist.”

If you’d been on Twitter in the early days, this all might strike you as a completely bonkers
development. Not long ago, it was Oates who was the far more notorious tweeter, having racked up a
series of Islamophobic, racist, classist, and simply out-of-touch posts that earned her widespread
notoriety within the literary world. (There was also that time she randomly posted a gross picture
of her blistering foot.) JCO was chided often, and rightly, for all those tweets, the output of an
old-school white liberal whose understandings of social justice and liberalism certainly predated
the Obama-era internet’s. Even more wildly, this was at a time when Elon Musk was the far more
normal tweeter, who chose to avoid wading into politics after getting yelled at for praising
Margaret Thatcher in the wake of her death.

How things have turned. Now Oates is the one ragging on Musk for his anti-empathy stance, citing the
great Denis Johnson in her attempt to try to understand the tech billionaire, and all but calling
him a loser while lauding Musk enemy Zohran Mamdani. The feud has become a trending topic on X,
summarized by its A.I. bots. And Musk fans are pointing to his _Fifth Element_ tweets to defend him.
To paraphrase Iain Banks himself, reality makes a lot less sense than fiction these days.
