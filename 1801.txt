I Did a Deep Dive Into Natalie Harp’s Letters to Donald Trump. One Particular Line Will Haunt Me.

====================================================================================================

A few things become immediately clear upon reading two letters Trump White House aide Natalie Harp
allegedly wrote to President Donald Trump, which were published Thursday by the Daily Beast: One is
that Harp, a 35-year-old woman in possession of a college diploma, a master’s degree, and a
$150,000-a-year job in the White House, has absolutely no idea how to use a comma. But another,
probably of more urgent concern, is that none of the media reports that describe Harp as scarily
obsessed with Trump appear to be exaggerating even a little bit.

The letters, previously seen by reporters and quoted in books and articles, were published in full
this week amid a late-August frenzy over Harp and her role in the Trump administration. (The
well-sourced but, according to some critics, embellishment-prone journalist Michael Wolff said he
obtained them while working on a recent biography of Trump. Though they haven’t been verified by
anyone beyond Wolff at this point, New York Times reporters have claimed to have seen letters too,
and the bits they’ve quoted appeared to match language contained in these.)

As to why everyone is suddenly talking about Harp, her official title is special assistant and
executive assistant to the president, but unofficially, she’s become known as one of Trump’s
closest aides and a key gatekeeper during his second term. She’s apparently so important that she
was one of the few people who accompanied the president when he secretly fled _Air Force One_ over
security concerns last month. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, not to mention a plane full of
journalists, was not among those whisked away, leading to reasonable questions about why a junior,
little-known figure like Harp was chosen. But all of this might have been quickly forgotten were it
not for Georgia Sen. Jon Ossoff, who decided to reference a certain “Natalie” in a recent
speech, kicking off a weeklong (and counting) news cycle all about Harp.

And now we’ve got these letters. Both appear have been written to Trump during or after a May 2023
trip he took to Scotland and Ireland to visit his golf courses there. The first is an apology, and
the second is sort of an apology as well, but also a journal entry–like reflection, thank-you
note, and love letter, all wrapped up into one. What is she actually apologizing for? The letters
are so overtly sycophantic that I’d believe that she makes a habit of regularly apologizing in
writing just in case she happens to have done anything wrong lately. In the first letter, she refers
to walking on one of the golf courses rather than taking a cart. Does that mean that Trump
specifically chided her for walking on a golf course instead of taking a cart, or does it mean that
Harp perceived him sneer in her direction and began to worry it had something to do with her chosen
mode of transportation? Both scenarios are believable and yet completely bizarre. Harp also
apologizes for calling Trump—doesn’t Trump famously love picking up the phone?—and not
returning texts promptly enough, though she shunts some of the blame for that one toward some guy
named John. “I want things to always be right between us,” Harp writes, sounding more like a
fictional voice-over from a young-adult novel than a real person.

In the next letter, Harp spends more time musing about her relationship with Trump. They have really
good conversations, she repeatedly asserts. Has anyone ever had a good conversation with Trump? If I
didn’t already suspect Harp of being severely delusional, that alone would be evidence enough. She
writes that she misses the days when she was just a “talkshow host,” when they used to talk
about “everything and nothing.” Uh, no wonder Melania Trump is apparently wary of this woman. At
the end of the letter, Harp chooses to quote Jane Austen, of all people: “I could not have parted
with you, to anyone less worthy,” she writes. In _Pride and Prejudice_, this is what the main
character’s father says about the man she eventually marries, Mr. Darcy, as in: You found a good
match. When Harp says it, she’s talking about her late father, and how he is probably proud that
she’s with Trump now, but, needless to say, she is not with Trump in the same way Elizabeth Bennet
was with Mr. Darcy. _Unless._ “And, I will add, it is I who is unworthy.” I am just quoting
that part to make you barf, whether from the grammar or the meaning or both.

Trump is known for being surrounded by lackeys and yes-men, but as he himself has pointed out, most
of them also have goals of their own underneath their boosterism. Even the ones who agree with him
for the most part politically seek to appease him, and want power and fortunes of their own
eventually. Harp is different, though. Her devotion to Trump is so pure, so guileless, so observably
unlike other humans’ that one can only reach for cartoon and fantasy characters to compare her to:
She’s like a Smithers from _The Simpsons_ meets Dobby from _Harry Potter_ meets LeFou from _Beauty
and the Beast_, all in the body of a young Fox News blonde. Does she have a crush on him, does she
want him to be her dad, what is it? Either way, it should be disqualifying for a job in the White
House, and those letters should be a smoking gun. For Trump, though? I wonder if they’re stuck to
the White House fridge with magnets or pinned to a bulletin board, maybe under the heading “Fan
Mail.”
