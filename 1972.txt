TV tonight: the British TV event of the year finally arrives!

====================================================================================================

THE CELEBRITY TRAITORS

8PM, BBC ONE
Whom will Michael Sheen pretend to be in his latest role? How long before James Blunt gets his
guitar out? Will Prof Hannah Fry prove to be too clever? And how well do Rob Beckett and Romesh
Ranganathan really know each other? Welcome to the TV event of the year! Last year's inaugural
starry series provided dozens of sublime small-screen moments and made the show the hottest
celebrity booking in the business. Can this year's maintain the standard? _Phil Harrison_

BREAK CLAUSE

10PM, CHANNEL 4
A comedy built around the housing crisis was only a matter of time. This series follows Lil and Ben
as their move to London goes pear‑shaped. In the time it takes Ben to confess to a drunken
one-night stand, the couple go from conversations about what colour bedding they'd prefer to
labelled sections in the fridge. Unfortunately, they've signed a six-month tenancy, so they're
stuck. It's amusing rather than uproarious, but Lara Ricote and Samuel Bottomley are sweetly
bewildered leads. _PH_

SAM & ADE GO BIRDING

7PM, CHANNEL 5

[A man with a dark green winter jacket and grey woolly hat and a man with a green hooded jacket and
pale blue cap both holding binoculars lean on a fence smiling]
This attempt to approximate the magic of Mortimer & Whitehouse: Gone Fishing returns. It is rather
lovely in its own right, even if the actors Sam West and Ade Edmondson lack Bob and Paul's comic
chemistry. They begin in the Yorkshire Dales, where Sam drags Ade out of bed at 3am for the dawn
chorus. Ouch. _PH_

LONG LOST FAMILY

9PM, ITV1
Two more tearjerking searches are shepherded by Davina McCall and Nicky Campbell. First comes Anita,
who found a photo of a baby in her mother's wardrobe and is finally searching for the brother who
was given up for adoption. Then, Ruth traces her late sister's son, who was taken into care. _Hannah
Verdier_

TASKMASTER

9PM, CHANNEL 4
This customarily spicy mixture of comics is dominated by Richard Ayoade and his disdain for the
whole enterprise. However, Chloe Petts also causes problems for Alex this week by hiding something
in a very awkward place indeed. Somehow, Taskmaster's formula is sturdy enough to accommodate
everyone it encounters. _PH_

ALL CREATURES GREAT AND SMALL

9PM, CHANNEL 5
Siegfried works the veterinary practice flat out as he tries to "seize the advantage" from his rival
Granville Bennett, while James's devotion to work leaves Dorothy feeling neglected. It's time to
make amends: "Dorothy … would you consider making things between us more permanent?" _Ali
Catterall_

FILM CHOICE

BETTER MAN (MICHAEL GRACEY, 2024), 8.30AM, 8PM, SKY CINEMA PREMIERE

[Close up of a CGI chimpanzee wearing a red cap]
Michael Gracey's film about Robbie Williams sticks to the music biopic template of
rise-fall-redemption, save for one aspect: he's portrayed as a chimp. Manifesting Take That's cheeky
chappy as an ape provides a pleasingly surreal edge to the singer's lairy tale – as well as giving
Williams, who narrates, the chance to be amusingly self-deprecating. A fun, vibrant and silly film,
with one set-piece, Rock DJ on Regent Street in London, that's the equal of La La Land's freeway
number._ Simon Wardell_

THE HOLDOVERS (ALEXANDER PAYNE, 2023), 9PM, FILM4
Alexander Payne gets back with his Sideways lead, Paul Giamatti, for a witty, bittersweet story. The
actor is on fine misanthropic form as Paul Hunham, a widely disliked classics teacher at a New
England boys' boarding school in 1970. He is ordered to stay on site over the Christmas holidays to
supervise the children who can't go home, most problematically Dominic Sessa's smart, sardonic
teenager Angus. Also stuck there is the grieving canteen manager, Mary (an Oscar-winning Da'Vine Joy
Randolph). Forced into uncomfortable proximity, the three slowly eke out an understanding. _S__W_
