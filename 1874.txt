The History of Sound to In the Grey: the seven best films to watch on TV this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
THE HISTORY OF SOUND

Feted young actors Paul Mescal and Josh O'Connor have teamed up for this melancholy romance about
music and missed connection. Mescal plays Lionel, a gifted musician who meets a more scholarly
academic, David (O'Connor), while studying at the New England Conservatory of Music, in the midst of
the first world war. Reunited after armistice, the two travel around rural Maine together, recording
their folk music on the new-fangled "wax cylinders" and – ultimately, fleetingly – falling in
love. Director Oliver Hermanus's previous work includes Living, the Bill Nighy-starring re-imagining
of Kurosawa's 50s classic Ikiru, and this is a similar symphony of longing and decade-spanning
desire. 
_Saturday 12 September, 7.50am, 2.50pm, Sky Cinema Premiere_

-------------------------

IN THE GREY

[Jake Gyllenhaal and Henry Cavill in In the Grey.]
There's a conspicuous absence of cockneys in this new action thriller – but otherwise it's your
typical Guy Ritchie joint, elevated by the presence of Jake Gyllenhaal as Bronco Beauregard, one
half of a glorified, globe-trotting debt-collecting duo with Henry Cavill as his more reticent other
half, Sid. Eiza González is on – effectively – Miss Moneypenny duties as Rachel Wild, a lawyer
who employs Bronco and Sid to do her dirty work (ie all the cool stunts), on account of their
absolute willingness to operate in the legal-moral grey zone of the title. 
_Out now, Prime Video_

-------------------------

THE RED SHOES

[Moira Shearer in The Red Shoes.]
If you've never seen Powell and Pressburger's 1948 meta-dance-thriller, you've never seen the colour
red. It's that serious. Loosely based on Hans Christian Andersen's gruesome fairytale, it revolves
round aspiring ballerina Victoria Page (a resplendent Moira Shearer) and her toxic relationship with
the world-renowned impresario Boris Lermontov (Anton Walbrook). Boris forces her to choose between
her art and her love for composer Julian Craster (Marius Goring). But the story is near-irrelevant
when the cinematography and choreography are this spectacular. 
_Saturday 12 September, 9.50am, BBC Two_

-------------------------

RABBIT TRAP

[Dev Patel in Rabbit Trap.]
Dev Patel stars in this creepy folk horror which combines ASMR atmospherics with the timeless
eeriness of Tylwyth Teg fairy-folk legend. It's 1976 and sound recordist Darcy Davenport (Patel) and
his wife, musician Daphne (Rosy McEwen), have relocated to an isolated Welsh cabin, seeking
inspiration through immersion in the sounds of nature. But the couple is soon accosted by a nameless
child of indeterminate age and gender, who insinuates themself into their home. 
_Sunday 13 September, 6.50am, 10pm, Sky Cinema Premiere_

-------------------------

ROBIN HOOD: PRINCE OF THIEVES

[Kevin Costner in Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves.]
This month, two entirely different films are out in cinemas, offering alternative takes on the
legendary, wealth-redistributing outlaw. But neither the mortality-contemplating Hugh Jackman one,
nor the mud-splattered Andrew Garfield one, can compete with this Kevin Costner-starring 90s
classic. Alan Rickman was a deserved Bafta-winner for his definitive take on the Sheriff of
Nottingham, but the real villain was that Bryan Adams theme song. 
_Monday 14 September, 5.35pm, Sky Cinema Greats_

-------------------------

GET CARTER

[Michael Caine in Get Carter.]
It's one of the all-time great gangster films, offering a peer down the side-streets and back alleys
of late-60s Newcastle, plus Michael Caine in his charismatic, menacing prime. He plays Jack Carter,
the titular hardman who returns to his hometown after years spent in London's underworld in order to
investigate, then avenge, his brother's death. Writer-director Mike Hodges cleverly incorporated
aspects of real-life local crimes into his script, especially the notorious "one-armed bandit
murder" of 1967, helping to usher in a gritty new era of realism for British crime drama. 
_Monday 14 September, 11.05pm, BBC Two_

-------------------------

IT SHOULD HAPPEN TO YOU

[Judy Holliday and Jack Lemmon in It Should Happen to You.]
The great Jack Lemmon had his first major film role in this George Cukor-directed story of a young
woman, Gladys Glover (Judy Holliday), who yearns to make her mark on the world by any means
necessary. Gladys kicks off a curious kind of career by hiring billboards around New York City to
display her name, while suitor Pete (Lemmon) attempts to persuade her to find contentment in the
crowd. It's sharp as a romantic comedy, sweet as a prescient satire on the perils of fame, and
altogether a delicious treat. 
_Wednesday 16 September, 11am, Film4_
