Trump Fell Asleep on Camera at an International Summit—Again

====================================================================================================

_This story is adapted from the Slatest newsletter. You can_ _sign up here_ _to get it in your inbox
every day._

When the leaders of powerful nations gather, they always give journalists plenty to dissect. But
it’s pretty hard to focus on diplomatic pageantry or geopolitical stakes when Donald Trump keeps
falling asleep on camera.

The setting for the president’s latest nap was the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, which
took place this week in Shenzhen, China. As Chinese leader Xi Jinping delivered a speech, video
captured Trump, 80, generally looking conked out, sitting with his eyelids heavy, shoulders hunched,
mouth askew, and head nodding. (On Thursday, the White House communications director heatedly denied
that Trump had been caught dozing, claiming that the footage actually showed him studying
documents.)

In the president’s defense, international travel can be exhausting at any age. Yet Trump has
repeatedly struggled to stay awake on this side of the globe too. An abbreviated list of places
where he seems to have nodded off in recent years includes the Oval Office, Cabinet meetings, a 9/11
memorial event, and his own New York criminal trial. There are other signs that our aging president
may not be all there: flubbing words, confusing names and places, repeatedly visiting the doctor,
and more.

• Video from Slate 

This should be concerning to the White House—and the rest of us. But too often, it isn’t even
remarked upon anymore. Write-ups of the summit in the New York Times, the Wall Street Journal,
Washington Post, Politico, and Axios make no mention of Trump’s snooze. A one-sentence CNN
live-blog entry does (“Trump closes his eyes during Xi speech”), but this particular faux-pazzz
apparently didn’t rate the network’s list of summit takeaways. So far, only a handful of U.S.
outlets (including HuffPost, the New Republic, and the Daily Beast) have dedicated stand-alone
articles to it.

That lack of public scrutiny is striking given the media’s relentless focus on the risks of an
aging president when that president was named Joe Biden. It’s true that medical privacy laws, the
vagaries of aging, and outright deception can make it hard to ascertain the truth about a
president’s health. And it’s not as if there’s been _zero_ coverage of Trump’s; the Times
and the Journal both published stories about it this year. Trump’s aging may also be getting less
airtime because there’s no shortage of other things about him to cover critically.

But to be blunt, those are poor excuses. Having an octogenarian president in questionable health is
a problem, or it’s not. Political journalists collectively embraced the former attitude during
Biden’s term, and their coverage of Trump’s fitness hasn’t been proportionate either to its
importance or to the scrutiny that was (and in some cases still is) trained on his predecessor.
Biden’s disastrous 2024 debate, which unfolded before the entire nation, uncorked an
understandable media stampede to get to the bottom of his decline.

Trump falling asleep in front of the entire world should probably do something similar.

WHAT SHOULD YOU READ, PLAY, WATCH, AND DRINK TO FIGHT SLEEP AT THE END OF A LONG DAY? MY COLLEAGUES
HAVE SOME SUGGESTIONS:

SINK YOUR TEETH INTO A JUICY MYSTERY: “San Marzano” tomatoes are prized as the very best for
pasta sauces, soups, and other dishes. But what if one of the most familiar brands selling these
precious fruits—Cento, of the iconic yellow cans—was potentially misleading consumers about
what’s in its tins? A recent lawsuit has accused the company of “tomato fraud.” When food
writer Danny Palumbo investigated, he discovered a tale full of spicy court documents, Italian
warehouse raids, a mysterious outfit that sidesteps Italy’s official San Marzano police, and more.

PLAY _GTA 6_ THE RIGHT WAY: Is it possible to cheat on your partner by playing the new _Grand Theft
Auto VI_? Apparently, some people think so. Slate sex advice columnist Rich Juzwiak weighs in on
whether it’s possible to “micro-cheat” via a video game.

SKIP THE SBF SHOW: Netflix’s new limited series _The Altruists_ dramatizes the rise and fall of
convicted grifter Sam Bankman-Fried and his infamous FTX cryptocurrency exchange. Slate tech writer
Nitish Pahwa reported on SBF’s trial, so he has a unique perspective on how the Netflix show
compares with the true story. His verdict? “This is terrible TV that’s also incorrect on the
merits.”

CRACK OPEN A COLD ONE: Pennsylvania-based Yuengling lager has inspired an obsessive following. For
fans who live outside the brewery’s distribution area, it has become a tradition to stock up on
cases of the brew and smuggle them back to their home states. But now, with Yuengling expanding its
reach, N.C. Stevens asks: Will this hard-to-get regional beer still be as satisfying without the
thrill of the chase?
