TV tonight: Nicola Coughlan and Joe Cole's powerful drama about a toxic relationship

====================================================================================================

I AM HELEN

9PM, CHANNEL 4 
Helen and Steve have been together for five years. They're generally happy, but something is
starting to feel wrong. Steve wants to get married and have children; Helen is still undecided. But
that's not all. Steve is bored by the sex they have, too. And he'd rather Helen didn't pursue her
career dreams. There's also a workmate who is whispering ideas into his ear. As Helen and Steve's
relationship unravels, so, too, does a compelling exploration of toxic masculinity and its poisoning
of relationships. This two-parter is the latest brilliant drama in Dominic Savage's Bafta-winning I
Am anthology, with leads Nicola Coughlan and Joe Cole putting in career-best performances. _Hollie
Richardson_

I'M A CELEBRITY … GET ME OUT OF HERE!

9PM, ITV1 
Wayne Rooney has landed in Australia to face the jungle – and all the kangaroo-testicle eating and
being-buried-alive-with-snakes that usually comes with it. Also taking part this year are the show's
first wheelchair user, Ade Adepitan, and YouTuber Sharky (nope, no idea). _HR_

THE GILDED AGE

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC 
Julian Fellowes' period drama continues to slip down nicely in its fourth outing. Where will the
presidential dinner take place? With a magazine article putting a spanner in the works for Agnes
(Christine Baranski), Bertha (Carrie Coon) plays her card: "Perhaps I can be of some assistance?"
_HR_

PEELERS: THE PSNI FOR REAL

10PM, BBC TWO

[Officers stand beside Stephen Nolan, arms crossed, in a car park]
A well-deserved second run of this intense documentary series, which consciously evokes the dynamics
and subject matter of Blue Lights. The cocaine trade is once again at the heart of the action but
it's full of fascinating asides as embedded reporter Stephen Nolan manages private moments of
insight with officers and suspects alike. _Phil Harrison_

SOCIAL WORKERS ON THE FRONTLINE

10PM, CHANNEL 4 
As this documentary shows, few professions are as important – or as challenging – as social
work. It was filmed over a year in Birmingham and follows child protection officers as they make
impossibly tough calls about the safety of the city's most vulnerable children. It's also a look at
the increasing financial pressures faced by the sector. _PH_

YOUNG, BRITISH AND TRADWIFE

10.40PM, BBC ONE 
Instagram has a lot to answer for. Via performative social media, parts of society are reverting to
medievalism. This documentary sees Poppy Jay (who walked away from an arranged marriage) meet a
selection of traditionally formulated families, from the harmless (Megan and Joseph, who met at a
Christian worship event) to the problematic ("Christian nationalist" Aaron, who argues that "bad men
flourish under feminism" and delegates every single domestic task to his wife, Molly). _PH_
