TV tonight: Bafta-winning documentary makers follow far-right protesters

====================================================================================================

ENGLAND 'TIL I DIE

10PM, CHANNEL 4
Here comes the year's most depressing hour of TV. This film follows Liam Tomsett from Crowborough,
East Sussex, who positioned himself as an anti-immigration leader when the government announced that
an army camp would be used to house asylum seekers in the town. The cameras follow him and far-right
protesters as they wade deeper into the movement. From the Bafta-winning team behind Ukraine: Enemy
in the Woods, and the pulled BBC documentary, Gaza: How to Survive a Warzone, it is shocking and
scorched with anger._ Hollie Richardson_

THE REPAIR SHOP: BEYOND THE BARN

8PM, BBC ONE
The spin-off that looks more closely at the stories of items after being restored. A vintage steamer
trunk owned by the late Paul O'Grady's husband, André, gets revived, along with a bike once used by
gas lamplighters and a wolf statue smashed by the family cat. _HR_

SURVIVING EARTH

8PM, SKY ONE

[The silhouette of a dinosaur is seen on the crest of a hill with a dramatic sunburst in the
distance]
This apocalypse wow series continues and we're headed to the late Carboniferous period. "Swamp
forests" have sprung up, oxygen levels are at their highest: great for dragonflies as big as crows
(yikes), not so good for the reptilian Dendromaia battling against a forest fire. _Priya Elan_

KIRSTIE AND PHIL'S LOVE IT OR LIST IT: BRILLIANT BUILDS

8PM, CHANNEL 4
Kirstie and Phil revisit another pair of success stories. Sarah and Sean's sprawling Victorian semi
in Whitley Bay needed a £70,000 rescue, while Steven and Adeola's chaotic Stevenage new-build had
been pushed to breaking point. With Kirstie fighting awkward layouts and Phil hunting for
alternatives, did the couples finally agree to stay put, patch up, or get out? _Ali Catterall_

GRAND DESIGNS

9PM, CHANNEL 4
She chutes, she scores! But is Bryony's fixation on a hi-tech laundry solution something of a coping
mechanism during her challenging self-build project? As series 27 kicks off, the stakes for her and
husband Matt's environmentally minded Cornish home are high, and there's plenty for Kevin McCloud to
raise an eyebrow at. _Hannah J Davies_

ANDREW DAVIES REMEMBERS … MIDDLEMARCH

10PM, BBC FOUR
Davies – the writer behind many a classic BBC period drama – looks back at his 1994 adaptation
of George Eliot's Middlemarch. Heated rows with the director is just one thing that made the
experience "like carrying three big suitcases across a railway platform". _HR_

FILM CHOICE

[Black and white photograph shows a young man in a suit and tie holding a cigarette, talking to a
young woman in a black and white dress]
IT SHOULD HAPPEN TO YOU (GEORGE CUKOR, 1954), 11AM, FILM4
The great Jack Lemmon had his first major film role in this George Cukor-directed story of a young
woman, Gladys Glover (Judy Holliday), who yearns to make her mark on the world by any means
necessary. Gladys kicks off a curious kind of career by hiring billboards around New York City to
display her name, while suitor Pete (Lemmon) attempts to persuade her to find contentment in the
crowd. It's sharp as a romantic comedy, sweet as a prescient satire on the perils of fame, and
altogether a delicious treat. _Ellen E Jones_
