

====================================================================================================

Ellie & Natasia review – please give us more of this irresistibly weird comedy

====================================================================================================

In 2022, comics and actors Ellie White and Natasia Demetriou starred in six bite-size episodes of
their self-titled sketch show. Among the highlights: a woman who makes wild swimming her whole
personality before realising she hates freezing water; a pair of overbearing, overopinionated mums
who vlog from a sterilely clean kitchen ("you're not a real mum unless you're redecorating or
knocking through at least six times a year") and, of course, Internet Nails, about two decidedly
useless eastern European beauty entrepreneurs who were branching out into a variety of business
ideas ("please let us scratch you with our ink today, and if you change your mind we can burn the
tattoo off with professional match"). It was often hilarious, and was definitely weird, with many
parallels to the offbeat Channel 4 series Stath Lets Flats (which White and Demetriou starred in
alongside the latter's brother, its creator Jamie).

Four long years later – reportedly due to scheduling clashes – the duo are back. Ellie & Natasia
series two comprises just three episodes, although – gladly – these episodes are now half an
hour long rather than a mere 15 minutes. Internet Nails, in particular, remains a crucible for
unsettling comedy, with the salon having become a "premiere relax styling and elegance lounge for
all makeup, hair straightening and spray tanning mother-baby needs"). The fact that both women were
pregnant at the time of filming only makes it funnier, and the low-rent experience ("100% birthing
penthouse with perfect lukewarm Jacuzzi") is deeply silly and – for anyone who has gone to a
slightly shonky beauty shop before – astutely observed. The judgy mums are back, too, complete
with advice on how to make your wardrobe stretch from "conception to birth, birth to night, night to
bed, bed to menopause, menopause to coffin, and coffin to hell …"

Influencers have become even more inescapable since White and Demetriou's first series, and the
parodies are plentiful. These include two women who speak gobbledegook into their podcast
microphones, while their hats get bigger and bigger with every scene ("don't just dress for the job
you want," White implores. "If you want the dress, job for it"). There's also a bit about a
sack-wearing, trad wife-style content creator (White) who enjoys eating croissants, making collages
and, er, keeping the woman her boyfriend cheated with locked up in her basement. So silly, so
irresistible.

[A woman in peach dress with short blond wig and a woman in black dress and fascinator look at an
interviewer holding a mic up to them.]
Of course, this is a sketch show, so not everything lands. The two women who spend hours in posh
shops fondling candles and cooing are relatable but the concept wears thin, even when Sian Clifford
pops up as a wonderfully exasperated shopkeeper. An OnlyFans parody that involves the pair eating
grapes and mashed potatoes out of their cleavages feels more like an in-joke. See also: Mary Poppins
at the gynaecologist, complete with what can only be described as dangerously cavernous nether
regions. Unfortunately, that four-year gap also means that Mitchell and Webb pipped them to the post
with a Repair Shop send-up in Mitchell and Webb Are Not Helping, even if White and Demetriou's
version is funnier, with a better payoff.

Luckily, there's much that does work – eccentric and sometimes touching vignettes of life that
don't punch down but do cock a snook at almost every area of society, from a "wee little shop
selling tiny mini wee things" in Scotland to Lime bike addicts to aspirants like Donna and her
cousin Nikki, who composes a rap about Dubai chocolate's pistachio filling. A Crystal Maze-style
gameshow about a man who needs to get rid of all the snakes that are ruining the foundations of his
house ("I sleep with a knife on a stick!") probably doesn't say much about anything, actually, but
it is totally hysterical. Ditto a Fonejacker-esque recurring bit where Demetriou pops up to declare
"I made it, it was me!" about everything from a pot plant to a high-rise building to a pen. A
special mention, too, for "Women's-Only Only Fools and Horses", a new musical stage adaptation of
the TV series (a sheepskin-clad Demetriou explains: "Strip it back and at its heart it's a very
serious story about three generations of working-class men …").

The series also manages to pack in a ridiculous surfeit of British comedy talents, from Jamie
Demetriou to Steve Coogan, Katy Wix to Asim Chaudhry, Kiell Smith-Bynoe to Vicki Pepperdine. But, of
course, its brightest lights are White and Demetriou. "Pregnant and doing a sketch show? Does that
make it funnier?" asks Demetriou in one of the transition scenes where the two women natter to each
other. Not necessarily, but I could easily watch another 10 episodes.
