Perhaps the hourlong lines to buy Criterion Collection Blu-rays should have been a dead giveaway,
but one of the surprising winners at the Toronto International Film Festival this year was physical
media. Like most festivals, TIFF’s projectors have gone fully digital, but onscreen, print
journalism in particular was alive and well.

Or at least alive. Danny Boyle’s _Ink_, which is loosely adapted from James Graham’s
Tony-winning 2017 play, charts Rupert Murdoch’s takeover of the Sun, which ravenously ambitious
editor Larry Lamb (Jack O’Connell) turned from a flagging broadsheet into a ruthless tabloid that
within a decade became the bestselling tabloid in the U.K., a title it held for 42 years. Naturally
there’s some handwringing about the deterioration of journalistic standards, culminating in the
disastrous 1969 kidnapping of Muriel McKay. (McKay was mistaken for Rupert Murdoch’s wife, and the
movie blames the Sun’s reckless reporting for her eventual murder.) But from _Trainspotting_
onward, Boyle’s morality plays have always done battle with his love of the vulgar and grotesque.
