George W. Bush’s 9/11 Paintings Have to Be Seen to Be Believed

====================================================================================================

When George W. Bush decided to take up painting later in life, he could have played it safe, with
puppies and children and landscapes. He’s done some of that, but he’s also consistently
surprised people—there were those nudes that were discovered in an email hack and, after that, the
provocative decision to paint portraits of world leaders like Vladimir Putin and Tony Blair. And now
Dubya the shock artist has done it again: Just in time for the 25th anniversary, his latest
paintings depict his experience of the 9/11 terrorist attacks and their aftermath.

Bush shared four of his paintings on Instagram on Friday, writing, “Twenty-five years ago, the
actions of an enemy revealed the spirit of the American people. These and other paintings are my
reflections of this solemn anniversary.” He added that the work will be on display this month at
his presidential center in Dallas.

The first work he shared was the most notable, because it’s a painted version of a
well-known-to-the-point-of-memedom photo of Bush on 9/11, the moment when his chief of staff, Andrew
Card, interrupted a visit with elementary school students to whisper in his ear that America was
under attack. Online, this is commonly rendered with a caption of dialogue that goes, “Sir, a
second plane has hit the Twin Towers.” In brushstrokes that are spare, sketchy, and childlike,
Bush manages to perfectly capture his countenance in the original photo—he’s gotten much better
at painting since those nudes. (I would contend that he’s got a stronger style and point of view
than another up-and-coming artist from the political realm, Hunter Biden.) Still, it makes sense to
question why a painting of such a somber moment is so colorful, almost whimsical. The other
paintings in the gallery are of smoke rising above the New York City skyline, a hand holding a
police badge, and a group of people standing on top of a building holding an American flag.

What exactly is Bush doing here, sharing his own interpretation of a meme of himself? Online, some
users called out the paintings as disrespectful and even ghoulish; one person noted that Bush is
coming perilously close to fulfilling a 2014 Onion headline: “George W. Bush Debuts New Paintings
of Dogs, Friends, Ghost of Iraqi Child That Follows Him Everywhere.” Bush doesn’t deserve to get
to have feelings about a moment he fumbled so badly, this line of argument goes, nor should he get
the chance to rehabilitate his image given how he responded. At worst, the paintings make it seem as
if Bush is treating a profound national crisis as his own personal trauma, as if all the people who
died in the war on terrorism matter less to him than how embarrassing it is that he has to deal with
being a meme.

But I’m not sure Bush is self-aware enough to know that he’s a meme, that a lot of people
consider him a war criminal (he’d get some of this if he reads his comments, though, to be fair),
or that his ugly Putin painting could have had real geopolitical consequences. He’s just a dumb
guy who likes to create juvenile-looking paintings. It’s hard to hate on that. Yes, he could and
should be doing work in his retirement to reckon with and repent for his sins, save the Republican
Party, and make the world a better place, but he’s also not hurting anyone by farting around with
some oil paints.

Bush’s painting hobby doesn’t change any of the harm he did in his presidency, nor does it
rehabilitate anything, but the one little thing it does do is make him look comparatively human next
to our current president. It’s impossible to imagine Donald Trump taking up such a peaceful,
unremunerative hobby, just as it’s impossible to imagine him genuinely appreciating art for its
own sake, much less attempting his own. When he leaves office, Trump will not paint, or start doing
morning pages à la _The Artist’s Way_, or champion an important cause like slowing global
warming, and we’ll all be longing for the days of an ex-president who kept to himself and his
paintbrushes. If Bush’s weird little simpleton paintings annoy you, so be it, but I say let him
express himself—and embarrass himself—to his heart’s content.
