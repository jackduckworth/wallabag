1.

DONALD TRUMP

The recently resumed hostilities with Iran are not going well. Four U.S. service members were killed
this week, bringing the total to 18 since the war began. (Did you hear that, Department of Defense?
It’s 18.) The U.S. has bombed Iranian targets for the last two weeks to no apparent change in
Iran’s negotiating posture. The Houthis have saddled up to threaten shipping through the Red Sea,
while the Strait of Hormuz remains a mess. Oil prices have shot back up since the end of the
ceasefire, which affects a lot more than the price of gasoline. MAGA voters continue to sour on the
conflict. The war is also expensive.

All of this is making Trump incredibly mad, since there’s no clear way to get out of it absent the
deployment of ground troops. As one source told Politico this week, “The president is now fully
aware that the only way to the victory that he wants is completely politically impossible. The
American people just will not support the kind of escalation it would take at this point.” We
don’t mean to neener-neener the president at a delicate and dangerous moment. But this _is_ yet
another time for a reminder that if you’re thinking about starting a war, it’s wise to get
buy-in from important stakeholders such as the U.S. Congress, the American people, or any of your
allies. Anyone, really.

3.

TROY JACKSON

The Maine Democratic Party will hold its convention to nominate a replacement for Graham Platner in
the Senate race this weekend. When the convention was first announced, it sounded as if it might be
a thrilling opportunity to see real wheeling and dealing on a convention floor from which a nominee
would eventually emerge. Nope! Most of the delegates elected to the convention last weekend support
former state Senate President Troy Jackson, and all of his meaningful rivals quickly dropped out.

Jackson, who lost the Democratic primary for governor earlier this year, is a logger from far
northern Aroostook County whose views turned more progressive as time went on. In the build-up to
the convention, though, the Washington Post reported on some stories from Maine that had been
bubbling up on social media about his temper and shouting matches with fellow lawmakers. Most
notably, he “once angrily threw a water bottle in a meeting after he couldn’t persuade a
lawmaker to vote his way.” We don’t condone bottle-throwing in meetings. It’s certainly rude.
We also don’t really—what’s the word?—_care_. No more nominee takebacks, Maine Democrats.
This is your guy.

4.

JOHN THUNE

House Republicans this week passed the framework for a third reconciliation bill—which bypasses
the 60-vote Senate filibuster rule—of Donald Trump’s presidency. This framework, once fleshed
out, would offer up to $73 billion for the military and intelligence services to cover the Iran
war, $12 billion to bail out farmers screwed over by Trump’s trade policies, and $10 billion in
incentives to states to offer some of the election reforms from the SAVE America Act, the GOP’s
flagship voting bill that remains mega-dead in the Senate.

Well, this reconciliation frameworkis likely mega-dead in the Senate too. There are not at this
moment 50 Republican senators who want to vote on big bucks for the dreadfully unpopular Iran war or
elements of the SAVE Act, and Republicans certainlydon’t want to vote on unlimited Democratic
amendments in a campaign season. Senate Majority Leader John Thune isn’t concealing his lack of
interest in what the House is offering, saying that he wants to focus on government funding instead.
In a White House press briefing on Thursday, press secretary Karoline Leavitt said that Trump’s
“patience is running out” with the Senate. In response to this, Thune told reporters, “Maybe
she or somebody else ought to get on the phone and get the votes.” Everything’s going great.

5.

DARLINE GRAHAM

Move over, Maine Democrats: It’s South Carolina’s turn for a quickie Senate primary that
everyone jumps into. Candidate filing opened this week for the Republican primary to replace
deceased Sen. Lindsey Graham on November’s ballot. The primary will be held Aug. 11 with a
potential runoff on Aug. 25. It took many by surprise, then, when Lindsey Graham’s sister,
Darline Graham, decided this week to run for a full term, even though she was considered to be a
caretaker for the seat until the end of the year. She has Trump’s endorsement.

But no one really knows anything about Darline Graham, so a few others are deciding to give it a
shot. Rep. Russell Fry has entered the race, and why not? He’s allowed to keep running in his
House reelection campaign concurrently, so it’s a free roll at a Senate seat. Rep. Ralph Norman
did his best impression of a Maine Democrat by entering the race after losing his gubernatorial
primary earlier in the year, and the candidate that Lindsey Graham defeated in his primary earlier
this year, businessman Mark Lynch, has also joined.

But—God bless—our favorite entrant into the race is Mark Sanford, the former governor whose
political trajectory collapsed when he was busted visiting Argentina to see his mistress. Since that
scandal, he has since served in his old House seat again, gotten primaried out of it for not
appreciating Donald Trump enough, and run for president for a few months in the 2020 cycle, among
other reported activities. Hopefully the primary is venomous. We could all use a little pick-me-up.

6.

ZOHRAN MAMDANI

The New York City mayor promised during his 2025 campaign that he would order the New York Police
Department to arrest Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu if he stepped foot in the city. With
Netanyahu’s annual trip to the United Nations General Assembly in September looming, it was time
for the mayor to decide whether he would actually follow through with this. In a video he released
this week, he announced he would not. “My administration has reviewed every avenue available,
under applicable law, to determine whether New York City could execute the International Criminal
Court’s arrest warrant if Benjamin Netanyahu came here,” he said. “It is clear that we do not
have the independent legal authority to enforce this warrant.”

No kidding. May we ask: Did anyone really believe he would follow through on this bit of
aspirational rhetoric? Did anyone genuinely think that he would send a couple of cops from the night
beat to arrest a foreign head of state visiting the U.N., under a warrant from an international body
of justice that the United States doesn’t recognize? It was about as fantastical as believing that
Trump would have Mexico pay to build the U.S. border wall. There’s no reason for Mamdani’s
supporters to be disappointed, because it was never a serious idea to begin with.

7.

LAURA LOOMER

The conspiratorial MAGA influencer (and thus close confidante of Trump) has changed. Like most of
her ilk, she was exceptionally critical of Ukraine and President Volodymyr Zelensky for years during
the war, saying the country was full of “Nazi apologists” and calling Zelensky a “jihadi
apologist.” This week, however, she visited Ukraine, interviewed Zelensky, and changed her tune on
the conflict. She is now staunchly pro-Ukraine and observed, “I feel like such an asshole for
minimizing the struggle of Ukrainians for the last 5 years,” explaining, “We have been so
propagandized by Russia and we don’t even realize it.” (A lot of people realized that the
right-wing influencers had been propagandized by Russia.)

This is all well and good. Hopefully it makes some difference in right-wing politics and, more
importantly, Trump’s view of the conflict. But—now it’s our turn to play conspiracy
theorist—it’s also terribly difficult to take her conversion at face value. How does someone
who’s built her substantial MAGA following and income in part by checking the “Hate Ukraine”
box suddenly get on a trip to Kyiv to interview all of its powerful people and announce that she had
been wrong all along? We’re talking about a complete 180. Loomer, for what it’s worth, has
denied that she was paid by the Ukrainian government for the trip.
