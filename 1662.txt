TV tonight: Simon Pegg's cyber-thriller The Undeclared War returns

====================================================================================================

THE UNDECLARED WAR

9PM, CHANNEL 4
Espionage, malware and Simon Pegg doing his best serious face feature as Peter Kosminsky's
cyber-thriller returns for a second season. When we left him, GCHQ director of operations Danny
Patrick (Pegg) had discovered shocking secrets about both the internal workings of government and
his own marriage. Perhaps inevitably, things are now even worse. Saara (Hannah Khalique-Brown) has
observed irregularities in flows of information that can only mean one thing: a mole in the
intelligence agency. Plenty of scowling and running down empty corridors ensues, with Adrian Lester
back as the prime minister and Siân Brooke joining as Danny's wife, a former MI6 member. _Phil
Harrison_

PRISON WIVES OF TIKTOK

9PM, U&W

[Two women in wedding dresses and veils, laughing]
Women being able to broadcast the details of their romances with men behind bars on social media is
a recent development. This series follows five British women, explores their online community and,
inevitably, assesses their brand-building, money-making power. Four of the five are in relationships
with prisoners in the US so there's also an uneasy road trip. _Phil Harrison_

THE GREAT BRITISH SEWING BEE

8PM, BBC ONE
Sophie Willan hosts the crafty competition, which continues with a week dedicated to colour and
prints. The briefs for each round showcase a range of tastes and talents, but that doesn't mean it's
a cinch. "This is absolutely hideous," says one contestant, inspecting their own handiwork. _Hannah
J Davies_

MURDER 24/7

9PM, BBC ONE
Another series of this raw and grimly intimate show that takes viewers into the inner workings of
real murder cases. This time, Jo is found dead with a knife wound and her husband, Mohamed, is
arrested. But with no fingerprints on the presumed weapon and blood patterns not indicating a
struggle, something doesn't add up. _PH_

FAITHLESS

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC
The series concludes with a time-spanning double bill. In the late 1970s, actor Marianne (Frida
Gustavsson) is having a torrid affair with director David (Gustav Lindh), which reignites when they
work together at her theatre. Their increasingly reckless shenanigans reach fever pitch at a
birthday party. By 1980, Marianne is divorcing her husband when a tense night-time meeting ends in
tragedy. _Ali Catterall_

JOHNNY VEGAS' LITTLE SHOP OF ANTIQUES

9PM, QUEST
Season two of a pleasantly lackadaisical reality show, and Vegas – plus his best mate Bev and
girlfriend Vikki – has a new antiques shop to fit out, an electric van and dreams of taking the
business online. Meanwhile, the endless need for new stock prompts a trip to Anglesey, and another
haggling match with fearsome veteran Trish. _Jack Seale_

FILM CHOICE

THE PILLOW BOOK (PETER GREENAWAY, 1996), 1.10am, Film4
Peter Greenaway found perfect source material for his perennial obsessions with art, sex and death
(with a side order of lists) in the titular notebook penned by Sei Shōnagon, a lady in the imperial
court of late-900s Japan. In this exquisitely stylish drama, Sei's present-day counterpart is model
Nagiko (Vivian Wu), who gets sexual gratification from calligraphers writing on her body. Then she
starts a torrid affair with British translator Jerome (Ewan McGregor) and finds a very literary way
to take revenge on the publisher who destroyed her father. _Simon Wardell_
