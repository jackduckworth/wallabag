TV tonight: the unmarried women who were forced to give up their babies

====================================================================================================

STOLEN BABIES

9PM, BBC TWO
Ann in Wales was sent away by her parents when she became pregnant after being raped ("I didn't
blame them, I blamed myself"). Joan in Sunderland gave evidence in court against the teacher who
raped and got her pregnant – but he was acquitted. They tell their stories in this blood-boiling
documentary, along with other women who were unmarried, pregnant and sent to so-called "mother and
baby homes" between 1949 and 1976. People who worked in those places also speak, as do some of the
grown-up children who were adopted by mostly white, middle-class families. It brings their fight up
to date as they demand an apology from the state. Remarkably, there are some joyful moments to hang
on for. _Hollie Richardson_

9/11: DAYS THAT SHOCKED THE WORLD

10pm, Channel 4 
It is the 25th anniversary of one of this century's defining geopolitical events and this two-part
documentary offers a straightforward but still striking commemoration of that terrible day. It hears
from rescue workers, witnesses and survivors of the attack on New York but also explores the assault
on the Pentagon and the fate of the fourth airliner, which crashed into a field in Pennsylvania. The
horror still startles but so does the courage of many of the initial responses. _Phil Harrison_

SURVIVING EARTH

8pm, Sky One 
Two mating dinosaurs in Antarctica take the spotlight in this week's episode. How long before their
time runs out and they feel the ripple effects of the seven-and-a-half-mile-long asteroid that has
just landed in Mexico? Your enjoyment will very much depend on how much of the uncanny CGI you can
stomach. _Priya Elan_

BOOKISH

8PM, U&ALIBI

[Ruth Codd as India Flynn in Bookish.]
A suspicious death at a tailor's emporium is the latest two-part whodunnit for Mark Gatiss's tweedy
postwar sleuth Gabriel Book. But why is the 75-year-old proprietor so keen to confess to murder?
Meanwhile, Book's rascally apprentice Jack (Connor Finch) just happens to have acquired a snazzy new
suit. _Graeme Virtue_

PROFESSOR T

9PM, ITV1
After school inspector Tyrone Walsh is found murdered following a spate of burglaries, Professor T
and co explore the possible motives: was Walsh's "cultural bias against alternative education" a
factor in his murder? Meanwhile, Miss Snares (Sarah Woodward) investigates whether or not Dean is
Jasper's (Ben Miller) biological father. _Ali Catterall_

READING FESTIVAL HIGHLIGHTS 2026

10.40PM, BBC ONE
Beholdthe edited highlights of a star-studded and presumably quite messy weekend of music in a field
just outside Reading. This year, the festival sees performances from Chase & Status, Dave, Florence
+ the Machine, Raye and, both touting excellent new material, Fontaines DC and Charli xcx. _PH_

FILM CHOICE

[Dustin Hoffman in Little Big Man.]
LITTLE BIG MAN (ARTHUR PENN, 1970), 11.45am, 5Action 
Arthur Penn's revisionist western about the "sole white survivor of the Battle of the Little
Bighorn" may have Dustin Hoffman as its hero, but it is the Native Americans he meets, befriends and
lives with who are at the film's warm, melancholy heart. Hoffman's 121-year-old Jack Crabb relates
his picaresque history, from being orphaned after a Pawnee attack and raised by Cheyenne tribal
leader Old Lodge Skins (a deadpan Chief Dan George) to surviving in a "civilised" white world of
adulterers, snake-oil salesmen and, most horrifically, genocidal soldiers led by the likes of Gen
Custer. _Simon Wardell_
