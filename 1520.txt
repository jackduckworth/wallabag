TV tonight: the people fighting to clear their loved ones' names

====================================================================================================

THE ACCUSED: BEYOND REASONABLE DOUBT?

10PM, CHANNEL 4
"People are sitting in prison cells with life sentences when there is evidence that proves
categorically they didn't do it. And they call that justice." This new series speaks to people
fighting convictions they believe are miscarriages of justice. The first divisive case is that of a
street fight in 2005, when Jason Moore was found guilty of murdering Robert Darby. But Moore's
family and friends are campaigning for him to be freed. _Hollie Richardson_

GEORGE CLARKE'S BEAUTIFUL BUILDS

8PM, CHANNEL 4
Michelle in Windsor has lovingly called her bungalow "the hovel" for nearly a decade. With no
renovation experience, she now wants property developer George Clarke to step in and turn it into a
home she actually likes. From an "uncomfortably placed loo" to a "horrific plastic lean-to", he has
his work cut out. _HR_

CORNWALL: A YEAR BY THE SEA

8PM, CHANNEL 5

[A scenic view of the village of Port Issac in north Cornwall]
Life's not all ice-cream and pasties in this light documentary's latest visit to Cornwall.
Picturesque as Port Isaac is, it's on the north coast, which means challenging conditions for the
RNLI on a lifeboat training exercise. Down in the more sheltered south, however, the Tregothnan
estate continues to grow its own tea. _Jack Seale_

QUEEN JAMES: PASSION, PLOTS AND POWER IN THE COURT OF BRITAIN'S FIRST KING

9PM, BBC TWO
HistorianGareth Russell tells the intriguing story of King James I. A quietly influential figure,
James was a pacifist, a lover of the arts (he was a patron of Shakespeare) and a religious reformer.
However, he was also a controversial figure in his private life. James's letters and speeches are
brought to life by actor James McArdle. _Phil Harrison_

THE HARDACRES

9PM, CHANNEL 5
"I've come to collect a debt." She might be tiny, but the sinister Lady Imelda Hansen knows how to
make an entrance in a big way in this period drama. As Mary says about Ma's risking the house on a
game of bridge: "My mother has shown an appalling lack of judgment." Meanwhile, rebellious Emma
shows her own hand. _Ali Catterall_

SWIPE RIGHT FOR SPERM

10PM, BBC TWO
A horrifying investigation into unregulated "Tinder for sperm" websites which people who are unable
to access fertility treatments are paying to use. As well as speaking to a couple who used one, a
BBC Wales reporter pays £100 for a sample from a man who claims to have 180 children around the
world – and sends it the next day in a frozen food carton. _HR_
