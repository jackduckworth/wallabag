TV tonight: Dispatches asks Tommy Robinson awkward finance questions

====================================================================================================

DISPATCHES: TOMMY ROBINSON – WHERE'S THE MONEY GONE?

10PM, CHANNEL 4
Does his mum still call him Stephen? One of many awkward questions that could be asked about the
football hooligan turned far-right rabble-rouser Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, AKA Tommy Robinson. And
reporter Matt Shea is asking a few of them in this investigation, after spending a year analysing
public records and documents connected to Yaxley-Lennon's prolific yet somewhat shadowy fundraising.
_Phil Harrison_

KIRSTIE AND PHIL'S LOVE IT OR LIST IT

8PM, CHANNEL 4
Is it better to be open-plan or walled up, ask our hosts as we revisit two couples from 2023. Aneri
and Nihar were divided on their Hertfordshire house: was it isolated or did it have the perfect,
ahem, location location location? Meanwhile, Chester's Carl and Katy wanted to upgrade to a more
modern family abode. _Priya Elan_

BOOKISH

8PM, U&ALIBI

[A man looks at the camera, wearing post-second world war smart clothing including scarf and
wide-brimmed hat]
Miranda Richardson guest stars as former Nazi and notorious book thief Duchess Alberta in the
post-second world war detective drama this week. Book (Mark Gatiss) travels across Germany to the
curious medieval town of Würl to catch her – when a body is found in the creepy Schloss Von Bode
castle. _Hollie Richardson_

BRITAIN'S FORGOTTEN GIRLS

9PM, BBC TWO
The issue of grooming gangs has become a cynically exploited political football. This horrifying
documentary allows many of the girls involved to tell their stories. It's clear that there is no
cultural specificity to either victims or perpetrators – it's a problem related to other forms of
criminality, and to a poisonous version of masculinity. _PH_

PROFESSOR T

9PM, ITV1
An actor's nightmare plays out live on stage, when a prop knife is switched for a real one during a
performance. A tragic accident or something more sinister? DCI Maiya, DS Dan and Professor T
investigate a theatre company riven with rivalries and grudges. Meanwhile, Zelda Radclyffe's tarot
cards hint at a "highly significant" romance in Jasper's future. _Ali Catterall_

SPENCER BANKS REMEMBERS: PENDA'S FEN

10PM, BBC FOUR
David Rudkin and Alan Clarke's 1974 Play for Today, Penda's Fen, is an astonishing piece of work –
a coming-out and coming-of-age story but couched in Deep England mythology and folk horror tropes.
It gets a rare showing here but, first, lead actor Spencer Banks reflects on a career-defining role.
_PH_
