TV tonight: Ella Purnell returns as serial killer Sweetpea | Television | The Guardian

====================================================================================================

SWEETPEA

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC 
Things are looking up for Ella Purnell's casually murderous Rhiannon Lewis as the dark comedy
returns. Junior cop Marina has been warned to stay off her trail. And new office arrival Freya (Taj
Atwal) seems much more sympathetic to her ideas. Now, there's just the awkward problem of a body to
dispose of. Purnell is just the right mix of sullen and vulnerable but Sweetpea struggles to find
its tone: a drama about a serial killer should be more dynamic than this. _Phil Harrison_

ALDI: THE NEXT BIG SELLER

8PM, CHANNEL 4 
Buffaloumi, anyone? That's halloumi-style grilling cheese made from buffalo milk in Scotland – and
one independent product being pitched to the judges for the dinner aisle this week. Sri Lankan spice
mix, katsu chicken sausages and frozen food for toddlers are also on the menu. _Hollie Richardson_

SHETLAND

9PM, BBC ONE 
After last season's lacklustre whodunnit, series 11 feels as if Shetland is back on form. After the
historic death of Dr Linden, there's a shedload of deeply buried secrets and family tensions to
uncover, with cousins Peter and Emily having an especially knotty confrontation and another
character revealing a grudge (and motive?) against the good doctor. _Priya Elan_

WHY SINKHOLES COLLAPSE

9PM, BBC TWO 
The footage in this intriguing documentary is terrifying: cars, houses and power lines disappearing
into the suddenly gaping ground. But how do sinkholes happen? This documentary digs around and comes
up with answers involving underground streams and soluble rocks. However, while sinkholes are easy
to explain, they're hard to predict. _PH_

CUNK ON CINEMA

10PM, BBC TWO 
Cunk's big-screen odyssey concludes and she's worried about the demise of the blockbuster ("There
were two near me. Now, one sells vapes and the other's a Turkish barber"). But can human creativity
keep AI at bay? Philomena hasn't a clue so she recruits (fictional) Silicon Valley CEO Hal
Jutterhole to talk tech to her. _PH_

THE GOOD DAUGHTER

10PM, SKY ATLANTIC

[Two women – one with long mousy hair, another with dark hair, sitting at a table rummaging
through photographs]

This starry if ludicrously trauma-packed drama about a tragedy-riddled family of lawyers concludes
with a double bill. Meghann Fahy as Charlie finally tells Rose Byrne's Sam what actually happened in
the woods, and Sam discovers some shocking evidence while searching for a photo. Expect it to play
out with aplomb. _Alexi Duggins_

FILM CHOICE

THE SLIGHTEST TOUCH (RACHEL FLEIT, 2026), HBO Max 
Emma Fogarty is 40 and lives with epidermolysis bullosa, a rare inherited disorder that makes her
skin incredibly fragile, prone to tears and blisters. She's also good mates with the film star Colin
Farrell. Rachel Fleit's heart-shredding documentary follows their preparations for the 2024 Dublin
marathon, which he plans to run before pushing her in a wheelchair for the final 4km. Fogarty's
resilience in the face of near-constant pain is inspirational but hard to watch, while Farrell
reluctantly allows cameras into his private life, physically squirming at times as he opens up about
his addictions, sons – one of whom also has a genetic condition – and his beautiful friendship
with Fogarty. _Simon Wardell_

CABARET (BOB FOSSE, 1972), 11PM, BBC TWO

[A woman wearing a black headdress and black top leaning towards the foot of a bed with a lampshade
behind her]

Willkommen, bienvenue, welcome to possibly the greatest of all movie musicals. Bob Fosse's
adaptation of the Kander and Ebb stage hit adds now-classic songs (Maybe This Time, Money) alongside
his distinctively slinky choreography, while Liza Minnelli's Sally Bowles, the headline singer at a
club in 1931 Berlin, is an American (not British) force of nature. Michael York co-stars as her love
interest, English writer Brian, but he plays second fiddle to the Oscar-winning Minnelli, who owns
every song and brings pathos to her tragic character – a creative soul about to be crushed by
fascism. _SW_
