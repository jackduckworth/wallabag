More Man Utd flaws to the fore but Carrick relief comes as Martinez calls out Wilcox

====================================================================================================

It sums up Manchester United’s predicament that a Champions League defeat which displayed
deficiencies at both ends can be held up as an improvement.

Here, though, is where Michael Carrick finds himself. He will return from Atletico Madrid on the
back of a 2-0 loss no safer in his job – but in no more peril. And misery may now have some
company.

In Madrid, at least Carrick could point to solutions found to fix A MIDFIELD THAT WAS RAVAGED BY
WINLESS TOTTENHAM HOTSPUR on Saturday.

But, on this occasion, Carrick was let down by sloppiness at the back and, more than anything, an
abject lack of potency up front.

It took less than a minute for Atletico to rip through a defence starting together for the first
time as a back four.

Actually, the hosts tore through the entire United team in the build up to their opener which crept
over Senne Lammens’ line with 53 seconds on the clock.

Having copped so much criticism for the lack of intent in their press against Spurs, the United
players at least feigned some eagerness, waiting on the front foot as Cristian Romero stood on the
ball by his own box.

Two passes through the middle of the pack, though, took out 10 blue shirts. Perhaps the ease of it
confused Diogo Dalot – it doesn’t tend to take much – since he was caught looking the wrong
way as Alex Baena scampered in behind him. Baena’s pass across found Pablo Barrios, whose effort
was half-blocked by Ayden Heaven but, typical of Carrick’s luck, the ball eked over the line.

United were unfortunate earlier in the move. Romero’s pass was semi-intercepted by Carlos Baleba
but Atletico pounced on the loose ball. A single inadvertent touch, though, was all it took for
Carrick’s press to cave in. Atletico’s incisiveness made sure they could never recover from
there.

At least they regained some composure and United deserve some credit for that. Given their run and
the flak being flung at them, they were half-expected to fold. Instead, the Red Devils should have
flipped the scoreline inside a quarter of an hour.

Bryan Mbeumo wasted one glorious chance shortly before Marcus Rashford air-kicked another. There was
even less conviction in Mbeumo’s second attempt – also saved by Jan Oblak – before Baleba’s
weak header from the corner that followed was easily fielded by the Atletico keeper.

That lack of ruthlessness was the theme of the night for United. At least before Lisandro Martinez
unloaded in the immediate aftermath.

Martinez, of course, was right to call out, well, almost everyone. His words were pointed more
towards the boardroom and the layer between the manager and owners, which is where Jason Wilcox
currently cowers.

He, not Carrick, should take the blame for United being so blunt in attack. Of course they needed to
rebuild their midfield in the summer, but how can an organisation with United’s resources only do
one job at once?

United seem weirdly proud of conducting their recruitment while wearing blinkers, as if there is
something pioneering about it. Of course, there isn’t because just down the road, Liverpool seemed
to take a similar approach, which has f***ed them too.

Carrick needed midfielders, a striker and defenders – in that order. Failing to buy a forward not
only to cover Benjamin Sesko but to push him too, was a dereliction of duty. Of course, it
wouldn’t be Wilcox to pay the price, or even face the scrutiny.

Sure, those to whom the chances fell should have finished them off, but United only ever seem to go
through on goal in hope rather than expectation. Bruno Fernandes is usually the most ruthless but
even he was off target in the second half. Which might be attributed to the injury he is clearly
carrying.

United’s chances dried up when Leny Yoro was sent off for a brush with Jonathan David as last man
with quarter of an hour to go. Carrick described it as a ‘poor decision’ and, typical of the
bloke, he was being polite. Because it was a dreadful call.

Again, typical of the manager’s luck, Atletico’s second came as Harry Maguire and Matheus Cunha
waited to come on to prompt a change in United’s shape. If they didn’t look confident of scoring
one, two was always beyond them.

The biggest favour performed for Carrick by his players came after defeat from Martinez at the mic.
The spotlight on the manager won’t fade – Martinez pinpointed a lack of evolution in United’s
play –  but maybe now the focus will widen where it needs to.
