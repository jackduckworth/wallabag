The South Just Rose Again. And White Racist Republicans Aren’t Part Of It.

====================================================================================================

If watching the returns in the South on Tuesday night felt like watching Hungary eject Viktor
Orbán, then congratulations, you’re putting this in the correct geopolitical context.

You see, as Americans, we have been far too used to thinking of authoritarian takeovers as something
that happened in other countries (often with US State Department and CIA help _cough cough_),
something that was visited upon our shores _for the very first time_ when Donald Trump rode down
that golden escalator in 2015.

Meanwhile, lots of Black people in the South would be happy to explain that _actually_, they’ve
been living in authoritarian states their whole lives. States with names like Georgia and South
Carolina and Mississippi and Tennessee. States that, if the United States were not allegedly united,
would rank somewhere between Saudi Arabia and Belarus in The Economist’s Democracy Index.

That’s by design. Since the end of slavery, these states and their white supremacist leaders have
been laboring to establish a system where the only voices that really count are those of their
fellow white supremacist Christian men, and those men’s wives, as long as they agree to vote how
Daddy says and not to shout too loud for any rights of their own. (Conservative white women love it,
because they are also rank bigots.)

They gerrymander and slice and dice and criminalize and take away and intimidate and then when the
Supreme Court gives them permission, they gerrymander and slice and dice and criminalize and take
away and intimidate some more.

But sometimes they go way too far and something just snaps. Nobody knows the day or the hour, but
when it happens, it blows your fuckin’ mind.

There had been talk before these midterms about how Black folks, especially in the South, understood
the assignment, how they were _especially_ furious after the Supreme Court gutted what was left of
the Voting Rights Act, how something historic and earthshaking and electoral map-changing was going
to happen last night.

Well, it happened. Welcome to the New South.

Let’s see about some highlights:

Georgia and Texas _remade_ themselves, not out of new parts, but with the people who have been the
backbone of those states the whole time loudly shouting, “Never again, antebellum
motherfuckers!”

James Talarico beat Ken Paxton in Texas by 12 points. Gina Hinojosa, the state’s first Latina
governor, beat Greg Abbott (considerably more popular than Paxton, who’s hated by errrrrebody) by
a healthy seven. Creep Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick was ousted by a woman named Vikki Goodwin, and by six
points. Texas’s new attorney general will be Nathan Johnson, a Democrat. And just all the way down
the ballot. The Republicans’ racist gerrymanders backfired, and it looks like Democrats are going
to flip the state House.

And then there’s Georgia. Jon Ossoff was re-elected in an 18-point landslide against a pathetic,
tiny man Ossoff never forgot to remind us was a sad, craven bigot. And that bigot must be feeling
quite alone today, because have you heard about how the state is now just about entirely run by
Black women?

North Carolina _asserted itself_. The Republicans’ racist gerrymanders there completely fucked
them, and the states’ congressional delegation will now have a healthy blue majority.
Senator-Elect Roy Cooper, the Democrat, won by almost 20 points.

South Carolina and Mississippi got in line right behind Georgia and North Carolina and said, “Oh
hey y’all! We got next.” The new senator from South Carolina will be Annie Andrews, the
Democrat, and it wasn’t even that close. As we speak, Mississippi’s Senate race remains too
close to call, with Democrat Scott Colom in the lead by 65 votes over incumbent Republican Sen.
Cindy Hyde-Smith. (Or more: late-breaking, Decision Desk appears to have a new batch of votes
showing his lead expanding to over 1,800. As always, double-check for up-to-the-minute numbers.)

_Mississippi_. The state with the most brutal voter suppression laws in the country, and also a
Black population of approximately 38 percent. (These two things are related.)

As a lifelong southerner and fairly decent understander of the South, we have said many times, for
at least a decade, that the next two dogs to bark in the South, so to speak, would be Mississippi
and South Carolina. You can probably find us saying it in print somewhere. We have _especially_ said
it about Mississippi, and people have looked at us like we are crazy.

Now you know. (And if you want to know more, you need to learn about the “Black Belt” in the
American South.)

And don’t be quick to say, “Well, but Darline Graham was a particularly useless hunk of cat
mucus” and try to call it a fluke. That’s true, that’s what she is, and it’s sad how Lindsey
Graham died and then his dumb sister fell on her face and we all had to laugh at it, but it has to
start somewhere. People have to see that the confederate white supremacist slaveowner-descendant
stranglehold on their states can be broken. They have to _feel_ it. It has to be felt from within,
and also understood from without, by the funders who decide where it’s worth investing.

The work is just beginning in South Carolina and Mississippi, but now y’all know, and you can’t
fuckin’ pretend you don’t. One day, and it won’t be that long, they’ll be having their
nights like Georgia, North Carolina, and Texas just did.

And since we were right about South Carolina and Mississippi, hear us now when we say that the next
two are Alabama and Louisiana.

Alabama had one of those “flukes” almost 10 years ago now, when they decided to send Doug Jones
to the Senate, instead of Roy Moore the multiply-accused kid-toucher. And too many people saw it as
just that, a fluke, instead of taking the opportunity to say “Oh wow, look what is possible in
Alabama. Is there something we don’t understand about Alabama?”

Well, the statewide races didn’t go so great in Alabama last night. Tommy Tuberville will be the
governor, but he only beat Doug Jones by four points. Some Republican hick named Barry Moore is
taking Tuberville’s seat in the Senate, having beaten a Democrat named Everett Wess by nine, in a
race that got absolutely zero national attention.

But in the House races, in Alabama’s Second District, which white supremacist Republicans redrew
_specifically_ to confiscate Black voting power after the illegitimate partisan hack Supreme Court
gutted the remainder of the Voting Rights Act, incumbent Democrat Shomari Figures won anyway, by
eight points. Literally the seat that was designed to be taken from him and given to a plantation
owner. Voters said _fuck off_.

And that was not a fluke.

In Louisiana, one of the closest Senate races in the country — Louisiana! Closest! — was won by
Republican Julia Letlow, who barely managed 50.9 percent of the vote over Democrat Jamie Davis, a
Black farmer, who received 49.1 percent. That’s a race that wasn’t on anybody’s radar among
the mainstream punditry. We only really started hearing about it ourselves last week.

Also not a fluke.

And we don’t want to leave out states like Tennessee. As a Tennessean, we will have more to
specifically say about what happened in Tennessee on Tuesday, because from the outside it might not
look like Tennessee participated in the assignment. But it did. _Ohhhhhh_, it did.

Democrats flipped six state House seats. The incoming governor Marsha — _gag_ — Blackburn did
not win a majority, and indeed only beat her challenger Jerri Green by 2.5 points. In 2022,
Republican Governor Bill Lee won by 32.

The white supremacist Tennessee legislature redrew the maps this year to rig them against Black
Memphians, to take away the one majority Black district Tennessee had remaining, before Donald
Trump’s illegitimate partisan hack Supreme Court said that wasn’t necessary anymore. And
congrats, all three of the new districts that carved up Memphis went to Republicans, by margins of
five, six and, as of this writing, 1.1 in the new, absurdly gerrymandered Memphis Ninth.
