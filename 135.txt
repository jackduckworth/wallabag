

====================================================================================================

Man United robbed, Pochettino incensed and Conte relieved to have dodged extraordinary bullet

====================================================================================================

It is a blessed relief that Antonio Conte has never picked up the phone to an 0161 number, so
comprehensively doomed would his eternal search for a regular day of Barclays, Heineken, Mastercard
or any other competition or noodle sponsor be at Manchester United.

Mauricio Pochettino has been in charge for two games and witnessed three penalties, two red cards, a
goal scored through the medium of a divot, two goals disallowed based entirely on vibes, an overhead
kick from a spilled cross, and a preposterous amount of individual errors.

For the referee to be responsible for many of them is sub-optimal. POCHETTINO WAS LEFT INCENSED
after Leny Yoro’s premature – and since rescinded – sending-off ruined his first game; the
performance of Szymon Marciniak in Lisbon is unlikely to have lifted his spirits.

The late dismissal of Youri Tielemans will be described in most quarters as a second bookable
offence, yet in reality it was the first: the Belgian’s booking for existing in the same
stratosphere as Goncalo Inacio as he played Amad in to eventually score was an absolute farce.

That should have been the equaliser Manchester United sought to another questionable decision. There
should rarely if ever be any sympathy for Lisandro Martinez, and he was characteristically
remarkably naive in dangling a lazy leg as Geny Catamo looked for space in the area. But it was the
softest of penalties to concede and award.

It was also the first of two Manchester United gave away in as many minutes. Senne Lammens, seconds
after saving a tame Luis Suarez effort, went double or nothing for the All or Nothing cameras as he
crashed into Maxi Araujo on the edge of his area.

Suarez made no mistake this time and Manchester United, for the seventh game in succession, had
conceded the first goal.

Martinez atoned at the start of the second half, winning a penalty Bruno Fernandes duly converted.
And the Portuguese inexplicably put Manchester United ahead moments later, his tame shot bouncing
over Kaique Pereira and in off a divot he himself created minutes earlier when pressing.

As the captain said post-match: “That was the moment when we should have pushed a little bit more
and it was the other way around – we sat a little bit, we were a little bit afraid of the moment
also probably, and we end up conceding another goal.”

And what a goal. What a stupid, absurd, avoidable, infuriating goal. A high, deflected Ivan Fresneda
cross. An easy claim or punch for Lammens. Another ten minutes to see out. Yet the Belgian keeper
fumbled it under little to no pressure and Suarez reacted quickest to restore parity once more.

That sequence of events would have constituted one of the more ridiculous games of association
football in recent memory already, so Manchester United going up the other end to score a winner
pushed it over the edge and into the realms of nonsense.

And so Marciniak ruling that out as well actually felt fitting. The impediment to Pereira was
minimal and ultimately came after he palmed a Fernandes cross into his own net; it was also pretty
much adjacent to the situation with Lammens moments earlier. So obviously the officials came to a
completely different, Manchester United-screwing conclusion.

Pochettino, WHO MIGHT NOT REMEMBER SOME OF HIS PREVIOUS DEALINGS WITH MARCINIAK PARTICULARLY FONDLY,
appears to have brought some inherent Spurs energy with him.
