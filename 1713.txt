TV tonight: a heart-warming time with Welsh male voice choirs

====================================================================================================

VOICES OF THE VALLEYS

8PM, SKY ARTS
Gwlad! _Gwlaaad!_ A gorgeous three-part documentary following male voice choirs performing "a sound
that can put shivers down your spine" at competitions across Wales, and getting to know why they're
such an important part of their communities. It starts with the Beaufort Male Choir – whose tenor
and soloist Callum Rees will melt hearts as he puts his busking earnings towards the choir. _Hollie
Richardson_

CASUALTY 24/7: EVERY SECOND COUNTS

8PM, CHANNEL 5
The big red phone never stops ringing at Barnsley A&E, as cameras follow ward sister Jo. It's an
episode defined by the old and the very young: while elderly patients are fainting, collapsing with
suspected strokes or struggling with weak hearts, a three-year-old's cut lip might be severe enough
to see him relocated to Sheffield. _Jack Seale_

THE UNDECLARED WAR

9PM, CHANNEL 4
Despite the best intentions of everyone involved, Peter Kosminsky's cyber thriller refuses to catch
fire. However, it has at least managed a decent smoulder by the end of this penultimate episode, in
which Danny, Saara and Roz join forces to help Vadim escape. Then Danny makes a discovery that
changes everything. _Phil Harrison_

999: CRITICAL CONDITION

9PM, CHANNEL 5
To Queen Elizabeth hospital in Birmingham, where care teams calmly tackle potentially life-changing
injuries. Among the patients benefiting is pensioner Graham, who needs a multipart surgical
operation to remove a sinister inner ear tumour before it puts him in a coma. _Graeme Virtue_

GUY MONTGOMERY'S GUY MONT-SPELLING BEE

10PM, BBC THREE

[A cheery looking man in a suit and loud tie stands by a giant illustration of a bee]
Self-effacing speller Tom Walker is back to defend his title in the second series of Montgomery's
charmingly 70s-esque gameshow. But he didn't bargain on Hannah Gadsby's hidden talents or Lloyd
Langford's Welsh advantage. Come for the ridiculous job title guessing game, stay for the struggle
to spell the audience's names. _Hannah Verdier_

THE BIG CASES: THE FUNERAL HOME HORROR

10PM, BBC TWO
A look at the upsetting case of undertaker Robert Bush, who last week was in court for offences
including selling fake funeral plans, giving the ashes of strangers to grieving families and
preventing decent burials. The people betrayed have since been fighting for laws to make sure this
never happens again. _HR_
