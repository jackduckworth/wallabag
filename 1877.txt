TV tonight: inside the Bayeux Tapestry's 'treacherous' journey to London

====================================================================================================

SECRETS OF THE BAYEUX TAPESTRY: INSIDE THE MUSEUM

9PM, BBC TWO
State guests can only dream of the sort of welcome the Bayeux Tapestry has received in London. No
wonder one of the French conservators in this documentary was having nightmares about its
"treacherous journey beneath the channel". Michael Palin narrates the events of its move to the
British Museum, while historians such as David Olusoga and Janina Ramirez help tell the story behind
the stitches, with Rory Stewart also explaining why some call it a "dodgy dossier". _Hollie
Richardson_

EPSTEIN: MONEY, POWER AND ABUSE

8PM, BBC ONE
How did Jeffrey Epstein get away with it for so long? This report explores the paedophile and sex
trafficker's network of contacts among the super-rich and looks at his related cashflows. Clearly,
this is a story with implications that remain important – so how much information still remains
hidden? _Phil Harrison_

FOREVER HOME

9PM, CHANNEL 5
Adapted from Graham Norton's 2022 novel, this moreish six-parter sees a dementia diagnosis amplify
the fault lines in a family. As her older partner Robert (Douglas Henshall) becomes unmoored, Carol
(Amy Nuttall) begins to wonder what really happened to his runaway wife decades ago. Continues
Tuesday and Wednesday. _Graeme Virtue_

COLIN FROM ACCOUNTS

10PM, BBC TWO
After a three-year time jump, the superb Aussie romcom has seen its central couple Ash (Harriet
Dyer) and Gordon (Patrick Brammall) settle down with other partners. But does a frisson remain? They
attempt to clear the air with a brutally funny face-off at a swanky restaurant. Continues Tuesday.
_GV_

GEORDIE COPPERS

10PM, CHANNEL 4
This new series sets itself apart from most fly-on-the-wall UK cop documentaries by shadowing
Newcastle's neighbourhood policing unit. These public-facing bobbies and auxiliary street rangers
remain remarkably upbeat as they deal with unpredictable incidents ranging from Lurpak heists to
drunken brawls. _GV_

LUBAINA HIMID: THIS CULTURAL LIFE

10.30PM, BBC FOUR
John Wilson interviews Turner prize-winner Lubaina Himid, in a televised version of a programme
previously broadcast on Radio 4 earlier this year. Their conversation is wide-ranging but –
naturally – hinges on this feted British artist's pioneering place in the Black arts movement, and
her bold mixed-media works with a satirical bent. _Hannah J Davies_

FILM CHOICE

[A man with bow and arrow]
ROBIN HOOD: PRINCE OF THIEVES (KEVIN REYNOLDS, 1991) 5.35PM, SKY CINEMA GREATS 
This month two entirely different films are out in cinemas, offering alternative takes on the
legendary, wealth-redistributing outlaw. But neither the mortality-contemplating Hugh Jackman one,
nor the mud-splattered Andrew Garfield one, can compete with this Kevin Costner-starring 90s
classic. Alan Rickman was a deserved Bafta-winner for his definitive take on the Sheriff of
Nottingham, but the real villain was that Bryan Adams theme song. _Ellen E Jones_

GET CARTER (MIKE HODGES, 1971) 11.05PM, BBC TWO

[Man in a black suit on the phone]
It's one of the all-time great gangster films, offering a peer down the sidestreets and back alleys
of late-60s Newcastle, plus Michael Caine in his charismatic, menacing prime. He plays Jack Carter,
the titular hardman who returns to his home town after years spent in London's underworld in order
to investigate, then avenge, his brother's death. Writer-director Mike Hodges cleverly incorporated
aspects of real-life local crimes into his script, especially the notorious "one-armed bandit
murder" of 1967, helping to usher in a gritty new era of realism for British crime drama. _EEJ_
