Not Today, Explosive Diarrhea. Not Today.

====================================================================================================

An unpleasant sensation is sweeping the nation: vomiting and explosive diarrhea that can last for
weeks. Caused by _Cyclospora_, a foodborne parasite, the outbreak has spread to at least 34 states.
But public health officials finally appear to be zeroing in on what’s causing it. Here’s what we
know—plus some tips for enjoying a weekend free of gastrointestinal distress.

GROSS. WHERE DOES THIS OUTBREAK SEEM TO BE COMING FROM?

At first it was a mystery. But now public health officials are eyeing lettuce—specifically
shredded iceberg that Taylor Farms, a California-based vegetable supplier, distributed to Taco Bell
restaurants in at least five states. Taylor Farms said today that it would recall certain products
after hearing from the Food and Drug Administration. Officials who spoke to the New York Times say a
single supplier in Mexico originally grew the offending produce.

HOW BAD IS THE OUTBREAK?

If you’re unlucky enough to have gotten the bug yourself, pretty bad! The illness generally
isn’t deadly, but diarrhea—which if untreated may last a month—can cause dangerous
dehydration. In terms of case numbers, things aren’t much better. This is already the U.S.’s
largest recorded outbreak of cyclosporiasis, the hard-to-pronounce illness _Cyclospora_ causes.
According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, more than 1,600 people fell ill after
eating at Taco Bell; we know of at least 141 who have been hospitalized. Michigan, the hardest-hit
state, reports more than 5,000 cases—nearly twice the number of people who get the parasite
nationwide in an average year. And because of incomplete reporting, that’s almost certainly an
undercount.

HOW DOES SOMETHING LIKE THIS HAPPEN?

Foodborne disease outbreaks caused by viruses, bacteria like _E. coli_, or microscopic parasites
like _Cyclospora_ are fairly regular occurrences. Cases often rise in the summer and, as my
colleague Nadira Goffe explains, frequently trace back to leafy greens. Cyclosporiasis comes from
food that’s gotten contaminated with human feces while being washed in fields or processing
plants.

I VERY MUCH DO NOT WANT THIS. SHOULD I JUST AVOID TACO BELL?

That might not hurt, especially if you live in one of the states where Taylor Farms is suspected to
have sent its wares: Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, and West Virginia. But as the food
microbiologist Lee-Ann Jaykus told Nadira, it’s also possible contaminated lettuce ended up
elsewhere—or even that there’s more than one outbreak happening at once. If you’re really
worried, skip uncooked produce like salad mixes, berries, basil, and snap peas for the time being.
Washing or peeling can lessen the risk of infection, but cooking food to at least 158 degrees
reliably kills _Cyclospora_.

AND NOW THE PERENNIAL QUESTION: IS THIS DONALD TRUMP’S FAULT? 

Not directly, but not not. As Slate’s What Next podcast recently explained, the president’s
two-front war on science and the federal bureaucracy probably hasn’t helped. Personnel cuts and
leadership turmoil at the CDC and FDA might be hindering those agencies’ response to the outbreak.
Until this week, states like Michigan had largely taken the lead on tracking cases.

WHEN WILL WE BE RID OF THIS PESTILENCE?

It can take weeks to develop symptoms after getting infected with _Cyclospora_. So even with a
possible culprit identified, the outbreak may get bigger before it gets smaller. The CDC says case
counts could keep rising into August. Happy summer!
