Why Did Trump Lose It Over Natalie Harp—and Who Is She, Anyway? Unfortunately, I’ve Learned Too Much.

On Sunday, Georgia Sen. Jon Ossoff said something that seems to have truly pissed off Trumpworld: He
gave a speech in which he dared to mention a woman in the Trump orbit named Natalie—just Natalie,
no last name given.

Speaking at a campaign event—he is up for reelection in November—Ossoff said the following about
Trump to a crowd: “While the sailors on the _Lincoln_ fight his war, while he fruitlessly drains
our munitions and oil reserves, the president sleeps through his meetings. He golfs and trades
stocks.” He went on: “He doesn’t want to do the job. He wants to build his ballroom and travel
with Natalie on their apparently defenseless flying palace gifted by the emir of Qatar.”

Who is this Natalie that Trump wants to—pause for a full-body shudder—_travel_ with? It’s not
anything so obvious as an affair, if that’s what you’re thinking. It’s somehow worse. Natalie
is Natalie Harp, but she’s better known as Trump’s so-called “human printer.” As an
executive assistant in the White House, she follows him around with a wireless printer, supplying
him with a steady flow of articles and memes about him for him to take in. If you’re wondering why
such a position is necessary in the age of the smartphone and tablet, recall that our president,
ever the boomer, prefers things in hard copy. (Who says he’s not creating jobs?) Harp also
sometimes posts for Trump on social media; people on his team have in fact blamed her for some of
his very worst, most racist posts. She makes a salary of $150,000 doing this, by the way.

Harp has been part of Trump’s staff, and been reported on in media outlets like the New York Times
and the Wall Street Journal, for a while now. After a stint as an anchor at One America News
Network, she joined Trump’s team in 2022, when she first earned the “human printer” nickname.
As that epithet might imply, she is not exactly beloved among the rest of Trump’s staff. It has
been reported that those employees have tried and failed numerous times to push her out, worrying
that she is a danger to herself and the president. The deal seems to be that she’s so scarily
devoted to Trump that she worries her colleagues, which is saying something. But scary levels of
devotion, it perhaps unsurprisingly turns out, are a register that really works for our president,
and all this has paradoxically ensured her continued employment in the White House. Maybe all it
takes to achieve job security in this admin is to do what Harp did and write creepy letters to Trump
in which you refer to him as your “Guardian and Protector in this Life.”

If all this is public knowledge and has been for some time, one wonders why members of Trumpworld
were so rattled by Ossoff’s mention of Natalie. And they were rattled: White House communications
director Steven Cheung wrote a post in response that began, “Jon Jackoff has to be the biggest
cuck loser in politics.” Another White House staffer called Ossoff a “cringeworthy, feminine
theatre kid cosplaying as Barack Obama.” Trump also snapped at a reporter who asked about the
remarks. Part of it may be that Ossoff is starting to look like a contender for the presidential
race in 2028. Another part may be that there’s a difference between newspaper and magazine stories
about Trump’s “human printer” and a clip that can travel around social media when a senator
mentions Natalie by name. Team Trump may figure that nobody reads anymore, but they do watch videos,
and the natural response to this one is to ask: Who’s Natalie?
