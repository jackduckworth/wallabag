TV tonight: a nightmarish thriller about a group of women accused of an awful crime

====================================================================================================

HUNTING ALICE BELL

9PM, CHANNEL 4 
Fran (Alexandra Roach) is a suburban mum working at a leisure centre. When she makes a video for her
work's website, it goes viral for the wrong reasons: trolls believe they have worked out that she
is, in fact, Alice Bell, a former nurse who helped her anaesthetist serial killer boyfriend, Jason
(Simon Pegg), then resumed life with a new identity. As events unravel, Fran learns she's not the
only woman who has been forced to deny she is Bell. But who is telling the truth? Is one of them
lying? Created by David Baddiel and the Guardian's chief film critic, Peter Bradshaw, it's a
nightmare that, in this AI world, never feels too far-fetched. _Hollie Richardson_

CELEBRITY RACE ACROSS THE WORLD

8PM, BBC ONE 
The beautiful Indonesian island of Sulawesi is the destination but, as our celebrities discover,
there are plenty of bus stations and ferry ports in which to bumble around first. Still, this year's
celebrities are a uniformly likable bunch – although some (hello Jeff Stelling) are less happy to
forgo creature comforts than others._ Phil Harrison_

MURDER IN BELFAST: SISTERS SEEKING JUSTICE

9PM, BBC TWO 
In the final episode ofJohn O'Kane's sobering three-parter, will there be any redemption for the
McCartney sisters as the investigation into the murder of their brother Robert concludes? With the
IRA's internal inquiry proving opaque, their hopes are pinned on a trial. The result is revealing in
terms of scars that refuse to heal. _PH_

WINTER

9PM, ITV1 
A defiantly ridiculous crime series ends in suitably overheated fashion, as rogue pathologist Winter
(Richard Armitage, giving it everything) looks into a potential case of a woman held in slavery. As
he strides manfully into the heart of darkness, has our man finally found a puzzle that even his
scalpel-sharp brain can't safely solve? _Jack Seale_

FIRST DATES

10PM, CHANNEL 4 
Fred ushers more hopefuls through the restaurant doors, including shopping TV host Alexandria,
74-year-old singer Lili and 21-year-old funeral director JJ – a Ken-lookalike seeking his very own
Barbie. Meanwhile, advertising exec William has returned for another stab at love – but this time
he's not looking for a wife; he's looking for a husband. _Ali Catterall_

KEN LOACH REMEMBERS: CATHY COME HOME

10PM, BBC FOUR

[A black and white image of a blond woman in a check coat and a dark-haired man in a trench coat]

Even in a career as storied as Ken Loach's, the 1966 TV play Cathy Come Home remains a highlight –
its account of a family becoming homeless still rings horribly true. In this interview, Loach
recalls making the film and the angry aftermath during which the BBC was accused of abandoning
impartiality. _PH_

FILM CHOICE

AVEDON (RON HOWARD, 2026), NETFLIX

"He changed everything." Such high praise is common in Ron Howard's scintillating profile of
photographer Richard Avedon, but based on the stunning images shown here they are fully justified.
His career ranged from shooting IDs for merchant marines to bringing movement and theatricality to
the staid fashion spreads of Harper's Bazaar and Vogue, but this childhood friend of James Baldwin
was also deeply political, with series on the Vietnam war and mental health institutions making
waves. _Simon Wardell_

ISS (GABRIELA COWPERTHWAITE, 2023), 9PM, FILM4 
Better known for investigative documentaries Blackfish and The Grab, Gabriela Cowperthwaite goes
into orbit with this speculative sci-fi thriller set on the International Space Station. Scientist
Kira (the always convincing Ariana DeBose) has just arrived to join the six-person crew – half
American, half Russian – when nuclear war breaks out (glimpsed ominously through the portholes).
Both sides receive orders to take control, but the futility of their efforts is clear as Earth burns
beneath them. Co-starring Chris Messina and Pilou Asbæk, it's a tense drama with a sharp edge of
despair. _SW_

MODESTY BLAISE (JOSEPH LOSEY, 1966), 9.10PM, TALKING PICTURES TV

[A blond woman wearing a light-blue gown sitting on the floor, with blue and pink square wallpaper
behind her]
It can be hard to spoof James Bond (see the Peter Sellers Casino Royale) and Joseph Losey's spy
caper is slightly too in love with the globetrotting and gadgetry that epitomise 007 to be truly
satirical. But with arthouse darling Monica Vitti swanning around in a series of outlandish costumes
as thief and/or secret agent Modesty, assisted by Terence Stamp's suave cockney, it rarely matters.
Dirk Bogarde, who would have been an exceptional Bond villain, is a louche foe in a proto-Barbarella
riot of colour and chaos. _SW_
