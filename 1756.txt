TV tonight: the tragic story of Britain's 'schoolgirl terrorist'

====================================================================================================

THE SCHOOLGIRL TERRORIST?

10PM, CHANNEL 4
What makes a teenage girl threaten to bomb a synagogue? In 2020, Rhianan Rudd, then 15, became the
youngest girl to be charged with terror offences in the UK. She took her own life two years later,
five months after the charges were dropped. This sensitive documentary hears from her family to
learn more about her vulnerable background, mental health issues and how she was groomed online by a
neo-Nazi during lockdown – she even used a bobby pin to scratch a swastika on her head. Was her
case handled correctly? _Hollie Richardson_

THE BIG DEAL WITH STEPH MCGOVERN

8PM, BBC TWO
This new reality series set in the art world has an unmistakable whiff of an Alan Partridge
Dictaphone idea, fused with Flog It!-esque daytime DNA and a dash of The Apprentice-style
wheeler-dealing. First up, our amateur art dealers must sell graduates' works. _Hannah J Davies_

A HOUSE THROUGH TIME

9PM, BBC TWO
David Olusoga's social history series has reached a sixth season for very good reason: the single
residence format remains a brilliant way of illuminating wider history via relatable personal
histories. This time, he's in Edinburgh, exploring a beautiful townhouse that has sheltered everyone
from sculptors to spies. _Phil Harrison_

BETTER DATE THAN NEVER

9PM, BBC THREE

[Two people dressed up in costume]
The return of a heartwarming show following new daters and, first up, 68-year-old farmer Dianne is
back for a second stab at love. Elsewhere, 28-year-old Kento is looking for a fellow fan of anime,
cats and anything to do with Japan – so sushi is the ideal dinner date with Maddie. Fingers
crossed! _HR_

THE COEN BROTHERS STORY

9PM, BBC FOUR
In 2000, documentarian Sarah Aspinall interviewed Joel and Ethan Coen. Now, she looks back at the
experience and how they have since conquered Hollywood, speaking to their star collaborators,
including Tilda Swinton, Jeff Bridges and George Clooney ("They really enjoy just making me the dumb
shit in all their movies … and I love it"). _HR_

POSSESSION

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC
As it reaches the halfway point, this supernatural thriller exploring Jamaica's colonial past is
building up an unsettling head of steam. After a weird encounter at the Hope Hill plantation, Gugu
Mbatha-Raw's conflicted lawyer Claudia is beginning to suspect her posh client Oliver (Jonny Lee
Miller) may not be quite himself. _Graeme Virtue_

FILM CHOICE

[A woman holding her stomach in a pink-painted room filled with streamers and cakes and balloons]
A Child of My Own (Maite Alberdi, 2026), Netflix
Maite Alberdi's documentary starts as a straightforward ripped-from-the-headlines dramatisation of a
secret adoption, shot in the vibrant palette of a telenovela. Mexican hospital worker Alejandra has
just had her third miscarriage when she meets Mayra, who doesn't want the baby she is due to give
birth to. Mayra agrees to hand her newborn over to Alejandra, who meanwhile will pretend to be going
through her own pregnancy. It's when their plan goes wrong that the film pulls the rug out from
under the viewer. As other voices enter the frame, is the narrative we have been consuming true at
all? _Simon Wardell_
