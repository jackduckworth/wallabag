Trump Finally Found the One Thing That Could Cause Senate Republicans to Grow a Spine

====================================================================================================

delivered to your inbox daily._

Question: Is a country trending in a _healthy_ direction if the head of its central bank releases an
SOS message about the political persecution he’s suffering?

On Sunday evening, the Federal Reserve posted a video of Chairman Jay Powell speaking directly to
the camera about how the Justice Department had launched a criminal investigation into the Fed.
While superficially related to excessive Federal Reserve building renovation costs, Powell said,
“the threat of criminal charges is a consequence of the Federal Reserve setting interest rates
based on our best assessment of what will serve the public, rather than following the preferences of
the president.” The administration is trying to spook Powell into cutting rates deeper and, if he
doesn’t, ginning up cause to fire him.

You don’t want a central bank that makes interest rate decisions according to presidential
demands. There’s significant overlap between countries whose leaders meddle in their central
banks’ decisions and those that suffer hyperinflation. This is one of those bright red lines
separating functioning countries—however barely, in the case of the good ol’ U.S.—from the
rest, and why the move against Powell has former Treasury secretaries and Fed chairs rushing to
start a Substack to condemn it.

In a political system with a functioning brain, the threat of criminal charges against a technocrat
for not juicing the economy to the president’s liking would be enough to open an impeachment
inquiry. In the United States political system circa 2025, we’ll get a handful of statements from
Republicans offering meaningful pushback. Fortunately, one of those Republicans has real power in
this situation.

“If there were any remaining doubt whether advisers within the Trump Administration are actively
pushing to end the independence of the Federal Reserve, there should now be none,” North Carolina
Sen. Thom Tillis said in a statement Sunday night. “It is now the independence and credibility of
the Department of Justice that are in question. I will oppose the confirmation of any nominee for
the Fed—including the upcoming Fed Chair vacancy—until this legal matter is fully resolved.”

This is more than your usual meaningless Senate threat. What he’s saying, in short, is that unless
Trump’s Justice Department drops this stick-up against Powell, he’s not going to get the Fed
chair of his dreams wants when Powell’s term as chair ends in May.

Tillis’ position is important for two reasons.

First, he’s retiring at the end of the year, which gives him the political space to behave like an
adult. Had he chosen to run again in 2026 and was facing down a primary from his right, he may well
have been in Powell’s driveway last night trying to make a citizen’s arrest.

Second, Tillis serves as a pivotal vote on the Banking Committee, which considers Fed nominees. The
committee is comprised of 13 Republicans and 11 Democrats. If Tillis joined all Democrats in
opposing a Fed nominee, the committee would be deadlocked and the nomination unable to advance out
of committee. And while the full Senate could try to force a nomination out of committee, a vote to
do so would be subject to the filibuster—meaning it would need Democratic votes to get to 60.
Powell, meanwhile, could stay on the Fed: While his tenure as chairman expires in May, his term on
the board lasts until 2028.

Then, there’s always the chance that Senate Republicans could go “nuclear”—meaning, to set a
new precedent, by simple majority vote, eliminating the filibuster on votes to force nominees out of
committee. There are 53 Republican senators, so Tillis’ vote wouldn’t be needed.

But are we to believe that Tillis, while being one of the few to express such outrage, is the only
Senate Republican who feels so strongly about preserving Fed independence? Of course not. His
retirement, though, allows him to do the dirty work and absorb whatever expressions of
disappointment from Trump are coming his way. He has the political breathing room, and the right
committee assignment, to constrain one of Trump’s very worst ideas on behalf of others who are,
privately, terrified about Trump’s move against the Fed.

And if you’re trying to measure how convincing Tillis’ statement was, well, consider the
markets: The day after the Federal Reserve chair, who manages monetary policy for the country that
mints the world’s reserve currency, released a hostage video, the S&P 500 was still somehow up 11
points at close on Monday.

Even with this effective pushback, though, what’s frightening is how precarious it all is. What if
Tillis were running again? What if he weren’t on the Banking Committee? Who, then, would stand up?

Don’t let the markets think too much about that.
