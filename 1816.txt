Premier League: 10 things to look out for this weekend

====================================================================================================

-------------------------

Enzo Maresca has a shiny new £85m midfielder he could select for a debut but, at 18 years old, he
may want Ayyoub Bouaddi to have a bit of time watching on from the bench to learn about the Premier
League from his elders. The Moroccan may not have anticipated one of those to be a centre-back by
trade. Marc Guéhi will be eager to make a triumphant return to Crystal Palace and may well have the
opportunity to do so from central midfield, where he played last weekend in the victory over
Bournemouth, scoring the equaliser. The centre-back was a surprise selection in the blocker role
next to Elliot Anderson, but equipped himself admirably and was not limited to the dirty work, often
being found in more advanced positions in open play and happy to have the ball at his feet. His
long-term future is unlikely to be outside of defence but further proof of Guéhi's adaptability
will be a positive for Maresca, as a long season awaits. WILL UNWIN

-------------------------

	*
_Crystal Palace v Manchester City, Friday 8pm (all times BST)_

2

LIVERPOOL NEED TO STOP GIBBS-WHITE

"It is just not personnel," insisted Andoni Iraola in response to Liverpool conceding two goals to
quick Newcastle transitions in his first Premier League game as the club's head coach. Some
long-term faults can not be rectified overnight – although that did not prevent some hysterical
reactions to the performance at St James' Park – and with Liverpool prioritising wingers in the
final days of the transfer window the answer to this particular weakness, as Iraola indicated, lies
on the training ground. Morgan Gibbs-White revelled in the spaces behind Liverpool's midfield last
season as he – and set-pieces – helped Nottingham Forest to a second successive league win at
Anfield. Iraola does not want to curb Ryan Gravenberch's attacking instincts but greater tactical
discipline and willingness to do the dirty work from the entire Liverpool midfield will be needed to
stop Gibbs-White producing a repeat performance. Forest's new record signing, Liam Delap, has also
been registered in time to play. ANDY HUNTER

-------------------------

3

EVERTON NOTE CHERRIES' SET-PIECE STRUGGLES

There were so many encouraging takeaways for Bournemouth despite an agonising late defeat at
Manchester City. The catalyst for City's turnaround was Marc Guéhi's 84th-minute equaliser, the
England defender heading in unmarked at a corner, after wasting a near-identical earlier chance. For
Bournemouth, it was a familiar source of pain; only Liverpool conceded more set-piece goals in the
top flight last season (20), with the Cherries and Crystal Palace both conceding 18. Bournemouth
sought to address their vulnerability in the summer by hiring their first dedicated set-piece coach
in Nicolas Gagnon, who worked with Canada at the World Cup and previously at Southampton and in
Saudi Arabia. Only Arsenal, Manchester City, Manchester United and Aston Villa claimed more points
away from home than Everton last season and David Moyes's centre-backs, Jarrad Branthwaite and James
Tarkowski, will be licking their lips if Bournemouth do not sharpen up their act. BEN FISHER

4

TOO EARLY FOR A RELEGATION SIX-POINTER?

Contrasting opening weekends for Coventry and Hull, harsh reality dealt out against the dream
scenario lived out. At Arsenal, Coventry were well beaten by the champions, while in East Yorkshire,
Manchester United were soundly, deservedly defeated. Despite contrasting starts, both teams remain
warm favourites for relegation. Two defeats in a row for Frank Lampard's team would dim the optimism
of a summer of anticipation. Hull seek to extend the reverie of last week though Coventry are
unlikely to be caught as cold as United last week, and surely cannot defend set pieces so badly. The
last three Championship matches between these two teams ended in draws, last season's meetings
goalless, although both squads have since been upgraded by brisk transfer business. At Hull, much is
expected of Lucas Herrington who missed Australia's fateful penalty against Egypt at the World Cup.
The teenage defender shone against Stoke in the Tigers' successful midweek Carabao Cup assignment.
JOHN BREWIN

-------------------------

	*
_Coventry v Hull, Saturday 3pm_

5

TOTTENHAM KEEN FOR TONALI TO TICK

It has not taken long for Sandro Tonali to be reunited with his former teammates. After succumbing
3-0 on the opening weekend at Brentford, a home victory will be the minimum requirement for
Tottenham. Roberto De Zerbi and his players will be desperate to show that things have changed after
a busy summer in the transfer market. Tonali will be a key part of that; he was the only central
midfielder who made it past half-time in west London, so at least has minutes in his legs to build
on. The Italian will be eager to not suffer at the hands of a team that served him well after
bringing him to England. Tonali is supposed to make his new team tick and a rousing home debut would
do a lot for everyone's confidence, while defeat will bring plenty more questions. WU

-------------------------

	*
_Tottenham v Newcastle, Saturday 5.30pm_

6

ACHEAMPONG FIGHTS TO STAY WITH BLUES

This is an important moment in Josh Acheampong's development. The 20-year-old academy graduate did
not have many opportunities last year but he started in Xabi Alonso's back three when Chelsea beat
Fulham on Monday. It was a big vote of confidence from Alonso in the young defender, who played
well, and may indicate that Acheampong will stay at Stamford Bridge this season. Several clubs are
interested in the England youth international and there is talk of a loan before the transfer window
shuts, but Acheampong would strengthen his position further if he is selected again for Sunday's
home game against Brighton. JACOB STEINBERG

-------------------------

	*
_Chelsea v Brighton, Sunday 2pm_

-------------------------

On Monday night, Jamie Carragher told a Sky Sports audience that he thought Sunderland might be
relegated this season. The former Liverpool defender compared Régis Le Bris's team to the Sheffield
United side managed by Chris Wilder that went down in 2020-2021 – their second season after
promotion – and suggested Sunderland were "arrogant". How will Le Bris's players respond against
Fulham at the Stadium of Light on Sunday? Has the moment arrived to end a winless Premier League run
against the west London side stretching back 12 years? If there are legitimate concerns about the
ability of Le Bris's players to cope with a Europa League campaign, regular Sunderland watchers
struggle to buy into the accusations or arrogance, let alone the analogy with a very different
Sheffield United side. Nonetheless, last Saturday's 2-1 defeat at Ipswich has prompted a certain
anxiety, leaving Wearsiders hoping Nordi Mukiele is fit enough to raise defensive standards. LOUISE
TAYLOR

8

LEEDS LOOK TO STRENGTHEN AS BEES BUZZ

Sub-plots abound at Elland Road as Leeds and Brentford meet after two wins apiece from their first
competitive matches of the season. There is the goalkeeping beauty contest between Daniel Farke's
showpiece summer signing, James Trafford – who has registered two clean sheets in his opening
games – and Brentford's Caoimhin Kelleher. Further forward, fans can ponder the question of which
centre forward, the Leeds favourite Dominic Calvert-Lewin or his opposite number Igor Thiago will
prove the more incisive. Similarly the technical area tactical duel involving Daniel Farke and Keith
Andrews looks extremely hard to call. Away from the pitch, Farke is hoping to win a race with
Sunderland to sign Marseille winger Igor Paixão for around £30m as the Leeds manager urges his
board to strengthen. "I'm happy with my group and my signings so far," said Farke. "But we know we
can definitely improve our squad. That is what Leeds supporters and Leeds United deserve." LT

-------------------------

	*
_Leeds v Brentford, Sunday 2pm_

-------------------------

9

CARRICK UNDER PRESSURE TO DELIVER

Manchester United are bloody-nosed and Ipswich Town definitely delighted after the tale of each
team's results on the opening weekend. At Hull City, Michael Carrick's team played with zero
cohesion and conviction and went down to a miserable 2-0 loss. At Portman Road Gary O'Neil's side
hosted Sunderland and claimed a rousing late 2-1 win, so arrive at Old Trafford with a bounce in the
step. Carrick has to lift his men and get a result – preferably victory. A loss, at home, to
another promoted team, would begin the latest inquest into a United No 1: a predicament suffered by
every manager post-Sir Alex Ferguson. JAMIE JACKSON

-------------------------

	*
_Manchester United v Ipswich, Sunday 4.30pm_

-------------------------

10

VILLA FANS BID FAREWELL TO KONSA

At a time when the manner of a player's departure feels particularly pertinent, especially at Aston
Villa, Ezri Konsa can expect a warm reception on his return to the club. Konsa could make his
Arsenal debut at Villa Park against his former club, where he excelled across seven seasons. A £12m
buy from Brentford in 2019, Konsa surely represents one of their finest signings in the Premier
League era. He joined fresh from a single season in the Championship with Brentford and left as an
England regular and Europa League winner. Konsa's role in Villa's recent success should not be
overlooked. Unai Emery can be judged by Villa's extraordinary run of league finishes – seventh,
fourth, sixth and fourth – but also his track record of turning promising talents, like Konsa,
into fully-fledged internationals. BF
