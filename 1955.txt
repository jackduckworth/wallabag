TV tonight: Michael Palin encounters 'recycled chicken' in the Philippines

====================================================================================================

MICHAEL PALIN IN THE PHILIPPINES

9PM, CHANNEL 5
Michael Palin remains an adventurous traveller in the Philippines, a complex and challenging
country. He explores the chaotic soap opera generated by the Marcos family, now back in government
thanks to the rise of Bongbong, the president – who turns out to be a Monty Python fan. Elsewhere,
Palin sees the country's poverty, meeting a woman selling "recycled chicken". _Phil Harrison_

JAMIE'S 20 MINUTE MEALS

8PM, CHANNEL 4
Jamie Oliver is back with a new gimmick: he's cooking his recipes in one single shot to offer proof
of concept. It's not really necessary – as ever, while the man himself may be an acquired taste,
his food (camembert and onion rice and a crispy chicken dish) looks both achievable and delicious.
_PH_

CLICKBAIT CLINIC WITH STACEY DOOLEY

8.30PM, BBC ONE
Is munching on a diet of only meat good for you? It sounds like a question that's too stupid to
answer, but Dooley is determined to give it a fair crack. A lovable bodybuilder states his case for
the carnivore diet, then doctors Nighat Arif and Alex George give their perspectives. _Hannah
Verdier_

FINDING BRITAIN'S SLAVE TRADERS WITH DAVID OLUSOGA

9PM, BBC TWO
The expansion of the slave trade in the 18th century spread wealth and commerce across Britain. In
Bristol alone, social-climbing merchants and investors funded charities, built grand homes – and
what ultimately became the Bristol Old Vic. "Institutions they knew would outlive them," as David
Olusoga points out, in the second part of this furious two-part exposé. _Ali Catterall_

PETE WICKS: FOR DOGS' SAKE

9PM, U&W

[A man sitting with a large grey dog]
The reality star begins his fourth series profiling the work of canine charity the Dogs Trust. It's
heartstring-tugging business as usual, as Wicks meets a traumatised jack russell cross undergoing
rehab for past trauma and a labrador hoping for a new life as an assistance dog. Hankies at the
ready. _Alexi Duggins_

COLIN FROM ACCOUNTS

10PM, BBC TWO
We all know where this sublime romcom is going – but the journey is as much fun as the arrival.
This penultimate episode once again proves that much of the show's hilarity is mined from real
emotional heft. Ash attends her grandmother's funeral and, with feelings running high, decides it's
time for her and Gordon to face facts. _PH_

FILM CHOICE

NICKEL BOYS (RAMELL ROSS, 2024), 11.05PM, BBC TWO

[two men looking up into a mirrored image of themselves]
Based on the Pulitzer prize-winning novel by Colson Whitehead, RaMell Ross's sublime drama is,
unusually, shot from the first-person point of view. It's a bold move that pays off, as we're
immersed in the life of Elwood (Ethan Herisse), a Black student raised by his grandma (a standout
Aunjanue Ellis-Taylor) in early 60s Florida. A miscarriage of justice sends him to a brutal,
segregated reform school called the Nickel Academy. His only respite is a friendship with another
inmate, Turner (Brandon Wilson), as he tries to work out how to live in a world intent on beating
him down. _Simon Wardell_

IMMACULATE (MICHAEL MOHAN, 2024),MIDNIGHT, BBC THREE
This ripe piece of nunsploitation from Michael Mohan owes a lot to Rosemary's Baby – but the evil
forces at play here are entirely Christian. Controversy magnet Sydney Sweeney plays a young American
who arrives at a rural Italian convent to take her vows. Soon, however, she falls mysteriously
pregnant. "It's a miracle," proclaim the mother superior, doctor and priests (including Money
Heist's Álvaro Morte) – but how did this virgin birth occur? With creaky doors and creepy
sisters, it's classic, bloodily accomplished horror. _SW_
