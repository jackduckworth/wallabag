

====================================================================================================

Premier League: 10 talking points from the weekend's action

====================================================================================================

-------------------------

1

EVERTON NEED GREALISH'S ENERGY

With 25 minutes to go against Manchester United, the Everton supporters had something to roar about
as Jack Grealish made his return after rejoining on loan. The attacking midfielder remains
universally popular thanks to his desire to entertain, getting the hosts back on their feet by
creating dangerous situations within seconds of his arrival. Everton were limited in the final third
for long periods as Thierno Barry sat on the periphery. If David Moyes is to keep his side pushing
for a top-half finish, he will need Grealish to be a creative focal point. WILL UNWIN

Match report: EVERTON 2-2 MANCHESTER UNITED

-------------------------

2

TZOLIS STANDS OUT AMONG NEW FACES

There was no shortage of highlights from the new arrivals as Arsenal battled back to beat Chelsea,
Morgan Rogers setting the tone with a well-taken goal within two minutes. Emi Martínez got a
hostile reception on his return to the Emirates but, while he may feel disappointed not to have kept
out Kai Havertz's opener, he made a handful of excellent saves, a diving stretch to deny Bukayo Saka
the best of the bunch. Ezri Konsa, on his first start for Arsenal, had the home fans on their feet
after a thunderous slide tackle to stop Rogers early in the second half, the standout moment of a
solid performance. It was Christos Tzolis who really took the game by the scruff of the neck,
however, harrying Chelsea's defence with mazy dribbles and driving runs forward. He also provided
the diagonal ball which, via Havertz's audacious dummy, set up Martin Ødegaard to score the winner,
earning a first Premier League assist. WILL MAGEE

Match report: ARSENAL 2-1 CHELSEA

[Arsenal's Riccardo Calafiori and Christos Tzolis sandwich Chelsea's Pedro Neto]
-------------------------

3

SCOTT SHOWCASES ENGLAND POTENTIAL

If Thomas Tuchel required a reminder of precisely how good Alex Scott is, the Bournemouth midfielder
provided it at St James' Park. The 23-year-old reads the game a little faster and a little better
than those around him and his stellar passing range raised the tone in a game Marco Rose's side
really should have won after cantering into a 2-0 lead, with Scott helping to create both goals.
While Newcastle's manager, Matthias Jaissle, can be heartened by his team's refusal to surrender
and, courtesy of goals from Harvey Barnes and Jacob Ramsey, their ultimate collection of a point,
for long periods his players found no answers to Scott's questions. Surely a first senior England
cap beckons. The forthcoming Nations League fixtures against Spain, Croatia and Czechia offer ideal
openings for Tuchel to put Scott on the international stage his talent demands. LOUISE TAYLOR

Match report: NEWCASTLE 2-2 BOURNEMOUTH

-------------------------

4

ARBELOA ALREADY SEEKING SOLUTIONS

The omens don't look good for Álvaro Arbeloa. Only twice before have Fulham started a top-flight
season with three successive defeats and it ended with relegation on both occasions: in 1951-52 and
in 2020-21 under Scott Parker. Arbeloa tried to concentrate on the positives after throwing away the
lead twice against Crystal Palace and ending up losing to Ben Chilwell's winner. His side looked
dangerous in attack, with Josh King having already developed an understanding with the summer
signing Gonzalo García, even if the Spaniard played a comical role in Tyrick Mitchell's second
equaliser. Fulham's defending is another matter entirely: Joachim Anderson's lack of pace was
repeatedly exposed by the high line they deployed against Palace and Arbeloa must find a solution
before facing his former club Liverpool and then Manchester United. "We knew that this month would
be very tough," said Arbeloa. "But I told my players it will not define our entire season." ED
AARONS

Match report: FULHAM 2-3 CRYSTAL PALACE

-------------------------

5

GAKPO DELIVERS CREATIVE EDGE

Several positives for Liverpool in Suffolk on Friday: Bradley Barcola showing off his lightning
speed in his cameo, Ronald Araújo solid in the troublesome right-back position and Alexander Isak
coming back to the boil with two well-taken goals. Receiving the most plaudits in post-match was
Cody Gakpo, who appeared headed for Manchester City or Tottenham in the transfer window but stayed
on to supply both Isak goals. "Cody knows how much we value him," said Andoni Iraola. The confirmed
exit of the sporting director, Richard Hughes, on Saturday brought assessments of a mixed legacy.
Eventually, Isak repeating the form of his Newcastle days may stamp the decision to pay a record fee
for the striker. Florian Wirtz, another expensive Hughes purchase, continues to receive similarly
mixed reviews. At Ipswich, attention was drawn to his losing possession 22 times. It was left to
Gakpo, whose purchase and sale were deals Hughes did not do, to provide Liverpool's creative edge.
JOHN BREWIN

Match report: IPSWICH 0-2 LIVERPOOL

[Liverpool's Cody Gakpo tries to break clear of Ipswich's Julio Enciso]
-------------------------

6

NDIAYE COULD NOT REFUSE CITY'S OFFER

Iliman Ndiaye enthused about working hard in his Manchester City debut after his £65m transfer from
Everton. "I ran my backside off for the team wanting to get the win and you have to do the dirty
side sometimes," said the Senegalese forward, who was removed on 87 minutes, and brushed off how he
might have joined Tottenham instead. "It was always going to be City. I wanted Champions League
[football] and to play for one of the best teams in the world. I'm not saying Tottenham is not but
when Manchester City comes through you don't say no. It's been a good week for me and topped off
with a win. I was just being patient and when City came through I was buzzing." JAMIE JACKSON

Match report: MANCHESTER CITY 1-0 COVENTRY

-------------------------

7

BRIGHTON FIND ANOTHER DIAMOND

There were plenty of questions for Fabian Hürzeler to answer after Brighton's draw with Leeds,
namely why it took them the best part of an hour to wake up. But, for consecutive weekends, the
Brighton manager had the pleasure of lauding Malick Yalcouyé's performance. The 20-year-old was
barely known even by his own club's fans at the start of the campaign, having spent seasons on loan
at Sturm Graz and Swansea since joining from Gothenburg for a reported £6m in July 2024. He has
since started against Chelsea and Leeds, excelling in an attacking midfield role. Hürzeler said:
"He shows a lot of energy, a lot of effort, he is good in possession and out of possession." The
Brighton manager has previously flagged Yalcouyé's physicality as an area to improve and it is
notable just how slight he is at just 5ft 7in. The Ivorian's confidence, eagerness to enter combat
and close control are in no doubt. He could be the next player off the conveyor belt of young talent
who Brighton sell for a vast profit. BEN BLOOM

Match report: BRIGHTON 1-1 LEEDS

-------------------------

8

TONALI DUEL SUMS UP TOTTENHAM'S SPIRIT

On the eve of Tottenham firing blanks for the third straight game, Roberto De Zerbi joked Allan
Dixon's bodybuilder physique could help them threaten at set pieces. Tottenham's player liaison, who
is Daniel Levy's brother-in-law, moved up the pecking order again after Spurs failed to score in
their first three league games for the first time since 1974-75. The club spent more than £300m
this summer, Sandro Tonali the pick of the new signings on a blunt outing at the City Ground. De
Zerbi praised Spurs' spirit in the stalemate, epitomised by Tonali during a first-half duel with Dan
Ndoye which culminated in a Forest corner. "I think it will be an advertising clip for the Premier
League," said Forest's head coach, Oliver Glasner. "This is what the league is famous for and
everyone loves." BEN FISHER

Match report: NOTTINGHAM FOREST 0-0 TOTTENHAM

-------------------------

9

SUNDERLAND'S AGENT OF CHAOS

Nilson Angulo, who scored for Ecuador against Germany at the World Cup, remains an enigmatic
presence, at once robust and delicate, cunning and naive, deft and clumsy. At times, not obviously
related to the game going on around him, he will simply stop, and waggle his legs around as though
stirring paint with his feet. One volleyed flick with the back of his ankle to return an overhit
cross to the middle was remarkable. He is a master of accelerating and suddenly slowing to create
space, control immaculate, then at other times he will simply charge out of play. The only thing
harder than marking him, perhaps, is playing alongside him, trying to work out if a cross is ever
going to come. He is a magnificent agent of chaos, that is clear; whether he is a high-grade
footballer is less obvious. JONATHAN WILSON

Match report: BRENTFORD 1-1 SUNDERLAND

[Sunderland's Nilson Angulo attempts to keep the ball in play against Brentford]
-------------------------

10

TEENAGE DEFENDER COMING OF AGE

Not many teenage centre-halves play regularly in the Premier League, but the Australia international
Lucas Herrington is quietly establishing himself in Hull's acclimatisation to the top flight. The
summer signing from Colorado Rapids celebrated turning 19 on Saturday by coming off the bench
against Aston Villa to help the Tigers keep another clean sheet. Herrington is rated as one of the
finest defensive prospects in world football and underlined his promise with a vital block to deny
Tyrone Mings. In all three of Hull's Premier League games this season, Herrington has featured from
the bench and the Queenslander's star should continue to rise throughout the season. The Hull
manager, Sergej Jakirovic, said: "Herrington is excellent and has great potential, so all these
minutes are invaluable for him. I don't have any fear when he's on the pitch and I believe in him
100%. Provided he stays healthy, anything is possible." ROSS HEPPENSTALL

Match report: HULL 0-0 ASTON VILLA
