The Details of Natalie Harp’s Relationship With Trump Are Only Getting Stranger

====================================================================================================

The journalist Michael Wolff, who has been chronicling Trump for years, says he obtained both
letters while writing a book about the president’s 2024 campaign. Wolff is an imperfect source;
he’s previously been accused of making up quotes. But although no other media outlet has
independently confirmed the letters’ authenticity, they match excerpts the New York Times quoted
in a 2024 story about Harp joining the White House.

The letters’ full text makes for some pretty uncomfortable reading. In one, apparently written
during or after an overseas golfing trip, Harp apologizes for having sprinted after the president
for lack of a golf cart. “I want things to always be right between us,” she allegedly wrote,
adding, “I see myself being lowered in your eyes and good opinion. That is the fear you see,
because I never want to bring you anything but joy. I’m sorry I lost my focus. You are all that
matters to me. I don’t want to ever let you down.” That letter was signed “With all my
heart” and had Harp’s signature at the bottom. In the second letter, signed “Always,” Harp
describes being envious of people “whose only ‘job’ seems to be to talk with you, and look
pretty,” adding, “I want that job!!” She later writes that she misses the days when she and
Trump would talk on the phone “about everything and nothing.” As the Daily Beast notes, the
letters were written when Harp was 31 and Trump was 76.

There’s more. Harp started working for Trump in 2022, after a stint parroting his lies about the
2020 election as a TV host at the far-right One America News Network. Her job—which earned her the
internal moniker “the human printer”—has included posting remarks Trump dictates to Truth
Social, texting on his behalf, and schlepping around a portable printer so she can feed him hard
copies of news articles in person. According to the Times, Harp regularly filters content from
social media and fringe websites to the president, including a video Trump’s account posted that
depicted Barack and Michelle Obama as apes and an article that included an image of him preparing to
hit a political opponent’s head with a baseball bat. She is frequently by the president’s side,
often smiling and staring fixedly at him, and once reportedly rode in an SUV trunk to accompany him
to a court date because there wasn’t a seat for her. Her proximity to Trump—and to sensitive
government information—has concerned some fellow staffers; Harp reportedly worked at the White
House without a security clearance for a year. But Trump seems flattered by her devotion. He calls
her “sweetie” and has told other aides that “Harp is the only one who loves him as much as his
wife and children” and “will never leave him,” the Times reports.

We don’t know for sure. Ossoff, notably, didn’t mention a romantic connection. And the facts he
referenced in his speech—that Harp was one of the few lucky White House aides to secretly escape
_Air Force One_ with the president via an airport catering truck last month—had been reported that
week. The Times, CNN, and other outlets that have covered the imbroglio have scrupulously avoided
using the word _affair_. Maybe they’re just being careful. Or maybe Harp’s relationship with
Trump really hasn’t tipped into romance.

ARE THERE OTHER POTENTIAL EXPLANATIONS? 

Harp credits a 2018 bill Trump signed with giving her access to an experimental cancer drug that she
says saved her life. She is, of course, currently his employee. In both alleged letters, Harp
reflects on her father’s suicide and thanks Trump for “always being there for me” as her
“Guardian and Protector in this Life.” In a CNN interview this week, Harp’s estranged brother
Preston argued that her fascination with Trump stems from her upbringing. “My mom raised us with
the idea that American exceptionalism was the correct way of looking at things, that the United
States is the boss of the world,” he said, adding, “Trump is, like, everything that my mom
taught us to respect. So I think that’s where her infatuation for Trump comes from, honestly,
because I don’t think it’s a physical attraction. Let’s get real.” And for what it’s
worth, the White House has strenuously denied that there’s anything inappropriate about Trump and
Harp’s relationship.

SURE, BUT DEMOCRATS SAY MEAN THINGS ABOUT THIS PRESIDENT ALL THE TIME. WHY HAVE OSSOFF’S COMMENTS
ABOUT HARP DRAWN SUCH A HEATED RESPONSE FROM TRUMPWORLD? 

As the Atlantic reported this week, the “pushback was so overwhelming, so obviously coordinated,
so ugly and personal that _it_ became a story.” On Monday, White House Communications Director
Steven Cheung called Ossoff “the biggest cuck loser in politics.” Trump spokesman Davis Ingle
labeled him “a cringeworthy, feminine theatre kid cosplaying as Barack Obama.” And a White
House–run X account flamed a CNN journalist who asked Trump about Ossoff’s remarks, calling her
“a disgraceful, humiliating embarrassment to her alleged profession” and “callous and
vindictive” in a way that her children would one day be “sickened and embarrassed” by. The
core of this counterargument is that it’s sexist to assume that a woman working in the White House
is romantically linked to her boss, but all the protest is a bit confusing: For one thing, Trump is
the king of innuendo and sexism. Let’s not forget that his political career took off due to his
anti-Obama birther slurs, or his suggestion that Ted Cruz’s father was connected to the JFK
assassination, or the scores of vile remarks he has made about women over the decades. Second,
Ossoff never said Trump and Harp were romantically linked. The line that set off the frenzy, in its
entirety, is: “He wants to build his ballroom and travel with Natalie on their apparently
defenseless flying palace gifted by the emir of Qatar.”

Then again, maybe the White House’s intended audience is more narrow. Melania Trump has in the
past been sensitive to discussions of Trump’s infidelities. In 2018, when news of his hush money
payments to the porn actor Stormy Daniels broke, the first lady reportedly left Washington abruptly
and later took a separate car to her husband’s first State of the Union address. “The first lady
doesn’t like these types of things,” someone close to the White House told Semafor this week. On
Thursday, she and Trump conspicuously held hands at a White House event. “I heard you missed
me,” Melania joked to the crowd. “Here I am.”

IN TRUMP AND MELANIA’S DEFENSE, HASN’T THE PRESIDENT’S ORBIT ALWAYS INCLUDED STRANGE PEOPLE?
SO IS HARP’S DEVOTION REALLY THAT UNUSUAL? 

It’s a fair question. Trump revels in flattery, which has enabled even some pretty controversial
figures to get close to him. In his first term, the former _Apprentice_ star Omarosa Manigault
Newman reportedly had free access to the Oval Office even though she was later forced out of the
administration over a series of scandals—including letting members of her wedding party traipse
around the White House on a Saturday. Trump’s frequent proximity to the Islamophobic conspiracy
theorist Laura Loomer during the 2024 campaign similarly fed rumors that the two were having an
affair. (Loomer denied them, but they also didn’t draw nearly the official pushback that the Harp
stories have.) And Trump has been willing to overlook public embarrassments from Pete Hegseth,
Howard Lutnick, and other Cabinet secretaries because they are—or appear to be—true believers.
Earlier sycophancy aimed at the president has even taken epistolary form. In his first term, Trump
exchanged what he famously called “love letters” with brutal North Korean dictator Kim Jong-un.

DO WE THINK OSSOFF MEANT TO TRIGGER THE FIRESTORM HE DID?

I’m a believer that politics is usually more like _Veep_ than _House of Cards_; even
world-changing events unfold more often by accident or luck than by design. Maybe Ossoff’s
reference was just a tossed-off line that happened to blow up. But Ossoff, who’s running for
reelection in a swing state that Trump won twice, is a deliberate and relentlessly on-message
communicator—sometimes to the point of looking and sounding scripted. The notion that he’d
ad-lib a mention of Harp, or include one in his prepared remarks without thinking through the
possible ramifications, strains believability.

And either way, he has since doubled down. “It’s been amazing to watch this White House melt
down over this all week,” he told MS NOW, calling Harp fair game. “These White House staffers
are adults, public officials paid by the taxpayer with immense power in positions of public
trust.” Polls suggest that Ossoff will easily win his Senate race this fall, which has stoked
speculation that he may run for president in 2028. And if he does, this may not be the last we hear
from him about Natalie.
