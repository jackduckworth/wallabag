Even Donald Trump Is Taking Note of Hunter Biden’s Online Rampage. I Have Concerns.

====================================================================================================

There’s something so vintage about Hunter Biden’s sudden Twitter spree. The son of former
President Joe Biden rejoined the platform in May and this week started posting at a remarkable clip:
102 posts Monday, 85 on Tuesday, 74 on Wednesday, and so on. (These counts come courtesy of People.)
Circa 10 to 15 years ago, it seemed like hardly a day went by without a famous person joining
Twitter and going viral for their “clapbacks,” which would be celebrated by then-ascendant
digital media outlets like BuzzFeed. Now Twitter/X and BuzzFeed are shells of their former selves,
and celebrities tend to be a little more savvy about wading into the cesspool. But as someone who
knows from cesspools—having survived addiction, immense personal loss, and the Republican
Party—Hunter Biden apparently didn’t flinch at the opportunity.

Biden’s tweets have leaned into his status as an “ironic icon,” as the New York Times once
called him, a sleazy-hot failson whose weakness for drugs and sex workers only makes him more
lovable to certain demographics, namely progressive but disillusioned millennials and Gen Zers. A
common theme in his posts has been candid and self-deprecating references to his struggle with
substances. Biden’s most widely circulated tweet this week was a reply to a user who demanded that
he admit that a bag of cocaine that was infamously found in the White House during Joe Biden’s
presidency was his. “It most definitely was not,” the younger Biden wrote. “I would never have
forgotten my drugs.” In another reply, he clarified that cocaine was actually not his drug of
choice. “And least get it right,” he wrote. “I smoked crack. I would never have wasted coke on
snorting it.”

Sometimes, these drug references were made in service of a point, like when Biden attempted to
counter a user’s suggestion that he is a member of the “elite oligarch class.” He responded
with a familiar photo of himself, shirtless and ciggy in mouth, from the long-ago stolen laptop that
became a lightning rod during the first Trump administration. “Do I look like I’m part of the
elite oligarch class. This was taken at a super 8 motel off I95 by the way,” he wrote. Other
posts, though, seemed to reference his past purely for the fun of it, like when he responded to a
meme of himself with a pipe: “I know this may sound petty, but I can’t stand it when people put
photoshop a meth pipe in my mouth,” he wrote. “A crack pipe doesn’t have that little bowl at
the end. This is why we can’t trust AI. Please make the appropriate edit. Thank you for your
attention to this matter.” (That end flourish, of course, is a reference to a phrase President
Trump often uses on Truth Social.)

Elsewhere, Hunter raged against CNN’s Jake Tapper and his criticism of the Biden family coupled
with a relative disregard for the Trump family’s corruption. He made several attempts to engage
with trolls humanely, trying to find common ground with one who called his family “a disgrace”
by pointing out that the user’s profile picture was of Johnny Cash, someone who he also considered
a hero. He said that Cash, like him, had brushes with the law and was a heavy drug user, but he got
clean and “spent the rest of his life singing for prisoners and addicts and the people the country
threw away because he knew he was one of them.” It was a surprisingly lovely little note.

Pretty much everyone online seems to be loving Hunter’s Twitter era, responding with a familiar
set of compliments: He’s posting bangers, we’re obsessed, he’s out of fucks and we’re here
for it, etc. He’s up to nearly half a million followers. This is all well and good and I agree to
a point: Thanks for all the lols, Hunter.

But I kind of hope that sort of talk stops before he gets, to use a perhaps unfortunate metaphor,
high on his own supply. Already, there have been calls for him to run for president in 2028, calls
that Trump himself has responded to. (He, too, is obsessed.) We’re seeing what happens when a
handsome nepo baby with an erratic online persona runs for office right now with Jack
Schlossberg’s campaign for Congress, and it hasn’t been pretty. As charming and loveable as
Hunter is, “really good poster” is a great lane for him to occupy, and we don’t need to Peter
principle him into the presidency.

Lest we forget, because it seems like everyone has, it’s only been two weeks since Hunter Biden
went onto the podcast of notorious antisemite and conspiracy theorist Candace Owens and called her
“probably the most effective communicator I’ve ever heard behind the microphone.” I think it
was part of his project to make his foes see him as a human, but even so. His defense of his
father’s 2024 campaign similarly has been both understandable and ill-considered. We all need to
slow our roll here. For all we know, Hunter will get tired of tweeting and drastically reduce his
output—500 tweets a week would be a crazy pace to sustain. If that happens, at least we’ll
always be able look back on this week of glorious tweets like a decadent cigarette in a Super 8
motel off I-95: a moment in time in worth appreciating.
