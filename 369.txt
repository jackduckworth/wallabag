Middle Class Holes 34 - Caitlin Moran

====================================================================================================

I’M MAD, ME!

Now, one of my rules when I compiled the list of Middle Class Holes was that it couldn’t include
people I know. And I did, many years ago, enjoy a night out in a smallish group that included
today’s subject. I found her somewhat overwhelming and voluble, as I expected, primed by my
memories of her spell as host of Channel 4’s 90s indie pop _Naked City_ (not half as exciting as
it sounds).

I’ve been accused of being ‘a bit much’ myself in my time, and not unreasonably. But when I
hit my late thirties the urge to fill awkward gaps in conversation with something/anything just
seemed to dry up, replaced by silent staring out of the window and sighing.

Still, I cannot in good conscience throw any stones about today’s subject on this specific front.
Like many live wire ‘creatives’, _Times_ columnist and prolific writer of cheerful pop-feminist
piffle Caitlin Moran is a person best experienced at some distance, delivering occasional little
_communiqués_, rather than up close. It’s like fizzy pop. A couple of gulps are delightful, and
the youthful can knock it back by the gallon, but after a certain age it brings only wind and
heartburn. Anyway, I don’t think one night out means that she counts as somebody I _know_.

Many of the Middle Class Holes are lacking in talent, but this is not true of Moran. It always feels
a little unsporting to pop at someone with wits, but talent doesn’t absolve you. And preparing
this entry has made me wonder again about this. I thought she was very good, but now I’m not so
sure. I’ve read quite a bit of her output in one go and I found it quite wearing. It is certainly
lively, in the way an early teen is lively. It’s a cliché that famous people are petrified at the
age they became famous, but it’s true. Caitlin’s precocious gifts were recognised early, and now
she is fifty going on fifteen. Some early developers improve hourly; I don’t feel this particular
bud has flowered very much further since its early discovery.

Then there are the photos. One of the things that always put me off considering a comedy career was
the thought of having to pull quirky faces in promo publicity. As in so many areas you’re forced
into being either an inanely grinning goon or a crusty curmudgeon. (Of course it’s even worse
being in a band, with its three pictorial options - lark like loons, smoulder, or pretend to be a
street gang blocking a corner. When you’ve witnessed The Dooleys attempt the latter, you’ve seen
everything.) Still, if you’re going to have your picture taken a lot, you might as well go for it,
and Caitlin certainly has.

What about Moran’s class status? Her background, much repeated and mythologised, with its own TV
series even, suggests that she was a grubby little oik raised outside the system. In fact, she was
reared by semi-posh hippy parents who dropped out to live on a council estate and raise their eight
children, of whom Caitlin is the eldest, at the public expense. It’s a kind of reverse shabby
genteel - genteel shabby.
