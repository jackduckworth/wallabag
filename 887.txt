The Oscar Nominations Suggest Best Picture Is Down to Two Movies—and Not the Ones Many Thought

====================================================================================================

Well, it seems the internet might have spent the past few weeks complaining for nothing.

One of the ongoing debates surrounding this year’s awards season was whether Paul Thomas
Anderson’s _One Battle After Another_ really deserved the prizes it was amassing in the run-up to
the Oscars—awards it was tipped to also sweep on Hollywood’s biggest night. And the internet’s
ire wasn’t simply limited to the question of the film’s sometimes controversial politics, which
touch on hot-button issues of race and sex and armed resistance. Instead, underlying much of the
disappointment was the question of _at what cost_? And that cost was, as fans saw it, the trophies
that should rightfully be going to Ryan Coogler’s _Sinners_—an unapologetically Black, musical,
vampire horror flick—which repeatedly lost to _One Battle After Another_ (and _Marty Supreme_, and
_Hamnet_) at the Golden Globes. This didn’t bode well for the fan-favorite film of the year where
potential Oscar wins were concerned, so fans lashed out in anger, whether they’d seen its
opponents or not.

But Thursday morning’s Oscar nominations suggested a different story. _Sinners_ not only beat out
all other titles in total nods, with a whopping 16, but it also set a new record for the most
nominated film in Oscars history. It wasn’t in itself a shock that _Sinners_ surpassed the
all-time high. It was widely predicted that _Sinners might_ break the previous record—14
nominations, set in 1951 by _All About Eve_ and subsequently tied by _Titanic_ and _La La
Land_—especially considering it was a lock on nabbing a nomination for Best Casting, the Academy
Awards’ first new category in more than two decades. But _Sinners_ exceeded even that expectation,
earning one more than needed to break the record, by securing the nominations in categories both
anticipated (Best Picture, Director, Actor) and otherwise, like Best Visual Effects, and Best
Supporting Actor, where 73-year-old legend of Black cinema Delroy Lindo earned a long-overdue first
Oscar nomination.

And as _Sinners_ galloped ahead, other movies proved unexpectedly slow out of the gate. Going into
Thursday morning, many thought Best Picture would come down to _One Battle After Another_, widely
considered the front-runner, and the possible spoiler _Hamnet_, which won best drama at the Golden
Globes. (_One Battle After Another_ won best musical or comedy.) _Sinners_, with its history of
earning plenty of nominations (seven at the Golden Globes and 17 at the Critics Choice Awards) but
struggling to actually walk away with the metal statues, looked like it would spend all of awards
season as the bridesmaid and never the bride.

However, _One Battle After Another_, which was believed to be the other contender to break the
previous record of 14 nominations, didn’t even tie it, coming away one shy with 13—a number that
simultaneously solidifies its status as a strong contender but suggests that it’s not quite as
unstoppable as pundits once thought. In particular, _One Battle_ came up empty in categories like
Best Makeup and Hairstyling—where voters passed over the movie’s prosthetics work for Sean
Penn’s third-act transformation in favor of two relative unknowns, _Kokuho_ and _The Ugly
Stepsister_—and Best Actress, where newcomer Chase Infiniti was widely forecast to be nominated
(the much-less-favored Kate Hudson grabbed one for _Song Sung Blue_).

And while _One Battle_ looks perhaps slightly weaker than anticipated, most other films seem to have
fallen out of contention entirely. In _Hamnet_’s case, the satisfying Lindo nomination likely won
out over a nomination for Paul Mescal, who has been continuously nominated in the category at the
preceding awards. _Hamnet_ also got blanked in Best Editing and Best Cinematography, where, instead,
_Sentimental Value_ and _Marty Supreme_, both of which were considered long shots on the
awards-prediction site GoldDerby, sneaked in. Best Editing, in particular, is considered one of the
best predictors of a Best Picture win, and the fact that _Hamnet_ wasn’t even nominated puts its
odds for winning Best Picture much lower: In the past 45 years, only two movies, the
seamless-looking _Birdman_ and the dark-horse winner _CODA_, won Best Picture without netting at
least a nom for editing. This is to say nothing of the other films that didn’t get the shine they
were expected to either. _Marty Supreme_ missed out on nominations for Best Supporting Actress for
Odessa A’zion, Makeup and Hairstyling, and Score. _Wicked: For Good_ drastically underperformed,
earning a startling zero nominations in contrast to its predecessor’s 10. On the betting market
Kalshi—and yes, I hate that these are part of the awards conversation now—_Sinners_ surged from
a 7 percent chance of winning Best Picture to up to 22 percent, in the space of a few hours, while
_One Battle_ faded slightly, to down to 72 percent, and _Hamnet_ cratered, from 13 percent to 4.

So, when it comes down to it, it seems like Oscars night might really become the _Sinners_ vs. _One
Battle_ war the fans on the internet feared. Unfortunately for Coogler stans, there’s still a good
chance that even with this record high number of nominations, the actual number of little gold men
_Sinners_ walks home with might be slim, which could lead to another round of online havoc. But, may
I posit that there’s something better to be angry about than _Sinners_ winning or losing on awards
night: How the _f_— did _F1_ end up in the Best Picture category?
