TV tonight: a great comedy drama about a flawed Muslim American

====================================================================================================

RAMY

12.10AM, CHANNEL 4
Four years on from its release, the third season of Ramy Youssef's great comedy drama about a deeply
flawed Muslim American gets a UK terrestrial airing. The good news is that it has aged rather
brilliantly: in particular, the second episode of this double bill featuring a disastrous Tinder
date. _Hannah J Davies_

ELGAR AT THE PROMS

7PM, BBC FOUR
A night celebrating the great British composer. Ryan Wigglesworth leads the BBC Scottish Symphony
Orchestra through Elgar's First Symphony, plus there's archive footage of Sheku Kanneh-Mason's
earlier Proms performance of the composer's much loved Cello Concerto. _Alexi Duggins_

GRANITE HARBOUR

8PM, BBC ONE
A workmanlike Scottish crime drama now in its third season, with the bones of the story becoming
visible in this, the middle of three episodes. The discovery of a murder weapon leads DC Lindo
(Romario Simpson) and team to think they know what they're dealing with, but then further
information leads them down an even darker path. _Jack Seale_

STATION ELEVEN

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC

[Danielle Deadwyler sitting at a desk looking anxious in Station Eleven.]
This adaptation of the bestselling post-apocalypse novel starts with a flashback episode to 2005 –
and it shows the origins of the Station Eleven graphic novel. In Chicago, Miranda (Danielle
Deadwyler) is drawing first sketches of Dr Eleven when an actor, Arthur (Gael García Bernal), takes
an interest. _Hollie Richardson_

ANN DROID

9.30PM, BBC ONE
More funny, and at times deeply touching, comedy about a widow and her socially awkward robot. Sue
(Sue Johnston) and her carer droid Linda (Diane Morgan) go on a trip to the seaside, where a
fortune-teller gives Sue a message from her late husband, telling her to have fun! _HR_

GRIMSBURG

9.30PM, SKY ONE
Jon Hamm's gravelly tones voice a pot-bellied police detective in this silly adult cartoon. Marvin
Flute returns to his home of Grimsburg – "The town I left behind like a perfectly timed elevator
fart" – to solve bizarre cases. _HR_

FILM

[Bob Odenkirk dressed as a sheriff seen with his hands up through a window pane with a snowy urban
landscape behind him]
NORMAL (BEN WHEATLEY, 2025), 8.25AM, 8PM, 1.10AM, SKY CINEMA PREMIERE
If you've seen Bob Odenkirk in the black ops caper Nobody you'll know what to expect from Ben
Wheatley's spry thriller. The actor plays Ulysses Richardson, the temporary sheriff of the small
town of Normal, Minnesota. There's nothing normal about Normal, of course, which Ulysses discovers
when a bank heist goes wrong and he realises it's not the robbers he has to be worried about. The
slapstick gunplay of Wheatley's own Free Fire mingles with the deadpan drama of Fargo and the still
delicious incongruity of Odenkirk packing heat. _Simon Wardell_
