TV tonight: Philomena Cunk is back – and taking on Hollywood

====================================================================================================

CUNK ON CINEMA

10PM, BBC TWO 
Diane Morgan's stealthy ascent to national treasure status has been supercharged by her brilliant
comedy Ann Droid. And now she returns to the faux-bewildered world of Philomena Cunk. "For
centuries, eyes were useless," Cunk states. "Because there was nothing worth looking at." So begins
a film history lesson that incorporates comic misinformation and contributions from the likes of
Mark Cousins and Benedict Cumberbatch – who are surely in on the joke? _Phil Harrison_

ALDI: THE NEXT BIG SELLER

8PM, CHANNEL 4 
Like Dragon's Den for foodies: six teams compete to win the chance to become suppliers to Aldi. The
competitors in this week's category of sweet treats include the creators of the traditional Indian
barfi and a woman who makes chocolates that look like slugs. But can they impress judge Anita Rani?_
Priya Elan_

SHETLAND

9PM, BBC ONE 
This sturdy drama returns for an 11th series and we're plunged into the action as a car is pulled
from the water. When the boot is opened, it becomes a matter for detectives Calder and Tosh (Ashley
Jensen and Alison O'Donnell). Gripping in its customary downbeat way. _PH_

RISE OF THE ROCKSTAR CHEF

9PM, BBC TWO

[A bald man wearing big black glasses eating fish and chips out of a cardboard box with a kitchen
behind him]
The deliciously enjoyable saga swaggers into the 90s, amid a blizzard of Britpop and TV deals. While
Marco and former protege Gordon go to war, a whiz-kid called Heston neatly undercuts them both,
bringing chemistry kits into the kitchen. "I wasn't trying to break the rules," he murmurs, "I just
learned to question them." _Ali Catterall_

THE GOOD DAUGHTER

9PM, SKY ATLANTIC 
This involving thriller is a grim story of modern America (gun crime, police brutality, traumatised
kids), told via the emotional trajectory of a family. Meghann Fahy and Rose Byrne star as Charlotte
and Samantha, sisters still scarred by a violent childhood event as a present-day horror plunges
them into their past. _PH_

AI: PROBABLY NOTHING TO WORRY ABOUT

10PM, CHANNEL 4 
The wild thing about the rise of AI is that sci-fi writers have been predicting it for decades, yet
everyone seems amazed by the reality. This fascinating, sprawling two-and-a-half-hour documentary
attempts to make sense of humanity's scourge/saviour with contributions from two of AI's pioneers,
Geoffrey Hinton and Demis Hassabis._ PH_
