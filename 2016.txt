Carrick sack unavoidable as Spurs make Man United crumble

====================================================================================================

Michael Carrick surely cannot hope to carry on as Manchester United manager much longer after Spurs
forced their latest surrender.

Roberto De Zerbi should be safe for now, mind.

1) ‘The one thing that game needed was a decisive result. A definitive winner and a doomed loser.
Water sprayed at one dumpster fire and petrol poured on the other. One crisis averted and another
aggravated. Tottenham or Manchester United? Either would suffice but a draw was the anticlimactic
grey area most expected and feared.

‘It was difficult to watch the majority of those 90 minutes and not feel as though the poison has
already set in at both clubs, with a change in coach the only possible instant antidote.’

FIVE YEARS TO THE MONTH AND TOO MANY MANAGERS LATER TO IMMEDIATELY RECALL, the sentiment remained
the same: a draw was the least satisfactory but most predictable outcome from the latest El Sackico
between two teams who over the last few seasons have systematically and seemingly deliberately
dispensed with their collective knowledge of how to win games of association football.

2) Nuno was sacked within two days of that October 2021 game, with Solskjaer gone the following
month. Dominic Solanke’s stoppage-time equaliser will preclude the history of the former being
repeated, but it would not be particularly surprising if one of Michael Carrick or Roberto De Zerbi
are removed from their post within the next few weeks.

3) This should buy De Zerbi some time, but not too much. The first equaliser of his entire Spurs
reign delivered an entirely earned point and underlined that there is some fighting spirit and grit
in this team.

Spurs were comfortably the better team for an hour, failed to take advantage of that superiority,
went behind to an eminently avoidable goal and then capitulated to the extent that a substitute was
sent off for a dreadful tackle and subsequent headbutt 12 minutes after coming on.

It was the archetypal Spurs defeat, and De Zerbi would have struggled to survive it. But that late
show of resilience and character to force a draw felt like a turning point.

While the expectation with this club and manager is that a brick wall is around that corner in this
infernal maze, Spurs finally have some positive momentum (to waste against Coventry on Monday).

4) Carrick is left in the weakest position of the two coaches by far.

This game contained multiple segments which could ironically effortlessly have challenged GARY
NEVILLE’S “WORST 15 MINUTES” ASSERTION AGAINST FULHAM. Manchester United’s lack of intensity
and physicality was exposed especially painfully early on as Spurs controlled the opening stages.

Spurs had almost two-thirds possession and five unanswered shots in the opening 25 minutes,
completing 91 per cent of their passes to the hosts’ 76 per cent. United eventually settled
somewhat but were still deeply uninspiring until a triple substitution on the hour sparked some life
into them.

Yet even with Bryan Mbeumo’s well-taken goal and Rodrigo Bentancur’s red card, they dropped
points at home against ten men for the second time in three games, conceded a stoppage-time
equaliser for the second time in six games and continued this season’s trend of playing without
direction, impetus or energy.

United started dreadfully, finished pitifully and weren’t much better in between.

5) And with the context of this game, that is damning for Carrick.

This was not only Manchester United’s first game after an elongated international break, but
Manchester United’s first game since their bitter rivals were found guilty of almost a decade’s
worth of systemic cheating. The supporters marked that occasion in fine combative, forceful voice
and might have expected that intensity and passion to be transmitted onto the pitch.

Within 20 minutes they were booing Senne Lammens, who had already been caught twice on the ball,
going long. There were jeers at half and full time. United didn’t have a shot for the first half
an hour. The enthusiasm and volume from the stands could have been channelled properly by the
players but was instead actively wasted.

At one point midway through the first half, Matheus Cunha tried to provoke some more noise from a
less interested, more restless crowd, downtrodden within 20 or so minutes of having to watch such a
miserable, identity-less team. Manchester United had that fan roar from the very start and failed to
even try channelling it.

6) Spurs deserve credit for a game plan which seemed specifically designed to silence that fervour.
They were relentless in their press, forcing United mistakes and turnovers with the sort of
insatiable pace only Conor Gallagher can really set, and the hosts never even tried to match.

The problem, as ever, was converting those moments into something more meaningful. Those
aforementioned Lammens panics, a Mohammed Kudus shot narrowly wide of the post and Leny Yoro’s
last-man slide tackle were the highlights of Spurs’ latest burst out of the blocks, but their lack
of final-third quality or fortune at the right junctures means they cannot capitalise.

The earliest Premier League goal Spurs have scored in the Premier League this season came in the
86th minute. That does seem sub-optimal.

7) Not to labour a point Carrick will inevitably bat away, but Spurs covered 4.6km more than United,
ran 3.1km further and sprinted far more.

Those running stats do not tell the whole story and can be used as a decontextualised stick with
which to beat United and their coaches. But they do feel instructive when watching this team, AS
DOES CARRICK’S STANCE ON THEM.

8) Youri Tielemans is, thus, THE POSTER BOY OF THIS LETHARGIC MANCHESTER UNITED. Not sure Old
Trafford is too fond of his penchant for receiving the ball facing his own goal and wanting five
touches. It is absurd how much he seems to be struggling with the speed of games.

9) The Belgian was sacrificed in that triple substitution on the hour, which Carrick can desperately
but justifiably point to as helping change the flow of the game somewhat.

Diogo Dalot brought more width and attacking impetus, Joshua Zirkzee had some nice touches and
Carlos Baleba impressed on his debut.

Soon after their introduction, Mbeumo scored after good work from Cunha in the build up.

But taking off Yoro felt questionable at the time and no less a mistake in hindsight. United’s
retooling as a right-back of a player who made his France debut at centre-half this month is
bizarre. He could have been shifted across and either of the subpar Harry Maguire or Lisandro
Martinez taken off instead.

10) Yoro might have provided more resistance for the Spurs equaliser.

A rare foray forward from the depleted visitors in stoppage time culminated in Sandro Tonali’s
floated cross into a box occupied by six red and three dark navy shirts. Martinez started goal-side
with the play in front of him, and ended up being spun around with a front-row seat to watch the
ball bounce past Lammens and over the line.

It was remarkably weak and hilariously non-butcherlike defending. Good job those big brains at INEOS
know that squads should be restructured specifically only in one position every year, with no
players ever sold.

11) It was wonderful centre-forward play from Solanke, whose faint touch guided the ball into the
corner.

As captain for the day, he needed that. Not to keep his position in a team with precisely no other
strikers, but to numb the pain of his two poor earlier misses and prove that he can at least vaguely
pretend to carry the mantle.

While Solanke’s all-round play was good, those misses would have defined his performance had he
not scored late on.

The first summed up his lack of confidence. Through against Lammens after the United keeper’s poor
kick, Solanke picked the side Maguire had inhabited on the line behind his keeper instead of the
other vacated corner of the goal. Then from Savio’s cross early in the second half, Solanke lofted
his effort over the bar after again bullying Martinez.

A first Premier League goal since March should help.

12) Savio is very fun. Perhaps not £75m worth, but fun nonetheless. He had at least double the
amount of touches in the opposition box as any other player, and completed at least two more
dribbles than anyone else.

His final ball is endearingly erratic. A couple of his low crosses into Solanke were exceptional,
but one moment summed Savio up best: after bringing down a Destiny Udogie diag with a sumptuous
first touch, the Brazilian beat his man, got to the byline and then gloriously overhit his cross.

Savio makes things happen. Often not especially good things at the end, and sometimes entirely by
accident. But he makes things happen.

13) It seems harsh to single out one poor performer from that Manchester United team. But also Bruno
Fernandes was dreadful.

With the atmosphere as it was, it seemed like there were only two possible scenarios: he would ride
that wave of emotion and inspire the hosts to victory; or he would let it consume him, get carried
away, throw his arms about a bit and spark a few days’ worth of narrative about whether he is a
suitable Manchester United captain.

What transpired was neither. Fernandes barely registered as a factor in the game at all. That
Athletic stat about the 23 minutes in this game being the longest he has ever waited to complete a
pass in a Premier League game is stark.

14) It would have been worse if Jan Paul van Hecke had properly connected with the first of two
headloss-coded challenges in the space of a few seconds from Spurs.

Luke Shaw avoided being caught in the crossfire of that one, only to be faced with the most obvious
choice of rock in recorded human history as Bentancur showed his hand early with an extraordinarily
telegraphed scissors tackle.

Shaw’s reaction was understandable, pushing Bentancur before the Uruguayan rose to his feet and
nudged his head into the defender’s for a red card as silly as it was inevitable.

Seven minutes after Bentancur came on for the impressive Lucas Bergvall at 0-0, Spurs conceded. Five
minutes after that, he was sent off. It should be remembered as one of the worst cameos in Premier
League history – and could temporarily boost Mateus Fernandes in the pecking order – but of
course De Zerbi “kissed” him and “said thanks” for forcing Spurs to react.

15) It was lost slightly in the melee but Van Hecke was booked for that reckless, abysmal tackle. He
remains the perfect De Zerbi defender: incredible on the ball, but frequently mediocre at the back.

His lack of pressure allowed Mbeumo enough space to turn, shoot and score, and rarely does he instil
confidence unless passing through opposition lines. It’s like Cristian Romero never left.

16) This season, Manchester United have: faced two promoted sides, losing convincingly to one and
beating the other in controversial fashion; played two teams who were bottom when they faced them,
drawing with both; played 95 minutes at home against ten men while winning or drawing, only to lose
by an aggregate score of 0-2.

All of which is to say that CARRICK IS A LOVELY FELLA, but much like with Solskjaer, it just clearly
isn’t going to work.
