

====================================================================================================

'It was crap': Wayne Rooney's candour helps BBC in World Cup final TV battle

====================================================================================================

"I thought it was crap," Wayne Rooney surmised on BBC One after enduring the half-time show. Amid a
tense final, the interval was always going to be prominent, theoretically bringing light to the
shade. Why the two UK broadcasters felt the need to transmit the karaoke session in full we will
never know but at least the pundits struggled through it, too.

Ally McCoist said it had "mentally scarred" him as someone in attendance, unlike many of his ITV
colleagues who were sat in Brooklyn. The studio has rightly earned them kudos, creating a home
alongside the East River with the Brooklyn Bridge providing the dramatic backdrop. With the game
only an expensive train ride away, it was a surprise to see Roy Keane, Gary Neville and Ian Wright
in familiar surroundings.

Mark Pougatch scripted a safe opening, which is what you expect from the ITV presenter, while BBC
made more effort by hiring an American voiceover artist and captured the mood of the match as Lionel
Messi v Lamine Yamal. Both sides were subtly seething with rage over England's failure to reach this
stage, which would have made it a greater spectacle for the domestic audience, with a lot of
editorial proposals floating past Manhattan.

The BBC was playing catchup, having spent most of the tournament in Salford rather than San
Francisco. With the authoritative Gabby Logan hosting from New York New Jersey Stadium and coaxing
the best out of Rooney, Joe Hart and Micah Richards – a trio that provide plenty of energy stood
up inside a stadium that was bursting into life as the minutes ticked by – ITV appeared more
subdued in absentia. Rooney proved he is not a regular in San Sebastián pintxos bars with his
mangled pronunciation of Mikel Oyarzabal, much to the amusement of his colleagues.

A backup team of Laura Woods, Karen Carney and Emma Hayes were in situ but reduced to cameos. Seeing
the venue, the noise and the colour that comes with it is always part of the occasion, something
missed pre-match by ITV, whose main interest came in Robbie Williams singing the ironically named
Fifa anthem Desire which no one wanted to hear. Twenty minutes of potential insight was lost on both
sides to national anthems and Phoenix Nights-style entertainment.

[The BBC World Cup final panel of Gabby Logan, Wayne Rooney, Joe Hart and Micah Richards]
Guy Mowbray and Alan Shearer are fine professionals, knowledgable and engaging, but lack the cutting
edge of the ITV trio on the gantry. Sam Matterface is not always to everyone's taste but he keeps
the viewer on their toes because he can throw in an unexpected line without notice, while Lee Dixon
plays McCoist's straight man, a necessary addition amid such a turgid match, joking that the final
was so bad it would turn the Americans back to baseball. The host broadcaster spent far too much
time finding every single celebrity in attendance. Mowbray did not seem to recognise Matt Damon,
while Matterface quipped "the talented Mr Scaloni" as the camera cut to the Argentina coach.

No requirement for adverts is always a benefit for BBC, even if pushing the licence fee continued,
allowing it to do some proper analysis before Ronaldo was released by Madonna from his kidnapping,
looking confused at re-entering society wearing a shellsuit and driving a dune buggy. ITV had to
appease its paymasters and did its best to sell everything under the sun but when back in the studio
everything was rushed, providing vague insight on a muted opening half.

There were ITV giggles over the referee's shaving foam spontaneously ejaculating to keep spirits up
to counteract the tedious football, while BBC stayed stony silent. "There's not more shows?" a
concerned Dixon asked as extra time loomed, as ITV brought the greater mirth, but it mustered only a
few seconds of punditry after the ads. "It was awful," Matterface said of normal time, a hard piece
of honesty on what is a career highlight for any journalist.

Amid the joviality Matterface immediately knew Enzo Fernández was heading for an early bath but
Mowbray did not realise a second yellow was incoming, indicating who had the calmer mind. ITV's
referee analyst, Christina Unkel, was clear with her view on what happened, while Darren Cann's
quiet review was less convincing.

ITV rested on laurels from earlier praise and should have had a greater presence at New York New
Jersey Stadium rather than taking the safe route. BBC's pundits were sharper and given the space to
air their views but ITV's more entertaining commentary balanced it out. No one will be too eager to
rewatch the match but, more than anything, the public should not be subjected to another half-time
show.
