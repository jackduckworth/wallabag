Youth to Brothers: the seven best shows to watch this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
YOUTH

Sharon Horgan's new comedy is a clarion call for messy fiftysomethings and the third part of what
she loosely conceived as a trilogy, following on from Pulling and Catastrophe. "No decade moves
faster than 50 to 60," says the intro. This means, of course, that if you haven't got your life in
order (Horgan's lead Alex very much hasn't), you're running out of time. What unfolds is by turns
scabrously funny and poignant as Alex deals with dates from hell, middle-age health problems and
empty nest syndrome. It's as uproarious as it is emotionally acute – Horgan retains the knack of
making her characters simultaneously the butt of many of the jokes and incredibly easy to root for. 
_HBO Max, from Monday 21 September_

-------------------------

BROTHERS

[Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson in Brothers.]
Could lifelong friends Woody Harrelson and Matthew McConaughey actually be brothers? Obviously, DNA
tests exist but is it more fun to simply wonder? This series runs with that idea and the result is
cheerfully vague and deeply self-indulgent. The pair play lightly fictionalised versions of
themselves – Woody has learned about this fraternal possibility while Matthew is preparing to run
for governor of Texas. Woody is staying at the McConaughey ranch while this plays out so multiple
levels of goofy contrivance are given free rein. As most siblings know, it's easiest to just go with
the flow. 
_Apple TV, from Wednesday 23 September_

-------------------------

OUTLANDER: BLOOD OF MY BLOOD

[Outlander: Blood of My Blood, season two.]
Back to the Highlands for season two of this era-jumping romance. As we return, Henry (Jeremy
Irvine) is very confused and, frankly, who wouldn't be? He has been found, wandering the moors with
a dagger sticking out of his shoulder. The doctors doubt his sanity but the truth is harder to
swallow, as a journey back to the Jacobite rising suggests. Meanwhile, poor Julia (Hermione
Corfield) is marooned in the 18th century, struggling with her husband, Lord Lovat. The big emotions
never let up – just don't expect it to make much sense. PH 
_MGM+ on Prime Video, from Saturday 19 September_

-------------------------

JAŸ-Z IN 8

[Jay Z and Rick Rubin.]
A hip-hop summit of sorts as Def Jam Recordings co-founder Rick Rubin sits down with Jay-Z for an
eight-episode survey of the rapper's back catalogue. The granular level of song-by-song detail might
constitute too much information for casual fans with the pair exploring Jay-Z's childhood artistic
development, block party adventures, ever-evolving writing process and formative influences. But,
shot in elegant monochrome and with Jay's words treated with a level of solemnity normally reserved
for spiritual gurus, it's a goldmine for serious heads. 
_Mubi, from Saturday 19 September_

-------------------------

WONKA'S GOLDEN TICKET

[From left: Austin, Tatum, Kellye in Wonka's The Golden Ticket.]
The early stages of this reality contest resemble the episode of The Simpsons in which Homer
fantasises about living in a land of chocolate. However, things get more serious once it becomes
clear how much money is at stake ("a lifetime's supply" apparently). What unfolds is essentially a
confectionery-based spin on Squid Game: The Challenge – and as players are eliminated, the tasks
become more fiendish and the Oompa-Loompas leading the competitors around the maze become less
friendly. Not remotely nutritious but guiltily snackable all the same. 
_Netflix, from Wednesday 23 September_

-------------------------

THE REAL ROONEYS

[Coleen and Wayne in The Rooneys.]
Eventually, streaming platforms might run out of retired footballers to make documentaries about.
But for now, the train rumbles on – this latest eight-part dispatch from a plush, modernist pile
in provincial Cheshire features Wayne and Coleen Rooney who, in fairness, seem a little more
characterful than certain of their contemporaries. As they invite us into their home, Wayne is
(euphemism alert!) "stepping back" from management while Coleen is coming into her own by designing
a Primark range. It's formulaic but the Rooneys seem a likable enough bunch. 
_Disney+, from Thursday 24 September_

-------------------------

AMERICAN HORROR STORY

[John Waters as Mr Phibes in American Horror Story.]
The relentless Ryan Murphy continues to churn out content of wildly variable quality: his latest
offering is season 13 of this horror anthology – whose finale will drop just in time for
Halloween. It's the usual parade of kitschy, bloody, slightly shonky gore with an impressive array
of big names in the cast: this season's ensemble includes John Waters, Tig Notaro, Sarah Paulson,
Jessica Lange and many more. The subject matter includes a creepy doctor with unspeakable hidden
intentions (Waters, obviously) and a freshly convened witches' coven. 
_Disney+, from Friday 25 September_
