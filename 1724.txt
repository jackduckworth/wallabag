We Just Saw What Happens to Trump Officials Who Dare to Tell the Truth. It’s Not Pretty.

====================================================================================================

_This story is adapted from the Slatest newsletter. You can_ _sign up here_ _to get it in your inbox
every day._

Nominally, Jeanine Pirro is the U.S. attorney for D.C., appointed by Donald Trump and tasked with
enforcing the law in the nation’s capital. In reality, Pirro’s job—like that of everyone else
in Trump’s orbit—is to protect the president’s ego, image, and delusion. Pirro briefly
confused these two roles recently, and it almost cost her her employment. It’s a lesson in how our
government works now, and also, it involves the best story of the summer: the total disintegration
of D.C.’s Reflecting Pool.

SORRY, START AT THE VERY BEGINNING. WHAT’S GOING ON WITH THE REFLECTING POOL?

The Trump administration, as part of its program to beautify Washington, announced that it was going
to “fix” the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, which had long been clouded by algae and
generally not super nice to look at. Trump promised to make the pool nicer than ever before, with
his team asserting that his predecessors were incompetent not to have done so. Except, of course,
keeping a massive outdoor pool pristine and leak-free is complicated, and Trump’s renovation
flopped: The algae came back, joined by pieces of the pool’s new lining floating in the water.
Beyond that, it came out that the administration had awarded a “no-bid” contract to the firm
tasked with making the pool great again, and that—per the National Park Service’s own
analysis—the firm was benefiting from an excessive profit margin. The whole thing became a
delicious metaphor for his second term: Claim you can easily fix something difficult, fail
miserably, and get some corruption in along the way.

OK, BUT WHAT DOES THIS HAVE TO DO WITH JUDGE JEANINE?

Well, Trump couldn’t just take responsibility for his pool flop, and so he did what he often does:
He made up a bunch of stuff. In this case, he blamed vandals for damaging the pool. It was briefly
funny, then it got ugly. Federal prosecutors charged several people with vandalism, including former
Olympic canoeist David Hearn, a 65-year-old who maintains he just picked up a piece of loose lining
because he was curious …

… WAIT, TRUMP MADE RIDICULOUS CLAIMS TO EXCUSE ONE OF HIS ADMINISTRATION’S FAILURES, AND THOSE
ACTUALLY BECAME THE GROUNDS FOR FEDERAL CHARGES?

Yes. Some real North Korea “Dear Leader” stuff.

STILL WAITING TO HEAR ABOUT PIRRO, THOUGH …

OK, here we go. On Friday, Pirro’s office asked a court to drop the charges against Hearn, telling
a judge that “botched installation”—and not this Maryland sexagenarian—was to blame for the
pool’s subpar condition. (Charges against other individuals were dropped on Monday, NPR reports.)
Pirro’s team blamed the Interior Department for the flub, saying it had misled prosecutors about
the pool.

I BET TRUMP DIDN’T LIKE THAT!

Indeed.Trump wasn’t going to let the truth get in the way of his preferred pool narrative, and he
certainly wasn’t going to stand for that truth coming from inside his administration. So the
nation’s top propagandist went to work, again claiming, without evidence (and, in fact, with clear
counterevidence), that the pool was plagued by vandals—and going after Pirro personally in an Oval
Office rant yesterday. “I was disappointed with Jeanine Pirro, really disappointed with Jeanine
Pirro,” he said. “She folded like an umbrella, and people get away with things. It’s a
disgrace.” He also said she “choked.”

SO IT’S CURTAINS FOR JUDGE JEANINE?

It got real close. She was summoned to the White House Monday to meet with Trump and Interior
Secretary Doug Burgum, whose department oversaw the renovation and who is standing by the
president’s official (phony) “vandalism” line. Pirro brought a box of documents to make her
case, and the meeting got heated, but ultimately, so far, she still has a job.

WHY SHOULD I CARE ABOUT THIS?

I also am not losing any sleep over Pirro’s employment status, and I’ve always believed that the
pool story is—as my colleague Christina Cauterucci explained—useful more as a metaphor than as a
policy issue. But this cycle illuminates an ongoing problem that afflicts the Trump administration.

That problem: When Trump says ludicrous things, his team scrambles to prove the president
right—all so he never has to admit he’s wrong. Sometimes, this is as banal as Stephen Miller
going on cable news and repeating Trump’s lies at a high volume. But increasingly, it involves
using government resources to manufacture fiction. And in this case, it means that people who are
clearly innocent are facing federal charges. Beyond that, by publicly dressing down Pirro for
putting that pesky “rule of law” ahead of the “word of Trump,” the president further made
clear to his entire administration that they work to reinforce his version of reality—even if that
requires bending the facts and breaking the law.

DID ANYTHING ELSE HAPPEN TODAY THAT IS EXTREMELY RELEVANT TO THIS TOPIC BUT IN A MUCH SCARIER WAY?

Glad you asked. Trump’s pick for U.S. attorney general, Todd Blanche, cleared a big hurdle in the
Senate. Blanche became Trump’s nominee by facilitating his conversion of the Justice Department
into a personal cudgel for use against the president’s personal and political enemies. He got the
job after his predecessor, Pam Bondi, was fired for not moving quickly enough to find ways to charge
those enemies with crimes. You can bet that Blanche won’t make Pirro’s mistake. And that means
trouble for all of us.
