_Sign up for the Surge__, the newsletter that covers the most important political nonsense of the
week, delivered to your inbox every Saturday._

Welcome to this week’s edition of the Surge, a newsletter that _could_ have chosen to do a fun
“ranking the World Cup teams by their fans”–type deal but instead has chosen, once again, to
torture you with information about American politics.

This week, Congress slopped it up once more before heading into a long recess. We examine the
quietest of the big Senate races, check in on the poisonous green slime dump on the National Mall,
pull out our wallets to pay for the Iran war, and question which powerful and connected figure who
was 79 years old in April—Donald Trump’s age at the time, weirdly—could have received the
latest miracle weight-loss drug.

But first, New York has gone red. (Not Republican red—the other one.)

1.

ZOHRAN MAMDANI

THE SOCIALIST KINGMAKER.

It was a big night for the left and New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Tuesday, as three leftists
the mayor had endorsed in congressional primaries won—two of them defeating incumbents. The most
notable of these was Darializa Avila Chevalier, who won the Democratic primary in New York’s 13th
District, which covers Upper Manhattan and a smidge of the Bronx, against Congressional Hispanic
Caucus Chair Adriano Espaillat. Meanwhile, Brad Lander, a former NYC comptroller, defeated incumbent
Rep. Dan Goldman, while democratic socialist Assembly member Claire Valdez won the primary for an
open seat representing parts of Brooklyn and Queens.

Let’s put it this way: Avila Chevalier’s win, especially, doesn’t make national Democrats’
lives any easier. We’d say that Fox News is going to make her into a cartoon character, but she
already is a Fox News cartoon character. The Ph.D. student and activist, who has worked in a public
defender’s office, is a prison abolitionist who opposes any and all deportations. She also has a
much longer and more provocative posting history that was uncovered during the course of the
campaign, though she has said she’s “grown considerably” since posting those tweets. But
there’s more than enough for Fox News and the broader Republican apparatus tomake her a celebrity.

That’s not the end of the world for national Democrats, as candidates in difficult races should
have no problem stating they don’t support her when asked. Besides, Republicans have a bit of a
cry-wolf problem here, in which they’ve been calling any and all Democrats “communists” for so
long that the public may yawn once someone approximating the real thing comes around. What _could_
be a problem is if Democrats nationally get in their heads that this sort of candidate is a
replicable winner across the board, when she essentially could have won only in this specific city
with its well-organized Democratic Socialists of America. Indeed, with Mamdani as mayor and New
York’s old political machines broken down, DSA is becoming New York City’s new establishment.

2.

THE SLIME POOL

WHY DO FUNNY THINGS ALWAYS HAVE TO TAKE AN AUTHORITARIAN TURN?

A week ago, Trump’s failure to fix the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool was good for a hearty
national laugh. After limited quality control and a no-bid contract to a Trump donor, the
administration, at the president’s behest, worked quickly to gussy up and repaint the Reflecting
Pool to the tune of $14 million. “American Flag Blue” paint would fill inbound passengers to
Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport with patriotic palpitations as they looked outside their
windows through the clean, crystal-clear water. Within a matter of days, though, the pool had become
an algae-infested green trash pool, and the blue paint began to crack and float to the surface.
Hydrogen peroxide—no friend to the local duck population—couldn’t clear the blooms, nor could
the “advanced nanobubbler technology” that Trump’s buddies installed. The pool is a free,
major tourist destination on public land in the nation’s capital, and the administration
couldn’t hide it. It left Trump exposed to mass mockery.

Trump can’t handle being mocked and lacks the capacity to admit that a mistake was made, though.
And so he needed a story. His first iteration over the weekend was that some vandal “took some
form of knife or blade, and put a 250 foot long gash into the beautiful facade of what took so much
work, competence, and money to build and complete.” By Monday, the gash had grown to a “350-foot
slit” made with a “box-cutter or a knife of some kind.” He provided no evidence. Six people
have now been arrested over alleged vandalism, including a former Olympian, and National Guard
members are patrolling the pool for vicious, un-American visitors who reach for pieces of floating
blue paint.

This is where we are as we approach the nation’s 250th anniversary on July 4: It’s becoming
dangerous—criminal!—to have a laugh at the president’s expense, and members of the military
will be used to conceal his folly. The more exposed he is to the laughter, and the more cornered he
feels, the more ridiculously he will have to respond—because he can’t admit to an error. It all
would have been too rich even for King George III’s blood.

3.

SENATE REPUBLICANS

THE SABOTAGE IS NO LONGER COINCIDENTAL.

Here’s a fresh one: The president is sabotaging his party in Congress ahead of the midterms, and
to the naked eye, it appears to be intentional. Incensed by a Senate War Powers Resolution vote to
end the Iran war, as well as its continued inability to pass the SAVE America Act, the president
called off a planned signing ceremony for the big, bipartisan housing bill that had recently passed
Congress. It’s legislation addressing home affordability that GOP members and senators _could_ be
selling back at home right now, in a terrible political environment. It’s legislation that Trump
_could_ be taking full credit for to lift his own lousy approval ratings and thereby help the party
going into November. And it’s legislation that he’s refusing to sign, because he’d prefer to
throw a tantrum.

Trump attended Senate Republicans’ lunch on Wednesday to blow off some steam, and it did produce
some results: Senate Republicans blocked another war powers vote later that day to give the
president more space in negotiations. But if Trump continues to insist that he won’t give
lawmakers any wins until they pass the SAVE America Act, and if Senate Republicans can’t pass the
SAVE America Act, will we be watching months more of this rug-pulling routine? That would be highly
entertaining, so let’s hope so.

4.

PETE HEGSETH

HERE COMES THE IRAN WAR INVOICE.

The defense secretary has been preparing his invoice to Congress and the American people over the
Iran war. Months ago, the word was that the Pentagon would request $200 billion dollars. But good
news: We’re going to get it on the cheap. The Defense Department will ask Congress for a mere
$80 billion–plus after blowing the nation’s already-depleted arsenal on its war of choice,
which it didn’t consult Congress or the American people about, for which 13 American service
members died while driving up prices across the world, and which Iran won.

So how, exactly, could Congress try to get this through? Republicans could attempt to pass it
through reconciliation—which isn’t subject to the 60-vote filibuster—but the party won’t
want to own this unpopular vote (or be subject to another open-amendment process that reconciliation
allows for). We expect to see a two-pronged approach from GOP leadership. The first is that the
party’s messaging will emphasize that restocking munitions was already necessary _before_ the Iran
war, so this addresses a lingering shortfall. That’s not wrong—but there also wouldn’t be an
emergency funding request outside normal processes if not for the Iran war.

The second is that Republicans will have to give Democrats something, or some things. And so they
may tie relief money for wildfires in California, or other blue state–centric emergency funds, to
the Pentagon money. Enough Democrats will at first find that offer insulting: Why does typical
disaster relief have to be conditional on anything else? But a price could still be found. Besides,
there are a lot of Democrats who want the military arsenal restocked too, even if they didn’t
approve of the manner in which it was depleted.

5.

SUSAN COLLINS

HER KAVANAUGH DEFENSE NEEDS SOME FACT-CHECKING.

On a couple of recent occasions, the Maine senator has defended herself against one of her biggest
vulnerabilities in this latest reelection: her critical 2018 vote to confirm Supreme Court Justice
Brett Kavanaugh, who went on to overturn _Roe v. Wade_ a few years later. Earlier this month, she
told Maine reporters that she does not “regret” her Kavanaugh vote and that although she was
“disappointed” in his _Dobbs_ vote, she had also voted to confirm Democrat-appointed justices
who voted the other way. Further, she said, Maine had voted to expand its abortion access since the
decision. It’s an argument you can take or leave.

This week’s answer, though, featured a factual error. Speaking on Fox News, Collins said, “The
fact is that whether Justice Kavanaugh were confirmed or not, _Roe v. Wade_ would have been
overturned, given the 6–3 vote.” Wrong. Although the court upheld Mississippi’s 15-week
abortion ban by a 6–3 vote, it then overturned _Roe_ on a 5–4 vote, with Kavanaugh joining the
majority. Collins’ team later clarified that she “misspoke” and asserted that even if
Kavanaugh hadn’t been confirmed, Trump’s team likely would’ve nominated another justice who
could have overturned _Roe_. True! And how would Collins have voted on that candidate? In short: Her
arguments could use a little tightening.

6.

ROY COOPER

CHECKING IN ON THE OH-SO-QUIET RACE TO FLIP NORTH CAROLINA BLUE.

Have you noticed that the Surge, like all other national political media, spends so much more time
talking about the Senate races in Texas and Maine than it does North Carolina’s _critical_ Senate
race? It’s one of Democrats’ top opportunities to flip a seat! There are a couple of
straightforward reasons why it’s not getting attention, and they’re related.

The first is that Democrats’ candidate, Gov. Roy Cooper, is well vetted, well liked, and boring.
You’re not going to see his ex-girlfriends go to the press with pillow talk about his Nazi tattoo.
(We think??) The second is that because of that, this race doesn’t look particularly competitive.
A poll this week showed Cooper leading his Republican opponent, former NCGOP and Republican National
Committee Chairman Mike Whatley, by 14 points. Election ratings outlets have moved the contest from
the “toss-up” to “lean Democrat” column. It wouldn’t surprise us if this is the last time
we include this race before the election.

7.

MYSTERIOUS 79-YEAR-OLD MAN GETTING THE GOOD DRUGS

WHO ARE YOU??

Eli Lilly is putting the finishing touches on its hottest new weight-loss drug, retatrutide, which
is going to make old-fashioned Ozempic and Mounjaro look like diet and exercise. And it turns out
someone has already tried it! STAT reported this week that the Food and Drug Administration approved
a lone application of the drug this spring under its “compassionate use” program. The April
applicant was a 79-year-old man, and STAT’s sources “said it drew the interest of top health
officials, suggesting the person receiving this drug was well connected.”

Prior to publication, the White House, the FDA, and the Department of Health and Human Services all
wouldn’t answer directly whether this applicant with “refractory obesity with obstructive sleep
apnea and pulmonary hypertension” was Trump. After the story published, though, a White House
spokesperson posted that “this application was not for the President.” There will be two ways to
tell whether the White House is lying. The first is that the mystery man will come forward and tell
us how it works. (If this person has grown horns or has three eyes but _is_ skinny, we’d call that
a net win.) The second is that Trump will suddenly be slim, because our man’s not about to get on
the Peloton.
