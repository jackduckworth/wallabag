Buried in Spam? There’s a Hidden Trick in Gmail to Make Your Inbox Less Chaotic.

====================================================================================================

delivered to your inbox daily._

_This is_ _One Thing__, a column with tips on how to live._

The other day, Slate’s VP of technology, Greg Lavallee, dropped a tip into a Slate Slack
discussion that immediately changed my life for the better. If you are one of the billion-plus
people who use Gmail, there is an extremely easy way to unsubscribe from marketing emails:

[In the gmail menu, the cursor is pointing to Manage Subscriptions.]
[In the gmail menu, the cursor is pointing to Manage Subscriptions.] Screenshot from Greg Lavallee

• Video from Slate 

In a tab labeled “Manage subscriptions” in the left rail of the Gmail interface, there’s a
list of all the promotional emails and newsletters that regularly make their way into your inbox.
Next to each one is an unsubscribe button. Two clicks, and you’re done. (You can also easily see
all the emails you’ve been sent recently by whatever hair-care company or politician, to jog your
memory on whether you do actually get value out of them.)

Gmail quietly rolled out the feature in July of 2025. It’s funny how the dumbest tech innovations
are the ones that make a splash; as of this writing, everyone is correctly up in arms at the
admission of the former CEO of Yahoo that she sees value in asking artificial intelligence to
surface bullet points on what makes her daughter special; this is a “service” that she is trying
to monetize. And yet, what often makes life easier is _less_ tech, not more. I dream of an inbox
that contains only messages that I really need to see, for example, not one that keeps me tethered
to every brand and political party I’ve ever come into brief contact with. (I bought one pair of
beautiful, eccentric shoes over the summer, and now I am constantly at risk of spending hundreds of
dollars more on such shoes, due to marketing emails, and for what?) This won’t solve our inbox
woes wholesale, but it will, at least, offer an easier path to a little relief.
