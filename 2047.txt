Premier League winners and losers: Jaissle in four-way sack race as Brighton and Chelsea impress

====================================================================================================

Crystal Palace, Sunderland, Matthias Jaissle and Nottingham Forest are circling the Premier League
drain, while Brighton enjoy themselves.

Over to Spurs to make everyone feel better tonight.

Brighton supporters
 Their last eight Premier League wins have all been accompanied by clean sheets, at an aggregate
scoreline of 27-0. Their goal difference after seven games has only been bettered by five teams in
Premier League history. Their total goals scored after seven games ranks joint fourth.

Brighton have thrashed their most bitter Premier League rivals, are through in the Carabao Cup and
top of the Conference League league phase as absurdly heavy favourites.

Fabian Hurzeler is right: “Enjoy a beer or something else and enjoy wearing the Seagulls’
colours”. The players certainly are. But he is oh so wrong about not looking at THE TABLE. F***
that; get it framed.

Alvaro Arbeloa
 It might well turn out to be terminal lucidity, SO UNRELENTING WAS THE MESS ARBELOA SEEMED TO HAVE
CREATED AT FULHAM. But the praise ought to register somewhere on the same volume scale as the
disrespect.

And with this first victory of the season came confirmation that Arbeloa can actually coach a solid
performance before utilising his bench effectively.

Fulham have dropped seven points to goals scored in or after the 75th minute against Sunderland,
Crystal Palace, Manchester United and Ipswich this season. Their supporters might well have feared
the worst when Hull made their fifth change by that mark, with the Cottagers yet to introduce a
single substitute at 1-0 up. But it was a calculated call based on a fair assessment of a game the
hosts dominated.

“When teams change things, it’s because things aren’t going the way they planned,” Arbeloa
said afterwards, damning his reign up to that point as much as Hull at the weekend, before adding
“we didn’t feel the need to change things” despite the opponent’s increasingly desperate
switches.

Fulham saw the game out with the midfield authority of Tom Cairney, rather than throwing on a Real
Madrid academy graduate to drown in a sea of duels and second balls.

Arbeloa did manage to give Cesar Palacios some cursory late minutes but relationships are built on
compromise and this one remains technically ongoing when defeat might have ended it.

Chelsea
 A first clean sheet since January. A first Premier League win without Joao Pedro scoring or
assisting since December – also against Everton. A third goal in the opening minute of a half for
Xabi Alonso’s block-bursting Blues.

THE MOST STRIKING ASPECT OF CHELSEA’S VICTORY AWAY AT EVERTON WAS THEIR CONTROL AND MATURITY. As
Xabi Alonso said: “Sometimes it is not about playing nice. It is about playing well. We needed a
result like this. We needed to show that we can win in this way.”

Chelsea dug in, scrapped and stayed the course. But they were still able to toe a line they stepped
all over last season.

After finishing bottom of the fair play table in two of the last three campaigns – and 19th in the
other – Chelsea are in the middle of the 2026/27 pack. Only ten teams have received fewer than
their 13 yellow cards; of those, six have incurred a red, too.

Just once in all of last season did Chelsea go more than seven Premier League games without having a
player sent off – the final 10 matches of the campaign, after Liam Rosenior had tried to age men
by openly calling out a squad-wide lack of discipline.

The message has belatedly landed, with Jordan Henderson’s presence as the designated driver
perhaps no coincidence when it comes to Chelsea players making better life choices. A really quite
vexed Moises Caicedo was itching to take off his gloves and throw down with Jack Grealish as early
as the third minute until Henderson jogged into frame, tapped him on the chest and beckoned him
away.

Any iteration 10of Chelsea over the last few seasons might have won, lost or drawn that game; the
only guarantee is that they would have done so with  men at most.

Jeremy Jacquet
 There were a couple of magnificent defensive interventions from THE IMPRESSIVE JACQUET, but his
most telling contribution against Brentford secured a crucial win for Liverpool.

It was difficult to see how the visitors would find their way back at the Gtech after conceding on
the stroke of half-time. Yehor Yarmoliuk walking into a fairly rudimentary Jacquet trap granted
Liverpool an opening which Rio Ngumoha and friends eventually exploited.

Manchester United
 It is funny that what “felt like a bit of a fresh start today” for Michael Carrick still
resulted in THE MOST OBVIOUS OF 1-1 DRAWS FOR MANCHESTER UNITED AT ELLAND ROAD.

There were improvements, although it should be pointed out that could have been achieved by
blindfolding the players and tying their laces together when using THE SPURS PERFORMANCE as a
barometer.

For Manchester United, the real lesson learned from the past week of post-match outbursts and
introspection was the friends they made along the way. And that Noussair Mazraoui can play basically
anywhere when you’re under pressure.

Crystal Palace
 It might be a slight oversimplification of the situation, but Pierre Sage, Jaydee Canvot, Chris
Richards and Matt Hobbs are not Oliver Glasner, Marc Guehi, Maxence Lacroix and Dougie Freedman.

Away at Brighton, the reigning champions of thinking five steps ahead to seamlessly transition from
seemingly unimprovable cogs in a machine designed to punch well above its weight, that was most
harshly proven.

Palace have, IF NOT A WORSE COACH, then certainly one handed a far less appealing hand and in more
adverse conditions. Combined with an ill-equipped squad and a sporting director in enough above his
head to apparently believe Rob Edwards is the solution to these problems, and Palace are realising
the cost of not building better foundations when given an unexpected chance.

They could have used the FA Cup and European glory as a launching pad into a sustained period of
something, anything more meaningful than sliding back into the Premier League’s
lower-to-mid-table, if not out of the division altogether.

That chance might never come again. For that to be underlined, italicised and emboldened by
Brighton’s elite-level succession planning is particularly painful.

Sunderland
 Nordi Mukiele decided against laying into the recruitment team and sparking a week’s worth of
tiresome narrative, but his post-match declaration that Sunderland perhaps “became arrogant” on
the back of last season was eye-opening.

Regis Le Bris was little more tempered in saying they “were not Sunderland” against Bournemouth,
but the reality is that NO TEAM HAS LOST MORE PREMIER LEAGUE GAMES IN 2026, and Spurs are the only
ever-presents in that time to score fewer goals.

This _is _Sunderland. And if they truly “became arrogant” on the basis of those two wins to
end last season, Le Bris might not be alone in counting the considerable cost of such haughtiness.

The relegation battle is yet to take shape and perhaps only Villa from eighth down can confidently
declare themselves good enough to pull clear. If anyone at Sunderland believes themselves to be
above that scrap, they are in for a shock.

Matthias Jaissle
 Newcastle have faced the second-most shots this season, conceded THE SECOND-MOST PREMIER LEAGUE
GOALS IN 2026, and ought to be more grateful to Crystal Palace for ensuring neither of those stats
are any worse.

At the other end, no player has scored more than two goals across the club’s last 10 Premier
League fixtures, and Dan Burn was just sent on to try and salvage a home point for a team which has
spent £170m on centre-forwards over the last two summers.

It is starting to feel like JAISSLE IS NOT LONG FOR NEWCASTLE’S WORLD.

EVERTON
It honestly feels like going into the season with a £4m first-choice right-back who never wanted to
play there, and a single senior centre-forward who has missed more big chances (11) than Liverpool
have created (10), might have been a mistake. IT’S WEIRD THAT NO-ONE MENTIONED IT AT THE TIME.

When deploying the smallest squad in Premier League history, it also doesn’t help if your manager
makes his selections to combat one specific opposition player who then does not play.

The tactical revelation of the season so far, however, came from James Garner: “We got into
half-time at 0-0 and our plan was obviously to just stay in the game and chances will come.”

That five-word, Nike and EA Sports mangling of a David Moyes motto will never not infuriate a
fanbase on such an afternoon. The prospect of Everton Just Staying In The Game before an ultimately
limp defeat at the Emirates next weekend is positively titillating.

Brentford
 As one of only two teams, along with Manchester City, who have led in every Premier League game so
far this season, Brentford can take pride in their start.

But ONE MOMENT OF IDIOCY has turned no defeats in six into a single victory in six.

Brentford have not quite yet proven that they can do anything other than either comfortably dispatch
London teams 3-0 at home, or DROP POINTS FROM WINNING POSITIONS in eminently avoidable ways. Any
sort of middle ground would be nice.
