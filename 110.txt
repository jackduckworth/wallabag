

====================================================================================================

16 Conclusions from Liverpool 4-0 Man Utd: Poch, Ngumoha, Wirtz, Yoro, De Ligt, Neville, six pints

====================================================================================================

Mauricio Pochettino’s first game as Manchester United manager went… poorly. Thanks to a
combination of Leny Yoro, Matthijs De Ligt and, let’s be fair, Pochettino for picking them, United
had lost at Anfield almost before they’d begun.

And Liverpool find themselves in the odd position of winning one of their biggest league games of
the season 4-0 and being something of an afterthought. Despite Andoni Iraola delivering the
signature Anfield occasion he had so far lacked, this game was almost entirely about Manchester
United. And, quite obviously, not in a good way.

1. It has felt like one thing lacking from Andoni Iraola’s early work at Liverpool has been a
proper signature Anfield win. It is, frankly, a slightly harsh idea given there was a 2-0 WIN
AGAINST BRIGHTON and A CHAMPIONS LEAGUE EPIC AGAINST VILLARREAL.

Both those teams are much better than this abysmal current iteration of Manchester United, and
swatting aside this comically poor team shouldn’t be more important than those wins. But obviously
it is. This is a game that will be remembered not just for the final 4-0 scoreline but for the very
reasonable observation that it could and maybe should have eclipsed even the infamous 7-0 given the
sheer horrifying scale of Manchester United’s implosion.

2. And yet for all that this game decisively, once-and-for-all sates the hunger for a compelling
Iraola Anfield success, it does feel like this will be a game whose fallout is dominated by
United’s incompetence rather than Liverpool’s brilliance. That’s an unusual position for
Liverpool to find themselves in, but for all the assorted excellence of Bradley Barcola and
Alexander Isak and especially Florian Wirtz but _especially_ Rio Ngumoha, it does feel like the
defining and overriding memories of this game will be of Matthijs de Ligt and Leny Yoro combining to
such catastrophic, dirty-protest, game-ending effect in the opening stages.

3. It’s genuinely hard to remember two defenders producing such a disastrous combination of
back-to-back catastrophe-exacerbating mistakes in succession, and for United the game was
essentially lost from that moment.

What on earth De Ligt was doing in allowing Jeremy Jacquet’s long clearance to pass unencumbered
into the path of Isak only he – and perhaps not even he – will know. Yoro was the lesser villain
in this particular shambles, but he was wildly out of position to provide any cover for any kind of
De Ligt error and then made a heroically poor decision on how to try and rectify this unrectifiable
situation.

It is, obviously, not easy to just let a striker go off and enjoy a run on goal. Especially when
that striker is one as dangerous as Isak. But that _had_ to be the choice here. Yoro had to just
accept he wasn’t where he should have been and hope for the best. Instead he brought Isak down and
gave the referee no choice but to send him off. That’s a decision that might make some sense at
0-0 with eight minutes remaining, but not with eight minutes on the clock.

4. A moment here too to entirely and unequivocally dismiss Gary Neville’s complaint at this point
about Ruining The Spectacle. Even if you subscribe to the idea that this was a ruined spectacle, and
we’re really not sure that we do, that is not and cannot ever be the referee’s concern.

At the very least, any pundit who wants a referee to consider The Spectacle in his decision-making
should receive a liftetime ban from the future bemoaning of some other refereeing decision or other
that “All we want is consistency.”

If you must identify a Spectacle-ruining villain for that early red card, might we humbly put
forward the defender who did something so stupid and cynical that its outcome was inevitable.

5. Of course, it didn’t make Yoro’s decision look any better that he sacrificed himself and The
Spectacle to turn an Isak one-on-one into a Dominik Szoboszlai free-kick, which amounts to roughly
the same thing. He duly thumped the ball into the bottom corner with a sense of grim inevitability
and you already knew this was going to get very, very bad for United, for Pochettino, for the
players left on the pitch and above all else for an insanely rattled and audibly unravelling Neville
on commentary. By the end of the game he was reduced to lashing out at Joshua Zirkzee’s Cruyff
turns and simply listing old Man United footballers who could defend, actually, unlike the current
mob.

We might recommend a mini-retirement for Neville to get over it all.

6. Before Pochettino and United had any chance to react, it was 2-0. Rio Ngumoha, setting the tone
for the kind of abuse he would dish out to poor old Diogo Dalot for much of the rest of the
afternoon, crossed for Florian Wirtz to finish cleverly, given the ball arrived at an awkward point
slightly too high to be ideal and also a fraction behind him.

Would he have scored that goal with that level of intelligent instinctiveness without the benefit of
a lovely old international break with Jurgen Klopp’s arm around his shoulder? Impossible to know
for sure, but we know what we think.

7. By now it was already damage-limitation for United, who switched to the flattest and deadest back
five in a desperate bid to avoid further humiliation. The problem for Pochettino was that he
obviously needed that back five, but one of his centre-backs had already been sent off, and another
was clearly crying out to be replaced. The numbers meant that wasn’t an option, so on came Harry
Maguire to throw himself in the way of things in on-brand fashion and attempt to put out as many
fires as he could.

Bryan Mbeumo was the man sacrificed to allow United to switch to a 5-3-1 that did at least help
prevent Liverpool truly running up the score as it seemed they would after the second goal. We
shudder to think what would have happened had Isak not passed up a glorious opportunity for a third
goal after dragging De Ligt out of position and utterly rinsing him before the reshuffle had taken
place.

8. It must be said that United’s respite had something to do with Liverpool, too. For the
remainder of the first half and a good chunk of the second they were actually a bit disappointing.
It never looked like they were in meaningful danger of getting themselves in actual bother with
regards the result, but there was a jugular exposed for which Iraola’s side seemed oddly reluctant
to go.

9. United will probably feel like they’ve slightly got away with it in getting out of that game
‘only’ losing it 4-0. It would be churlish to make that too much about Liverpool failing to
fully capitalise on the opportunity in front of them, because it really does tell us so much more
about United and their current reduced status.

The truly alarming thing for club, supporters and new manager is that there is absolutely no
hard-luck story here. Beyond the fact they deservedly climbed to their eventual third-placed finish
last season and that This Is Manchester United Football Club We’re Talking About, there is no
great mystery to their current disastrous location.

Manchester United are second from bottom in the Premier League this season because they have been
and remain the second worst team in the league. They are only occasionally convincing going forward,
they have no legs in midfield, and they absolutely cannot defend. And they haven’t won a Premier
League game in nine attempts.

10. There is mischief in the ‘worst defence in Europe’ stat that keeps doing the rounds. Yes,
United have conceded more goals in all competitions than anyone else in the continent’s top
leagues, and no, 34 goals shipped in 16 games cannot be defended in any way. But it also really just
means ‘worst defence of any major European team to qualify for Europe’ which isn’t quite the
same thing.

Far more damning is ‘second worst defence in the Premier League’. THE ONLY TEAM TO HAVE SHIPPED
MORE THAN UNITED’S 23 GOALS ARE THE ONLY TEAM BELOW THEM IN THE LEAGUE. They’ve conceded more
Premier League goals this season than, say, Hull and Tottenham combined. And there are loads of
other combinations you can use there, if you wish. Leeds and Fulham is a good one. Arsenal and
Forest has its charms. We’re slightly annoyed Liverpool didn’t rack up a couple more to give
United a worse combined defensive record than Arsenal, Brighton _and_ Leeds combined. But it’s
still bad enough.

And it’s clearly unsustainable, but what can be done? These are the players United have for at
least another six weeks. Maguire’s poitnlessly sterling effort in a futile backs-to-the-wall
attempt at avoiding further embarrassment probably make him a starter, but it’s hardly a
compelling solution to the wider malaise.

It’s not a new observation, but United’s complete failure to upgrade their defensive
capabilities at all during the summer is becoming one of the great transfer mistakes of the age.
Especially when the laser focus on midfield produced such humdrum results anyway, with neither Youri
Tielemans nor Andrey Santos looking like they can deliver the oomph Pochettino requires from that
part of the pitch.

Sometimes you get transfers that work out great for everyone involved, but it’s a rare one that
ends up working out badly for everyone. Tielemans-to-United is starting to have that air to it; he
has offered precious little for United, yet Villa undoubtedly miss his experience and quality. A
triumph all round.

11. The red card simply cannot provide a get-out-of-jail-free, criticism-dodging caveat to this game
for the beleaguered visitors. Not least because the red card itself was a direct result of
United’s defensive shortcomings, and also Pochettino’s wrong-headed selection.

His first major call as United manager was to hand a first Premier League start in 12 months to De
Ligt. Only Pochettino and perhaps that lad with the drone will know what he saw in training to
justify that decision, but within the first 10 minutes it was exposed as the wrong one.
Devastatingly so.

Yes, it’s only one game and one decision, and Pochettino could not possibly have imagined it going
so spectacularly wrong quite so spectacularly fast, but even the most casual Man United observer
will have seen that teamsheet and shat it. Pochettino has inadequate resources at his disposal, we
know this, but that his first big call was to decide this particular arrangement of his Titanic
deckchairs was the best one is a huge early concern.

The cruellest part of it all may well have been that while De Ligt’s error led to a red card, it
wasn’t for him, meaning he faced a far worse punishment than Yoro: he had to stay on and be
humiliated time and time again for another 80-plus minutes. You wouldn’t wish it on your worst
enemy.

12. And he might have to play next week as well now. Because we’re really not sure there is any
combination of defenders available to Pochettino currently where he can trust having just four of
them on the pitch at any one time.

It’s also unclear how he goes about doing much about this until the desperation chequebook can be
whipped out in January, by which time things could be looking bleak indeed.

It boggles the mind that United are playing Champions League football this week, but it’s
happening, and that packed schedule means limited time for Pochettino to get this rabble on the
training pitch and knock some heads together to try and come up with some kind of solution for a
team that has now conceded four goals in successive Premier League games and at least twice in seven
out of 11.

13. Liverpool did, at least, manage to find a couple more goals in the end to make the scoreline
offer something approaching an accurate assessment of how the match had played out.

We struggle to think of an obviously excellent player who is less convincing when asked to shoot
than Bradley Barcola, a man who you can almost visibly see whirring through multiple options when
presented with a shooting chance before selecting the wrong one with unerring regularity.

But that uncertainty is replaced with gimlet-eyed clarity when he’s asked to run at pace into some
open green grass before fizzing over a cross, as he did for the magnificent Ngumoha to score the
goal his performance so obviously deserved.

It’s been a handy weekend for absurdly talented teenage English wingers.

14. The fourth goal was finished wonderfully well by Victor Munoz, who was given the absurdly
enjoyable task of coming on to run at a beaten, broken and exhausted United defence for the last 20
minutes here. With a crushing inevitability, the goal was created first by yet another De Ligt error
and then by a Wirtz assist.

The result was long decided by this point, but the nature of those final two goals summed the game
up every bit as neatly as the events around the first two.

15. It was a painfully difficult afternoon for Gary Neville, perhaps not helped by sharing co-comms
duty with such an obviously delighted Jamie Carragher, but he still produced an iconic moment with
his COMPARISON OF DE LIGT PLAYING HIS FIRST PREMIER LEAGUE GAME IN A YEAR TO A CHAP WHO’S SIX
PINTS DEEP.

We know a new buzzword or catchphrase when we hear it, and that is one we fully expect to hear
wheeled out again and again by lesser men. Probably next weekend, and probably about another United
defender. That’s a glibly easy answer, but the real fun lies in finding out which one. It could be
any of them, but for what it’s worth our money’s glibly and easily on Diogo Dalot.

16. The final and surely most important takeaway from the game is this, though. Don’t ask us to
explain what we mean by this or its significance, but we cannot shake the thought that Senne Lammens
looks more like Mauricio Pochettino’s son than Mauricio Pochettino’s son does.
