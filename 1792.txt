

====================================================================================================

Meet the Owens to Dog Park: the seven best shows to stream this week

====================================================================================================

PICK OF THE WEEK
MEET THE OWENS

First the elephant in the room, which Michael Owen addresses head on. Is he really, as he describes
himself, "a boring prick"? This documentary series sees the former Liverpool and England striker
welcome cameras into both his racing empire (he owns 140 horses) and his family life (he has four
children). As he messes around cheerfully, the impression that emerges isn't exactly boring, more
average. He's a well-to-do, middle-aged bloke who embarrasses his kids, indulges his interests and
seems amiable enough, within the confines of a documentary edit. Accordingly, this series is likely
to confirm most people's preconceptions about its subject. 
_Prime Video, from Friday 28 August_

-------------------------

ADULTS

[From left: Amita Rao as Issa, Jack Innanen as Paul Baker, Owen Thiele as Anton, Lucy Freyer as
Billie and Malik Elassal as Samir in Adults season two.]
A comedy about house-sharing twentysomethings in urban America? It's a concept that never runs out
of commissioning steam, though most such shows seem doomed to wither and die in the shadow of
Friends. Adults has at least made it to a second season and it celebrates with a self-referential
cameo from Girls star Zosia Mamet ("You think you're the first ones to be young and adorable in New
York?"). Although it never transcends its forebears, Adults remains perky enough, but the plotting
doesn't really go anywhere – it's still a directionless stew of bad decisions and casual hookups. 
_Disney+, from Friday 28 August_

-------------------------

9/11: REUNITED

[Debbie St John and Stefan Horlacher in 9/11: Reunited.]
A quarter of a century has passed since the terrorist attacks on the US that reshaped global
politics. For documentary-makers, the struggle to find angles that don't feel repetitive or
exploitative continues. This series seeks an uplifting take on those awful events, speaking to
people who survived because someone risked their life to save them. In many cases, they have never
met their guardian angels, but this is put right with frequently moving results. As 9/11 recedes
into history, this is a reminder of the humanity behind the numbers. PH it's still a directionless
stew of bad decisions and casual hookups. 
_National Geographic, from Sunday_ _23 August_

-------------------------

DOG PARK

[Brooke Satchwell and Leon Ford in Dog Park.]
While we await more Colin from Accounts, here's another canine-centred meet-cute comedy from
Australia. Roland (Leon Ford) is a grumpy, emotionally constipated man with a crumbling marriage.
When his wife goes abroad for work (or possibly just to get away from him), Roland is left in charge
of their teenage daughter and lively terrier. Soon, he's dragged into an over-friendly community of
dog walkers ("Did your daddy forget that this is a leash-on area?") with inevitably redeeming
results. Sweet rather than sharp but an easy, gentle watch all the same. 
_U, from Monday_ _24 August_

-------------------------

THE AIRPORT CHAPLAIN

[Hugo Weaving and Shabana Azeez in The Airport Chaplain.]
Airports are emotionally charged places so it makes sense that most of the major terminals have a
religious minister or two on hand. This slightly odd drama stars Hugo Weaving as Tobias Wallace, an
unorthodox airport cleric who, like many a small-screen authority figure before him, sometimes cuts
corners but usually gets results. However, his rule-bending eventually goes too far and causes a
serious security breach – which brings him into the crosshairs of his new manager Mira (Shabana
Azeez of The Pitt fame) who can't relate to his maverick ways. 
_Netflix, from Wednesday_ _26 August_

-------------------------

DALLIANCE

[Hugo Weaving as Billy in Dalliance.]
It's boomers behaving badly in this drama set among the well-to-do sixtysomethings of Sydney. Hugo
Weaving stars as Billy, whose chance encounter and instant chemistry with restaurateur Dani (Heather
Mitchell) sets off a chain of events that threatens to tear his family apart. Billy is happily
married to Rose (Georgie Parker), so much so that he thinks he can introduce his new lady friend to
his wife. This proves to be a miscalculation with serious consequences. It's soapy but it's good to
see an exploration of the romantic adventures of older people. 
_Paramount+, from Thursday 27 August_

-------------------------

DARK MATTER

[Jennifer Connelly and Joel Edgerton in Dark Matter.]
Back to the Dessen family multiverse for a second season of this smart sci-fi. With many alternative
realities to choose from, Jason and Daniela seem to have found a good one. But the arrival of
another Jason creates a desperate flight to the Box. Meanwhile, tech CEO Leighton Vance wants to use
the portal for good – but he's hard to trust. There's plenty to like about Dark Matter,
particularly the performances from Joel Edgerton and Jennifer Connolly. But the show doesn't quite
convince as either existential reflection or freewheeling thriller. 
_Apple TV, from Friday 28 August_
