Are former special counsel Jack Smith, the Atlanta Hawks, and Fulton County District Attorney Fani
Willis connected in a deep-seated conspiracy to persecute Donald Trump? The seemingly obvious answer
would be no. It is not so obvious, though, to Missouri Sen. Eric Schmitt, who seemed to think on
Tuesday that he had concocted the perfect gotcha moment against Smith. During a Senate judiciary
hearing, an overly zealous Schmitt claimed he had text messages proving Smith attended an Atlanta
Hawks game _the day after_ Willis made headlines for admitting to maintaining a romantic
relationship with a prosecutor on her staff. You might be asking, “So what?” It’s a good
question. But according to Schmitt, Smith’s alleged attendance at this basketball game meant that
he and Willis had colluded on her 2020 election interference indictment of Trump and 18 others,
looking together for a way to keep it alive after Willis’ romantic escapades jeopardized her
prosecution.

• Video from Slate

Smith, as you might expect, was confused by this line of questioning, stammering that he didn’t
recall attending any Hawks games. This was when Schmitt, believing he was on the verge of a slam
dunk, accused Smith of perjury. Schmitt’s theory, though, couldn’t even hit the rim: It turned
out that Smith had not attended the Hawks game in question. As Democratic Sen. Amy Klobuchar pointed
out, Schmitt had confused the Atlanta Hawks with the Iowa Hawkeyes, who were playing a women’s
college basketball game the same day of the NBA Hawks game in question, but in Maryland, not
Georgia.

The whole exchange has to be seen to be believed.

Klobuchar’s staff then confirmed that Schmitt had in fact gotten the teams’ names wrong. On Feb.
3, 2024, the Iowa Hawkeyes had a game in Maryland with Clark scoring 38 points.

“For a U.S. senator to have false information and imply, based on a confusion about the name of a
team—which I knew, knowing my Midwestern sports—that you were in another city, that you were
somehow involved that day in what was happening in Georgia,” said Klobuchar to Smith. “And then
call you a ‘dirtbag’ deserves an apology.” Spoiler alert: Schmitt did not apologize.

Instead, Schmitt continued to push the blatant falsehood. Immediately after Tuesday’s hearing, the
Republican senator was on _The Charlie Kirk Show,_ where he said the “only NBA or NCAA men’s
game going on at that time was in Atlanta”—a moot point, since Smith said he attended a
_women’s college_ basketball game. Schmitt also told the show that his job as a senator was simply
to “ask the questions,” dumbing down the responsibilities of one of Congress’ highest elected
offices in an obvious effort to distance himself from any responsibility of such an embarrassing
mistake. He also made sure to repeat that he believed Smith had “lied under oath time and time
again,” likely an attempt to keep perjury inquiries into Smith alive and well.

For years, Trump has attacked Smith, from calling him a “thug” who should be investigated and
put in prison to “deranged” and suggested Smith’s indictments were a “Democrat scam.” Now
with control of the White House and both chambers of Congress—for now—Trump is likely frothing
at the mouth to bring yet another vindictive prosecution against the man who brought two massive
criminal indictments against him, publicly revealing how classified documents were unlawfully taken
from the White House and stored in a Mar-a-Lago bathroom.

Terrified of angering the president, Republicans have largely fallen in line to support the failed
prosecutions of New York Attorney General Letitia James, James Comey, and others. Schmitt’s latest
scheme against Smith is yet another example of that blind loyalty. It’s also consistent with
Schmitt’s penchant for white nationalist conspiracy theories.

It has been years since Smith first unveiled his two indictments of Trump, one over the unlawful
retention of classified documents and the other over efforts to overturn the 2020 election results.
Both of the cases were ultimately dismissed, and days before Trump’s second inauguration, Smith
resigned from his special counsel post. But the GOP has refused to let Smith go, hoping to find a
way to prosecute him for daring to hold Trump and his allies accountable. Their sights seem to be
set on perjury, seeking desperately to find inconsistencies in Smith’s recollection of his
criminal investigations.

Unfortunately for Schmitt, pinning potential perjury charges on a basketball game Smith never
attended was probably never going to work.
